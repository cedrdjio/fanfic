# Chapitre 8 — Deep Forest, Meeting the Fox

The wild rabbits provided a stable source of Vitality, but Shuichi soon discovered a problem.

The number of wild rabbits in the pine forest was limited. When he went for the third time, he only found one in the same area, and it was one he hadn’t noticed during the previous two trips—it was smaller and more alert. Lue circled above the pine forest for an hour, but only managed to lock onto this one.

« Rabbits will migrate from areas that have been Plundered. » Shuichi squatted by the stream, watching the small wild rabbit in his palm gradually wither. « Living rabbits will realize this place is unsafe and run deeper into the forest. »

He calculated in his mind. The wild rabbit population in the pine forest was about five or six. He had already Plundered three ; the remaining ones had either migrated or were hiding deep in burrows he couldn’t find. To continue raising his Vitality with prey at or above the level of a wild rabbit, he had to head deeper into the mountains and forests.

Lue circled above his head, its wings gleaming with a metallic, grayish-brown luster in the morning sun. Over the course of more than a month, Lue had completely shed its fledgling down, its flight feathers were neat and sharp, and its wingspan was nearly as wide as Shuichi’s outstretched arms. Its movements in the air were no longer awkward ; circling, diving, stopping abruptly, and turning back—every action was smooth and precise.

« Head northeast. » Shuichi raised his arm and pointed deeper into the pine forest. « Toward the edge of the Forest of Death. Let’s see if there’s anything bigger. »

Lue let out a clear cry and flapped its wings, flying toward the northeast. Shuichi continued walking along the path at the edge of the pine forest. The ground, paved with pine needles, became thicker underfoot, and the spacing between the pine trees grew wider. Sunlight poured down through the gaps in the canopy, casting patches of golden light on the ground. The scent of pine resin in the air was gradually replaced by another, damper, and richer scent of soil.

The pine forest came to an end. Ahead was a Mixed Forest where oaks and deciduous oaks grew intertwined ; the canopy was much denser than the pine forest, and low shrubs grew thickly on the ground. Shuichi stopped at the edge of the woods. The Climbing Ability he had digested from the Tree Frog allowed him to easily climb to the middle branches of an old oak tree, where he observed the surrounding terrain from a high vantage point.

This Mixed Forest was more than ten times larger than the Mixed Forest. The canopies were layered, and the shrubs on the ground formed a natural barrier. About two hundred meters to the north, the terrain began to sink noticeably, and a dry riverbed could be vaguely seen. Further north of the riverbed, the trees became taller and denser ; that must be the edge of the Forest of Death.

Lue flew back from the northeast, circling at a lower altitude than usual. It circled twice above the oak tree where Shuichi was, then made a short dive in a north-northeasterly direction—that was the signal for locking onto prey.

Shuichi slid down from the oak tree, lowering his body as he moved in the direction indicated by Lue. After digesting the Climbing Ability, it had become his instinct. While moving through the bushes, his footsteps were much lighter than they had been over a month ago ; his soles could automatically find the most stable footing on the ground, and his body’s center of gravity shifted naturally between the branches, making almost no sound.

After walking for about five minutes, he caught a scent. It wasn’t pine resin, nor was it soil, but a more primal, stronger animal scent. Shuichi squatted behind a cluster of low oak trees and parted the branches to look out.

On the opposite bank of the dry riverbed, a Red Fox was lying on a large rock warmed by the sun. Its fiery red fur was almost glowing in the morning sun, and its fluffy tail rested on the edge of the rock, rising and falling slightly with its breathing.

Shuichi held his breath.

A fox. Its size was about three to four times that of a wild rabbit, making it a true mid-sized predator. Its Vitality concentration was clearly visible in Shuichi’s perception ; if a wild rabbit was a bowl of thick soup, this Red Fox was a whole pot. Then there was the fox’s hearing, sense of smell, night vision, and hunting instincts—each was more than an order of magnitude stronger than those of a wild rabbit.

If he were to Plunder this Red Fox’s Vitality, the gain would be about four to five times that of a wild rabbit. The mental energy consumption would account for about half of his current total.

It was worth it. But the problem was that a fox is not a rabbit. Its hearing and sense of smell far exceeded those of a rabbit, and the slight sound Shuichi had made when moving through the bushes had likely already been picked up by it.

Sure enough, the Red Fox’s ears twitched.

Shuichi did not move. He squatted behind the low oak trees, keeping his breathing to a minimum. The Red Fox raised its head and glanced in his direction, its shiny black nose twitching slightly in the air. The wind from the pine forest was blowing from west to east, and he was on the east side, meaning the fox was upwind. It couldn’t smell him.

But the fox saw the branches of the low oak trees swaying slightly. It wasn’t the wind ; it was the residual trembling left behind when Shuichi had parted the branches earlier.

The Red Fox stood up from the rock.

Shuichi knew he couldn’t wait any longer. He rushed out from behind the low oak trees, his feet stepping on the gravel of the dry riverbed, and crossed the riverbed in three seconds. The Red Fox turned to run, but Shuichi’s right hand was already pressed onto its back.

Plunder.

The instant the suction force erupted from his palm, the Red Fox let out a piercing scream.

It was not the silence of a wild rabbit, nor the stiffness of a tree lizard. A fox is a predator ; it has sharp teeth, strong limbs, and the instinct to fight back to the death when pushed into a corner. The Red Fox’s body rolled violently under his hand, its four legs kicking frantically against the gravel on the riverbed, its claws scraping harsh sounds against the stones. Its head whipped around, mouth wide open, and its snow-white canine teeth aimed to bite down hard on Shuichi’s wrist.

Shuichi twisted his wrist, and the fox’s teeth grazed his sleeve, tearing off a piece of fabric. He pressed down on the fox’s neck with his left hand, while the suction in his right hand continued to erupt. The burning heat in his right palm was more intense than ever before. The Vitality did not pour in all at once like it did with a wild rabbit ; instead, it was like a river forced to change course, being drawn out of the fox in waves, moving slowly and arduously along the channel in his palm and up his arm.

The fox’s struggle became even more frantic. Its hind legs kicked Shuichi in the ribs ; fortunately, the force hadn’t reached the level of breaking bones, but the pain made Shuichi grunt. He gritted his teeth and pressed his entire body weight down. His six-year-old body, with the physique built up over two months of Plundering Vitality and digesting the Climbing Ability, barely held the upper hand over the seven or eight-pound fox, but only barely.

The first wave of Vitality surged into Shuichi’s chest. The warm current poured from his right arm into his chest, and his heart jumped violently, as if someone had punched it hard from the inside. The fox was still struggling, but its movements began to lose their previous explosive power. The kicking frequency of its hind legs slowed down, and the range of its head twisting became smaller.

The second wave of Vitality surged in. Shuichi’s spine, from the cervical vertebrae to the coccyx, heated up simultaneously, and sweat seeped from his forehead, dripping onto the fox’s fiery red fur.

The fox’s howling turned into whimpering. Its four legs were still kicking, but it was no longer the life-or-death struggle from moments ago ; it was a weak, powerless twitching. Its tail swept across the gravel ground once, then went limp.

The third and final batch of Vitality was drawn away. Shuichi felt his bones humming from within. The process of his bone structure becoming denser was magnified in his consciousness, accompanied by an indescribable, subtle sense of vibration.

The fox stopped moving completely.

Shuichi rolled off the fox, lying on his back on the gravel of the dry riverbed, gasping for air.

The Plunder lasted about ten seconds. Compared to the two or three seconds for a wild rabbit, these ten seconds felt as long as a full battle. His mental energy consumption was nearly half, much more than expected, because the fox’s struggle forced him to divide his focus to suppress it while Plundering, equivalent to multitasking. His ribs ached faintly, and there was a shallow abrasion on his wrist ; although it hadn’t bitten through, the canine teeth had broken the skin.

He turned his head to look at the mass of fiery red fur beside him. The Red Fox lay quietly, its fur still smooth and glowing in the sun, but it was empty inside.

« Mid-sized predator... » Shuichi panted, his voice hoarse. « It cannot be handled like a rabbit. The Plunder process is longer, the target will resist violently, and the mental energy consumption will increase additionally due to suppressing the target. One must also consider the possibility of being injured. »

This was an important lesson. Previously, from insects to tree lizards to wild rabbits, Plundering had become increasingly smooth, leading him to subconsciously believe that Plundering was just a matter of mental energy consumption. But the Red Fox had given him a solid lesson : when the prey’s size is large enough to resist, Plundering is no longer a one-sided extraction, but a close-quarters battle that requires physical skill to complement it. If his physique were still at the level of that five-year-old child from two months ago, the Red Fox’s struggle would have been enough to shake him off, and it might even have turned to bite his neck.

The absorption of Vitality was also much slower than with previous prey. After the Vitality of the wild rabbits and tree lizards poured into his body, it could be fully integrated within a few minutes. But the Red Fox’s Vitality was too dense. He could feel that hot current still slowly spreading within his body, from his chest to his limbs, and from his limbs to his fingertips and toes—like thick ink dripped into water, it needed time to disperse evenly. Fully absorbing this Red Fox’s Vitality might take an entire day.

Shuichi knelt on one knee on the dry riverbed, gasping for air.

After the life energy slowly soothed the pain caused by the wounds.

Shuichi dragged the Red Fox’s carcass under an oak tree by the side of the riverbed and covered it with fallen leaves. Lue flew down from the sky and landed next to the fox’s carcass, lowering its head to look at the fiery red fur. This time, it didn’t make any sounds of wanting to eat, because the fox’s size was too large ; Lue knew this wasn’t something it could drag.

« Let’s go back. » Shuichi stroked Lue’s wings. « I don’t have enough mental energy left. »

When walking out of the Mixed Forest, Shuichi looked back. The boundary of the Forest of Death was less than a kilometer north of the dry riverbed. That forest was a notoriously dangerous area in the Hokage world ; the Forest of Death from the Chunin Exams was only a small part of it, and even a Chunin might not be willing to enter the true depths. But danger meant abundant biological resources. The prey in the Forest of Death, whether in number or size, were far beyond what this Mixed Forest could compare to.

But not today. His mental energy was only a little over half remaining, not enough to handle any accidents in the Forest of Death.

On the way back, Shuichi walked very slowly. The Red Fox’s Vitality was still slowly permeating his body, and he could feel his body becoming stronger with every passing minute. His grip strength was now about the level of an average ten-year-old child, and his running speed might have already approached the level of lower-grade students at the Ninja Academy. And all of this had taken less than two months.
