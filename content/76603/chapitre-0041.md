# Chapitre 41 — Hitching a Ride

In the southern part of the Land of Fire, not far from Konoha, lay a small town.

It was livelier than Shuichi had expected. The sign at the entrance to the town read « Genya Town ». The two-zhang-wide main street was paved with bluestone slabs, lined with neat two-story wooden buildings. Shops were packed side-by-side : general stores, herb shops, Ninja tool shops, blacksmiths, inns, and even an open-air market. The market had over a dozen stalls selling dried goods, pottery, and mountain produce, with the sounds of haggling mingling together.

This was one of the towns closest to Konoha, attracting many merchants and travelers, which contributed to the town’s prosperity.

One could see Ninjas on the street. These Ninjas were all on missions—some acting as escorts for merchant caravans, others running errands for employers—sporting various emblems on their headbands.

Shuichi walked down the street, his gaze sweeping over the crowd as he calculated.

After leaving Konoha, Shuichi had not wandered aimlessly. He knew what his goal was : the Black Market. The Black Market was an underground network for intelligence and mission trading in the Ninja World, where rogue Ninjas without village affiliation, bounty hunters, and information brokers operated. Shuichi intended to accept bounty missions to hunt down Ninjas. This way, he could not only obtain the location of the target Ninja and their approximate strength, but also earn a sum of money. It was truly—as the saying goes—winning across the board.

However, the Black Market was not something displayed in the open ; there wouldn’t be a sign on the roadside saying « Black Market Here ». Shuichi needed to deduce where the Black Market might exist.

Places that bred darkness most easily were the prosperous ones, so Shuichi focused his targets on the prosperous towns of the Land of Fire.

How to get there became a new problem for Shuichi. Traveling on foot was a good option, but whether he could accurately locate places based on hand-drawn maps was a question ; one careless mistake could easily waste more time.

Therefore, Shuichi chose a safer method : following merchant caravans.

Shuichi had already stayed in the town for a day, having tried to talk to several caravans, but none had succeeded. Either the large caravans looked down on him, or their destinations were not Shuichi’s targets.

Shuichi stood at the entrance of the post station for a while, looking towards the west.

There were three ordinary cargo carriages loaded with cloth, grain, and general goods, each followed by an escort. The last carriage was the smallest, with only a few bundles of cloth and some wooden crates piled in the carriage bed, and an old brown horse hitched to the shafts.

A middle-aged man stood beside the last carriage, talking to the driver. Two men carrying swords stood nearby, acting as hired escorts—ordinary samurai.

The middle-aged man wore a brown cotton coat with a faded apron tied around his waist, looking like a traveling merchant. His face was round, with a tuft of unevenly trimmed short beard on his chin. His eyes were small but very agile ; one look and you could tell he was someone who had spent years on the merchant roads.

Shuichi walked over.

« Boss, which direction are you heading ? » Shuichi asked.

The middle-aged man turned to look at Shuichi, his gaze sweeping from top to bottom : the dark blue long robe, the short sword at his waist, and the crow squatting on his shoulder.

The Transformation Technique had altered his appearance, and combined with the « Reality » concept imbued in his transformed form, it made the disguise more than just ordinary genjutsu—it was a more stable camouflage. It wouldn’t collapse due to Chakra fluctuations, revealing flaws.

Shuichi now looked to be in his early thirties, lean, with dark gray hair tied in a loose ponytail, and a few stray strands hanging over his forehead. His face was narrow, with slightly high cheekbones, a firm jawline, and a shallow scar at the corner of his eye. He wore a faded dark blue long robe, with an ordinary iron short sword tucked at his waist, the scabbard worn with a few scratches.

This image was something Shuichi had come up with on a whim while preparing his disguise. In a game he had played in his previous life, there was a character who carried a sword, and Shuichi felt that the man’s temperament was quite suitable for a wandering samurai—down-and-out but not decadent, silent but not gloomy. He designed this persona based on that feeling.

This type of person was very common on the borders of the Ninja World and would not arouse anyone’s suspicion.

Lue squatted on Shuichi’s left shoulder, its black feathers shimmering with a dark green luster in the sunlight. After Shuichi had plundered the physical characteristics from several different types of birds and imbued them into Lue, Lue’s appearance had changed into that of a crow. The originally sharp eagle beak had become a crow’s short beak, and the golden eagle eyes had turned into pitch-black round eyes.

« Heading south to Takino Town, » the middle-aged man said, his gaze still lingering on the short sword at Shuichi’s waist.

Takino Town.

He remembered Tanzaku Town, a famous gambling and hot spring town in the Land of Fire. That place was a melting pot, with all kinds of people gathering there, and it was highly likely that a Black Market existed nearby. And there was a town near Tanzaku Town : Takino Town. It wasn’t big, but because it was close to Tanzaku Town, it attracted many merchants and travelers, serving as a transit station for all sorts of people.

This kind of place was exactly what Shuichi needed.

Shuichi’s expression remained unchanged.

« I’m heading south too. Can I hitch a ride ? I can help if we run into trouble on the road. »

The middle-aged man didn’t answer immediately. His eyes rolled, as if he were weighing something.

Shuichi noticed his hesitation. When a strange wandering samurai wants to hitch a ride, a merchant’s first reaction isn’t joy, but vigilance—is this person really just wanting a ride, or are they trying to figure out how much cargo is on the carriage ? Not all wandering samurai are honest people ; some are lone wolves, others are scouts for gangs, hitching a ride to scout the situation, and then their accomplices come out to rob them in remote areas.

« Don’t worry, Boss, » Shuichi said in a flat tone, « I’m just looking for a ride. »

The middle-aged man looked at him again, then said as if casually : « There’s not much cargo on this trip back, just some leftover cloth and miscellaneous goods that didn’t sell. I’m just working for someone, just delivering the carriage back to finish the job. »

Hearing the middle-aged man’s casual remark, Shuichi understood immediately.

This person was telling him two things : First, there was nothing valuable on the carriage worth robbing ; second, he was just an employee and had no money on him.

If Shuichi were a robber, he would likely have lost interest upon hearing this and wouldn’t continue to ask for a ride. If Shuichi were not a robber, then the merchant would have gained a free guard—although such wandering samurai would likely run away when encountering real danger, having one more person standing by could at least scare off those petty thieves.

Shrewd.

« Doesn’t matter, » Shuichi said, « I’m just going the same way. »

The middle-aged man’s expression relaxed slightly. Shuichi hadn’t given up on hitching a ride because of the « no cargo, no money » claim, which meant he truly just wanted a ride.

« Fine, » the middle-aged man clapped his hands, « I’m Yuanda, in the cloth business. What’s your name ? »

« Shu, » Shuichi said.

« Mr. Shu, get on. We’re leaving soon. »

Shuichi nodded and walked over to the carriage. The two escorts glanced at him, one of them letting his gaze linger on the short sword at his waist before looking away. The escorts were also ordinary samurai, not Ninjas, with no traces of Chakra on them. Their swords were longer than Shuichi’s, but they were poorly maintained, with rust on the scabbards.

Shuichi lifted the curtain at the back of the carriage. Inside were several bundles of cloth and some wooden crates, not much space, but there was room to sit in the corner. He climbed up and sat in the corner.

Lue flew up from his shoulder, landed on the crossbeam at the top of the carriage, and crouched down with its neck tucked in.

Yuanda gave a few instructions to the driver under the carriage, then climbed into the carriage as well. He found a comfortable spot among the bundles of cloth, sat down, and pulled a grease-paper packet from his bosom, which contained a few rice balls.

« Mr. Shu, would you like one ? » Yuanda offered.

« No need, » Shuichi said.

Wandering samurai wouldn’t eat food handed to them by others. Walking on the borders, mutual suspicion was a basic survival rule. You could talk, but you didn’t touch the food. Yuanda clearly understood this rule, didn’t say anything more, and started munching on the rice ball himself.

The carriage started slowly, the wheels creaking as they rolled over the bluestone road. Five carriages lined up and headed south along the main street towards the south gate. The town gradually shrank behind them, and the scenery on both sides of the road changed from wooden houses to farmland, and then from farmland to sparse woods.

Yuanda sized up Shuichi while eating his rice ball.

« Mr. Shu, your crow is quite well-behaved. Rarely see anyone keeping a crow. »

« Found it, » Shuichi said.

« Oh. » Yuanda nodded and didn’t ask further.

By noon the next day, they had passed the checkpoint in the southern part of the Land of Fire, and the road condition had worsened, turning from a packed dirt road into one mixed with gravel and mud. The carriage jolted violently, and Yuanda hugged the pile of cloth and sighed, fearing his remaining goods would be shaken to pieces.

In the afternoon, Shuichi sensed something unusual.

In the woods about 800 meters ahead, there were heat sources. Not animals, but people. Two heat sources ; one had Chakra activity a cut above ordinary people, Genin level, approaching Chunin. The other had no Chakra fluctuations—an ordinary person.

Shuichi said nothing and continued to close his eyes and rest.

After another ten minutes or so, the carriage passed a stretch of road flanked by dense forests.

« Stop ! »

A sharp shout came from ahead.

The carriage stopped abruptly. Yuanda nearly tumbled off the pile of cloth, grabbed the edge of the carriage to steady himself, and poked his head out to look forward.

Two people stood in the middle of the road.

The one in front wore a gray-black short jacket, with a dirty headband tied around his head ; the metal plate on the headband was worn so badly the emblem was unrecognizable. He held a short sword in his hand, with dried dark stains on the blade.

A rogue Ninja.

The one behind was sturdy, carrying a machete in his hand and two daggers tucked at his waist.

A rogue samurai.

Rogue Ninjas and rogue samurai teaming up was not uncommon on the borders. The Ninja was responsible for dealing with combat-capable targets, while the samurai was responsible for crowd control and moving loot.

The rogue Ninja in the middle of the road looked at the carriage, the corner of his mouth twisting.

« Leave the goods and money, and you can go. »

Yuanda’s face turned pale. He turned to look at the two escorts in front ; the two escorts also saw the two people in the middle of the road, and their expressions were not good either.

The escort on the right gritted his teeth and drew his sword.

« It’s just one Ninja— »

Before he could finish, the rogue Ninja moved.

His speed was much faster than the escort. In one step, he was in front of the escort, slashing horizontally with his short sword. The escort instinctively raised his sword to block, but the rogue Ninja’s sword had already changed moves—from a horizontal slash to a downward chop, and from a downward chop to a thrust. Within three moves, there was no pause, flowing as smoothly as water.

The escort’s chest was pierced, the tip of the blade protruding from his back.

From the attack to killing the first person, it took less than three seconds.

The other escort saw his companion killed, and his face changed drastically. He didn’t rush up, but turned and ran, running faster than a rabbit.

The rogue samurai walked towards the carriage with the machete in hand, blocking the path of escape.

Yuanda slumped in the carriage, his lips trembling.

He regretted it.

He regretted it very much now. He should have gritted his teeth and spent more money to hire a Ninja as an escort. Ordinary samurai were like paper in front of a Ninja, killed with one strike. Now it was all over ; the guards were dead or had run away, and in the carriage, there was only one merchant, one driver, and one hitchhiking wandering samurai.

A wandering samurai couldn’t possibly beat a Ninja ; the scene just now was proof.

The rogue Ninja in the middle of the road sheathed his sword and looked at the carriage. The rogue samurai stood aside, hoisting the machete onto his shoulder.

« Goods and money, » the rogue Ninja said again.

Yuanda’s hands were shaking as he slowly reached out to untie the money bag at his waist.

Inside the carriage, Shuichi opened his eyes.

He stood up, lifted the curtain, and stepped off the carriage.

The rogue Ninja saw him. The rogue samurai also saw him.

The rogue Ninja frowned. The rogue samurai sneered.

« Another one with a death wish, » the rogue Ninja said.

Shuichi drew his short sword.

The blade was not long, just over a foot, an ordinary iron blade with no Chakra attached. It was just an ordinary short sword held in the hands of an ordinary wandering samurai.

Then Shuichi moved.

The rogue Ninja’s pupils contracted sharply.

Too fast.

Shuichi’s starting speed far exceeded his prediction. No Chakra acceleration, no Taijutsu burst, just pure speed.

Shuichi’s short sword slashed diagonally from below, the blade grazing the rogue Ninja’s wrist. The Ninja instinctively raised his hand to block, but Shuichi’s sword had already changed from a diagonal slash to a horizontal wipe, the blade sliding from the wrist to the forearm, and then from the forearm to under the ribs.

The rogue Ninja looked down at his ribs. The fabric had been cut, the flesh was exposed, and blood was gushing out.

His hand could no longer hold the sword.

Shuichi’s short sword pulled out from under the ribs, followed by an upward flick, the tip of the blade piercing the rogue Ninja’s throat.

Shuichi released the hilt with his right hand and pressed it onto the rogue Ninja’s chest. His palm grew slightly warm, and a stream of Chakra was drawn out from the rogue Ninja’s body, flowing along his right hand into Shuichi’s meridians. The rogue Ninja’s body trembled slightly in Shuichi’s hand, and then completely lost its life.

Plunder Chakra.

The rogue samurai saw the whole process.

He saw his companion killed in four seconds. He knew now that he had to run.

The rogue samurai turned and rushed into the woods.

But just after turning and taking a few steps, Shuichi’s short sword pierced into his thoracic spine from behind, precisely severing the spinal cord. The rogue samurai’s legs instantly lost sensation, and he fell forward, slamming face-first into the mud.

Shuichi pressed his right hand onto the back of the rogue samurai’s head.

This time, what was plundered was not Chakra, but wisdom.

The concept of human wisdom.

Shuichi had previously fused the Monkey King’s forebrain cognitive structure into Lue. But a monkey’s wisdom was, in the end, a monkey’s, and there was still a chasm between it and humans.

The concept of human wisdom was completely different.

Shuichi’s perception unfolded within the rogue samurai’s brain ; the forebrain cognitive structure was more than a level more complex than the Monkey King’s. Shuichi carefully peeled it away, compressing the complete wisdom concept and locking it into his mental power barrier.

Plunder complete.

Shuichi stood up, wiped the blood off the short sword on the rogue samurai’s clothes, and then sheathed it.

Yuanda was stunned in the carriage.

He had seen the whole process. That taciturn wandering samurai had used an ordinary short sword to kill a Ninja in four seconds, and then killed a samurai with one strike.

But the precision and speed of those few strikes made Yuanda’s back go cold.

« Mr. Shu... » Yuanda’s voice was trembling, « You... you are... »

« An ordinary samurai, » Shuichi said.

Yuanda opened his mouth, wanting to say something, but in the end, nothing came out. He looked at the two corpses on the ground, then at Shuichi’s calm profile, and swallowed all his questions.

Some things shouldn’t be asked.

At this moment, the driver crawled out from under the carriage. Seeing that both men were dead, he breathed a sigh of relief, but his legs were still weak.

Shuichi returned to the carriage and sat down in the corner again.

Lue flew down from the crossbeam and landed on his shoulder. Shuichi used his mental power to push the newly plundered human wisdom concept into Lue’s body. Lue’s pupils contracted slightly, then slowly dilated. It didn’t react as violently as it did when fusing with the Monkey King’s wisdom last time, because Lue already had the Monkey King’s wisdom as a foundation ; this fusion was more like filling in details on the existing framework.

Lue tilted its head, its black round eyes looking at Shuichi.

« Shu, » it said. The voice was clearer than before, the articulation of syllables smoother, no longer jumping out one word at a time as before.

« Hmm. »

« I... can think faster, » Lue said.

This was the first time Lue had used such a complete sentence to express its feelings.

Shuichi looked at Lue’s black round eyes, and the corners of his mouth moved slightly.

« Digest it slowly. »

Lue nodded. Then it tucked its neck and crouched down again.

The carriage set off again. Yuanda sat in the carriage, stealing glances at Shuichi from time to time. The way he looked at Shuichi now was completely different from before—previously it was vigilance and calculation, now it was awe and relief.

He was glad he had let this man hitch a ride.
