# Chapitre 3 — The Eaglet

On the fifth day after awakening his memories, Shuichi noticed that something was wrong with his skin.

When washing his face in the early morning, the face reflected in the basin was still the round face of a five-year-old child, but when he pressed his fingertips against his cheeks, the sensation was wrong. Beneath the skin, it felt like it was lined with a thin layer of cardboard, and the rebound after pressing was half a beat slower than normal skin.

He turned over the back of his right hand and examined it carefully in the morning light. The scratch marks left by a stray cat on the back of his hand had already scabbed over, but the skin around the scab faintly shimmered with an unnatural light gray luster. It felt subtly rough to the touch, like the finest texture on tree bark.

Carapace Hardness. A constitution-type ability plundered from a rhinoceros beetle.

Shuichi stared at that small patch of gray skin, a wave of instinctive nausea churning in his stomach.

It wasn’t pain. It didn’t even hinder his movement. But watching his own skin slowly becoming unlike human skin—this feeling was more unbearable to him than any physical suffering. The aesthetic inertia from living twenty years as an ordinary person in his past life was screaming : « This is not how you are supposed to look. »

« Shuichi ? Are you done washing ? » Minako’s voice came from the kitchen.

« Almost done. » Shuichi suppressed the discomfort in his heart, quickly rubbed his face a few times, covered the back of his hand with his sleeve, and walked into the dining room.

Breakfast was miso soup and leftover rice balls from last night. Minako sat opposite him, holding a teacup in her hands. Her gaze passed through the steam as she looked at Shuichi, and she suddenly said, « You’ve been running off to the Naka River every day lately. »

Shuichi’s action of biting the rice ball paused.

« Mom isn’t trying to stop you. » Minako put down the teacup, her tone calm but hiding a hint of seriousness. « But there is a Training Ground upstream of the Naka River where Ninjas often practice Ninjutsu. If you run too close, and you accidentally get hurt by Fire Release or shuriken... »

« Training Ground ? » Shuichi raised his head, his eyes brightening. But he immediately covered it up, putting on a curious, childlike expression. « What kind of Training Ground ? »

« Just an ordinary Training Ground. There are target posts, obstacles, and a few large rocks by the river for people to practice walking on water. » Minako looked at her son’s suddenly shining eyes, assuming he was just yearning for the Ninja world, and smiled. « When you go to the Ninja Academy next year, the teachers will take you there. »

« Then can’t I go now ? »

« It’s too dangerous. You are still young, and you don’t have Chakra yet. If you run into an accident, you won’t be able to run away. »

Shuichi nodded obediently and continued eating. But his brain was already spinning rapidly. A Training Ground. This meant that the area had man-made facilities and more traces of human activity. And places with dense human activity often attract a specific type of animal.

Scavenging crows. And birds of prey that build nests on cliffs.

The latter was exactly what he needed.

Ninja Training Grounds are built in mountainous areas, and in order not to accidentally injure villagers, they are usually chosen in areas with undulating terrain on the edge of the Village. When Shuichi was observing Minato on the Naka River Stone Bridge, he noticed that there was an exposed rock wall on the north side of the Training Ground, about twenty meters high, with a few crooked pine trees on top. That kind of terrain was the ideal nesting site for raptors like hawks and falcons.

But he needed a reasonable explanation to get close to that area.

Shuichi put down his chopsticks, thought for three seconds, raised his head, and asked in the most innocent tone : « Mom, did Dad often go to that Training Ground in the past ? »

Minako’s hand paused slightly.

« Dad’s... mementos, is there anything of his left there ? » Shuichi carefully controlled his tone, not letting it sound too mature. « I want to go see the place where Dad practiced shuriken. »

This was the most reasonable excuse he could think of. It was unreasonable enough that a five-year-old child saying it would be dismissed as childish prattle, yet reasonable enough that a mother who had lost her husband couldn’t bear to refuse.

Minako was silent for a long while. Shuichi could see her eyes turning slightly red, but she was trying hard to control herself. Finally, she took a deep breath and reached out to touch Shuichi’s head.

« There are no mementos left there. » Her voice was very soft. « But if you want to go see it... let’s go tomorrow. Tomorrow, Mom will take you there. »

« Mhm. »

Shuichi lowered his head, hiding the flash of light in his eyes.

The plan worked.

---

The next morning, Minako held his hand and walked through the path on the north side of the Uchiha Clan Grounds, walking upstream along the Naka River for about twenty minutes. The Training Ground was larger than Shuichi had imagined, at least the size of two soccer fields. The ground was packed earth, sparsely scattered with wooden target posts and a few stone walls used for practicing climbing. Sure enough, by the river, there were several flat, large rocks, their surfaces worn smooth by countless feet.

« Your father liked that side the best. » Minako pointed to a row of target posts on the east side of the Training Ground. « His shurikenjutsu was the best among his peers. »

Shuichi followed her gaze. The target posts were already old, and the scars on them were layered, making it impossible to tell which one was left by his father. Minako stood beside him in silence for a long time ; the wind blew her hair, mingling with the sound of the Naka River in the distance.

Shuichi stood with her quietly for a few minutes, then let go of her hand.

« Mom, I’m going to go look by the river. »

« Don’t go too far. »

« Mhm. »

Shuichi jogged away from the main area of the Training Ground, bypassed the large rocks by the river, and approached the rock wall on the north side. From the direction of the Training Ground, the rock wall was partially obscured by a few dense oak trees. But when he walked to the base of the rock wall, his view suddenly opened up.

The bare rock was over twenty meters high, its surface weathered into a rough, off-white texture. The crooked pine trees at the top of the rock wall happened to form a small platform when viewed from a specific angle, and on the platform were piled dried pine branches and small bones.

A nest.

Shuichi’s heart skipped a beat.

He crouched down, using a protruding rock at the base of the wall as cover, and narrowed his eyes to observe the nest carefully. The distance was too great to see if there were any hatchlings in the nest. But the way the pine branches were stacked was very new ; the pine needles were still yellow-green and not completely dried out.

This meant the nest had been refurbished recently. It was spring now, the breeding season for raptors.

He walked halfway around the base of the rock wall, trying to find a route to climb up. Although the rock wall was steep, the Climbing Ability he had plundered from the Tree Frog allowed his fingers and toes to easily grip the tiny indentations on the rock surface. Theoretically, he could climb up with his bare hands.

But the problem was that Minako was not far away. He couldn’t do such a thing right under his mother’s nose.

Besides, even if he climbed up and found a hatchling, how would he explain suddenly having a hawk in his hands ?

Shuichi bit his lower lip, forcing himself to calm down. He couldn’t rush. The location of the nest had been confirmed. What he needed was an opportunity—an opportunity to come here alone.

Just as he was preparing to retreat back to the Training Ground, a sharp cry came from above.

The sound came from the top of the rock wall, short and obviously panicked. Shuichi looked up sharply ; a grayish-brown hatchling was sliding off the edge of the nest, its wings flapping wildly, but it clearly couldn’t fly yet. During its fall, it hit a protruding rock on the wall with a dull thud, then tumbled and rolled into a cluster of bushes at the base of the rock wall.

Shuichi was stunned for less than a second, then took off running toward the bushes.

He parted the branches of the bushes and saw the hatchling.

Grayish-brown down, a hooked beak—it was a hawk, but he didn’t recognize the species. The hatchling was about the size of two of his fists, more than ten times larger than a common sparrow. Its left wing was drooping, apparently injured during the fall. One claw was tightly gripping the bush branch, and its small, shiny black eyes were staring at Shuichi with vigilance and terror.

Shuichi slowly crouched down and extended his right hand.

No Plunder. He controlled the burning sensation in his palm. He just gently brought his fingers close to the hatchling, using the sensory function that came with the Plunder ability to probe its body, just like when he first touched the sparrow.

The information surging within the hatchling far exceeded that of a sparrow. A strong heartbeat, developing wing muscles, lung capacity far exceeding that of small birds. And its constitution foundation was very solid. This hatchling’s physical condition was sufficient to carry far more and stronger foreign abilities than ordinary birds.

Shuichi withdrew his right hand, the corners of his mouth couldn’t help but curl up slightly.

A candidate for a flight pet. And one that came uninvited.

But its left wing was indeed injured. If not treated, even if it survived, it might affect its future flying ability.

Shuichi took off his jacket, a thin navy blue short coat, and carefully wrapped the hatchling inside. The hatchling struggled twice, but its injured wing limited its movements. Shuichi gently held its body through the fabric, leaving its head exposed, and then walked quickly toward the Training Ground.

« Mom ! »

Minako was stunned for a moment when she saw him running over : his jacket was gone, he was holding a bundle of cloth in his arms, and a small, furry head was peeking out from the bundle.

« Shuichi, this is... »

« It fell from the rock wall. » Shuichi looked up, his eyes filled with undisguised earnestness. « Its wing is injured. Mom, can I keep it ? »

Minako crouched down and parted the short coat to check the hatchling’s injury. Her husband was a Ninja ; although she wasn’t one herself, as a Ninja’s wife, she knew how to make basic judgments about wounds. She gently pressed on the hatchling’s left wing, and the hatchling let out a faint cry.

« The bone shouldn’t be broken. But it fell quite hard. » Minako frowned. « Shuichi, this is a hawk. Hawks are very difficult to tame. »

« I will take good care of it. »

Minako looked at her son’s serious expression, then at the hatchling, its injured wing, its terrified eyes, and her child who had lost his father holding it. She was silent for a few seconds, and finally sighed.

« You can only keep it until it can fly. If it flies away, it flies away ; you cannot chase after it. »

« Mhm ! » Shuichi nodded.

What he didn’t tell his mother was : he didn’t need to chase it. What he needed was for this eaglet to be able to carry the abilities he would strip over—Flight Instinct, future reconnaissance sensory abilities, and perhaps more. When it could truly fly, it would no longer be an ordinary hawk. It would become his eyes, circling above Konoha, seeing everything he wanted to see.

On the way back, Shuichi held the warm bundle in his arms, a continuous pulsation coming from the palm of his right hand.

The sparrow’s Flight Instinct was still lying quietly in the mental slot, waiting for a host. And he had already found that host.

It was just that he couldn’t strip the ability yet. The eaglet’s injury hadn’t healed ; stripping the ability would consume its stamina and might even worsen its injury. He needed to wait for its wing to heal, for its body to recover to the point where it could withstand the impact of foreign abilities.

Shuichi looked down at the eaglet’s shiny black eyes and whispered : « You will get better. And then you will fly higher than any other hawk. »

The eaglet blinked and tucked its head into the folds of the short coat.

The sound of the Naka River gradually faded behind them. In the sky above, an adult hawk circled over the rock wall, emitting a long cry, not knowing if it was searching for the lost hatchling or marking territory for the new season.

Shuichi looked up at the sky, then withdrew his gaze and continued walking forward.
