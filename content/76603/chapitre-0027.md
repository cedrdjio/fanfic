# Chapitre 27 — Two Years

When the brown bear fell, Shuichi’s Kunai was still embedded between its third and fourth ribs.

There was no struggle.

There was no dying roar.

The bear had already died before it hit the ground—the Kunai had pierced through the gap between the ribs into its heart, and the Chakra infused into the blade had destroyed the heart muscle the moment it pierced through.

The four-hundred-jin torso slammed onto the pine-needle-covered ground, the dull thud echoing twice through the forest before fading away.

Shuichi deactivated the two-tomoe Sharingan in his eyes, pulled the Kunai from the bear, wiped it on the bear’s fur, and returned it to his weapon pouch.

The bear was his steady source of Vitality.

Large in size and dense in Vitality, one bear was worth ten wild boars.

Shuichi crouched beside the bear’s carcass for a moment, waiting for the wound to finish bleeding, then pressed his right hand onto its forehead.

Plundering Vitality was as steady as pouring water into a cup.

The bear’s Vitality was dense but slow ; it surged in from his right palm, sank down along his meridians, and gathered into a warm mass near his Dantian.

He kept about thirty percent for himself.

The remaining seventy percent was channeled out through his right hand and pushed into Lue’s chest.

Lue was perched on a fallen log, its body trembling slightly as it received this flow of Vitality.

Its iron-gray wings, with a span of over two meters, half-opened, quivered for about three seconds, and then folded back.

Lue tilted its head to look at Shuichi and let out a short, low chirp.

« You’ve grown quite a bit, » Shuichi said.

Lue looked down at its own talons, then back at Shuichi.

Two years ago, Lue was only 1.3 meters ; when it perched on Shuichi’s shoulder, its tail could still brush against the corner of his mouth.

Now, when Lue perched on his shoulder, its folded wings extended outward, forcing Shuichi to tilt his head slightly while walking.

According to the normal lifespan and Growth curve of a hawk, it would take at least four to five years for a hawk to grow to a two-meter wingspan.

Lue had only taken two years.

It wasn’t because it was special.

It was because, starting a year ago, every time Shuichi hunted prey, he shared a small portion of the Vitality with it.

It had now been two years since Shuichi awakened his memories, and he had grown from a powerless six-year-old child into someone who could freely enter and exit the Forest of Death.

In these two years, Shuichi had digested the concept of Growth twice, his Sharingan had successfully grown to the two-tomoe stage, and he had absorbed various abilities that could enhance his physique, though it was a pity that the mental-series abilities obtainable from animals were limited.

Moreover, two years of hunting had allowed Shuichi to form an automatic classification system in his mind.

Prey was divided into only two types : those used for absorbing Vitality, and those used for Plundering abilities.

Bears, wild boars, deer, mountain antelopes—these types of large mammals were the main source of Vitality.

The larger the size, the denser the Vitality ; one bear was worth ten wild boars, and one wild boar was worth five Red Foxes.

Shuichi kept eighty percent for himself to maintain physique advancement, and fed the rest to Lue.

Over the past two years, Lue’s physical foundation had completely surpassed the category of a normal hawk.

Its bone density was at least twice as high as an ordinary hawk, and the explosive power of its muscle fibers, based on Shuichi’s Sharingan estimations, was close to that of a medium-sized feline.

Tree vipers, geckos, crows—these types of small creatures that possessed special abilities were the sources of his powers.

What he Plundered from them was not Vitality, but Infrared Sensing, footpad adhesion, spatial memory, and the concept of Wisdom, which he intended to target next.

These types of prey had thin Vitality, and absorbing them provided almost no advancement for his physique, but the abilities they carried were things large mammals could not provide.

He alternated between the two types of prey.

Large prey maintained a continuous supply of physique and Vitality, while small special prey filled the gaps in Lue’s abilities.

Over the two years, the digestion speed of Infrared Sensing and auditory positioning had nearly doubled due to the continuous infusion of Vitality from large prey ; Infrared Sensing, which originally would have taken three months, was completely internalized by Lue in just two months.

Shuichi estimated his own strength to be around the level of a Chunin, and he could even rank among the better ones at that level, but his amount of Chakra was still insufficient.

Shuichi dragged the bear’s carcass to a fallen large oak tree and used his Kunai to make a few incisions to drain the blood.

The bear skin could actually be sold, but a bear skin of this size would be hard to explain if brought back to the village.

He covered the carcass with a few thick pine branches, stood up, and patted the dirt off his hands.

Lue flew up from the fallen log and circled half a turn above the pine forest.

The thermal signal was transmitted back.

The information transmitted back by Lue indicated that there were three small mammals active under the leaf litter on the east side of the pine forest.

In the direction of the river bend to the north, a medium-sized heat source was moving, heading upstream.

Just like usual.

Detect prey, locate prey, transmit information.

Shuichi opened his eyes.

Lue was still circling, its wingspan casting a constantly moving shadow in the afternoon sun.

Two years ago, when he found that fledgling hawk with a broken wing on the rock wall of the Training Ground, Lue’s talons couldn’t even grip a tree branch steadily.

Now, it could lock onto a burrow three feet deep beneath the leaf litter from high in the sky.

Its Growth speed was even faster than the fastest speed Shuichi had anticipated.

But Lue had a shortcoming.

It wasn’t that its abilities were insufficient ; its abilities were already excessive.

Rather, something was missing in the coordination between its abilities.

Self-judgment capability.

Lue could detect heat sources, distinguish sizes, and compare the distribution of heat sources between today and yesterday—this was progress that only came after Shuichi had dismantled the spatial memory module from a crow and given it to Lue two months ago.

But Lue still couldn’t judge the value of prey.

When it saw a deer or a wild boar, the information it transmitted back was essentially the same : location, size, direction of movement.

Was the deer worth chasing ?

Was the wild boar injured ?

Shuichi needed Lue to have Wisdom.

Not instrumental judgment, but the ability to decide the priority of targets on its own during reconnaissance.

If Lue had Wisdom, the hunting efficiency in the Forest of Death could increase several times over, because Lue could filter targets from high in the sky by itself, Shuichi wouldn’t need to personally approach to confirm every time, and communication in the future would also be more convenient.

But that wasn’t all.

Shuichi leaned against the oak trunk, looking up at Lue’s circling shadow.

Over the past two years, Lue had grown from a fledgling with a broken wing into a top-tier hunting hawk with a two-meter wingspan, and with four layers of perception stacked, no prey in the Forest of Death could escape its eyes.

But Lue remained just a tool.

An extremely excellent tool, but a tool nonetheless.

Shuichi wanted more than just a tool.

He needed more than just a hawk for reconnaissance.

He needed a capable assistant that could understand his intentions, execute complex instructions independently, and make correct judgments without his direct command.

To become an assistant, what Lue needed was not just the ability to judge prey ; it needed Wisdom—not the wisdom to understand commands, but the wisdom to understand emotions and to think for itself.

Only an assistant that could understand emotions could truly understand Shuichi’s intentions.

Only an assistant that could use language could fully convey the information scouted from high in the sky, rather than relying on Shuichi closing his eyes to sense heat source signals.

This required complete Wisdom.

Shuichi had listed targets in his mind from which he could Plunder the concept of Wisdom.

Crows—he had already tried that before, having dismantled sub-modules for spatial memory and causality, which Lue had digested well.

But a crow’s wisdom was, after all, at a bird’s level.

Although its cognitive structure was much more complex than a field mouse’s, it was a grade lower compared to primates.

Crows could not provide language ability and emotional modules.

He couldn’t find a suitable human target now, so he could only set his sights on monkeys.

The cognitive structure of primates was closest to humans, and only from primates could he strip away the complete concept of Wisdom that included language and emotion.

But the Forest of Death was not a natural habitat for monkeys.

Shuichi had once seen footprints left by a monkey near the cliff on the north slope ; five toe prints with mud on the stone, the spacing and depth were not those of a raccoon dog or a badger ; it could only be a primate.

But after following them for half a mile, the footprints stopped ; the monkey had probably climbed up the cliff wall and descended the mountain from the other side.

During several subsequent hunts, he had intentionally detoured to the cliff area to take another look, but he had never seen new footprints again.

If he couldn’t find monkeys, the plan for the concept of Wisdom would have to be shelved—or he would have to settle for the next best thing and use a crow.

But a crow’s wisdom was only enough to solve the problem of « judging the value of prey, » and could not solve « emotion and language. »

After Lue was equipped with the crow’s wisdom, it would become a smarter reconnaissance tool, but it would not become an assistant.

Shuichi did not want to settle for the next best thing.

Lue deserved better.

The direction was set.

But today was Sunday, this week’s hunting was already over, the bear’s Vitality hadn’t been fully absorbed yet, and Lue also needed time to digest that flow of Vitality just now.

Moreover, a year ago, Shuichi had set a rule for himself : Monday to Friday were for school and normal life, and hunting was only for the weekends.

He would go look again next week.

If there weren’t any near the cliff, he would look deeper.

The Forest of Death was big enough ; there was bound to be a corner hiding monkeys.

It was still early when Shuichi walked onto the stone bridge.

Lue flew up from his shoulder and landed on a plum branch ; the wind fanned by its wings blew a few dead leaves on the sandy ground of the courtyard to the base of the wall.

The plum branch bent into a visible arc under Lue’s weight ; now, Lue’s weight had already exceeded twice that of a normal adult hawk.

Shuichi took a look.

The plan to change Lue’s living quarters also needed to be put on the agenda.
