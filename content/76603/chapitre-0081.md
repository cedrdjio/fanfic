# Chapitre 81 — Turning Point

Nagato walked through the rain, holding Yahiko’s body.

Konan followed behind, not speaking, just following.

Her paper was soaked through by the rain and clung to her body, but she didn’t care.

Rainwater streamed down her hair, mixing with tears, but it was impossible to tell what was rain and what was tears.

She walked very slowly, her steps staggering, like a doll that had been drained of all its strength.

The drizzle hit Nagato’s face and streamed down Yahiko’s cold arms.

Yahiko’s body was already completely cold, from his chest to his fingertips, like wood soaked in rainwater.

His orange hair was wet with rain, plastered against his forehead, and his eyes were closed, as if he were sleeping.

But he was not breathing.

Nagato could feel it.

His arms holding Yahiko were going numb, but he did not let go.

Yahiko’s weight pressed against his chest, heavy, like a stone.

He did not use Ninjutsu to block the rain, nor did he quicken his pace.

He just walked forward step by step, each step treading into the muddy water, the splashing spray soaking his pant legs.

The rain soaked through his clothes and streamed down his back ; the cold sensation made him shiver slightly.

But he did not stop.

The rain in the Land of Rain never stopped, but it had been falling for an exceptionally long time today.

The gray sky hung very low, as if it were about to collapse.

The sound of the rain drowned out everything—the sound of footsteps, the sound of breathing, and the occasional sobs from Konan.

The entire world was left with nothing but rain, boundless and endless rain.

Nagato did not know how long he had been walking.

Long enough that his arms began to go numb, long enough that his legs began to feel weak, and long enough that Konan’s footsteps grew slower and slower.

He did not look at the road, nor did he look at the direction ; he just walked forward on instinct.

They walked to the edge of the small town, where there was an abandoned shrine.

Half of the shrine’s roof had collapsed, with roof tiles scattered on the ground, covered in moss.

The statue was only half-remaining ; the face could no longer be seen, leaving only a vague outline.

The incense burner was filled with rainwater, with a few withered leaves floating in it.

Weeds grew from the cracks in the floor and covered the steps, as if this shrine had been forgotten for many years.

Nagato walked into the shrine, his footsteps echoing in the empty space.

He walked to the front of the statue, slowly knelt down, and placed Yahiko on the floor.

Yahiko’s body had become cold in the rain, but Nagato’s arms remained stiffly in the position of holding him, as if he could not let go for a moment.

It took him a few seconds to pry his fingers open one by one, letting Yahiko lie flat on the floor.

Yahiko’s clothes were soaked through, clinging to his body, revealing his thin silhouette.

Nagato reached out to tidy Yahiko’s clothes and brushed his disheveled hair behind his ears.

Yahiko’s face looked pale in the dim light, but very peaceful.

He looked as if he were asleep.

If only he would wake up, open his eyes, and say with a smile, « Wait for my good news. »

But he would not wake up.

Nagato knelt beside Yahiko, the Rinnegan glowing faintly in the dimness.

He did not speak, just looked at Yahiko’s face, as if thinking about something.

Konan walked over and knelt beside the body.

Her shoulders were trembling, and paper drifted from her body, scattering onto the floor.

She did not make a sound, just knelt there, looking at Yahiko’s face.

Rainwater dripped from the leaky roof of the shrine, hitting Yahiko’s chest, where the place the Kunai had pierced had already been covered by Konan with paper.

She reached out and touched Yahiko’s face, her fingers trembling.

« Yahiko. » Her voice was very soft, so soft it was almost drowned out by the sound of the rain. « Yahiko, wake up. »

Yahiko did not respond.

« You said you would come back. » Konan said, her voice beginning to tremble. « You said to wait for your good news. You lied. »

Yahiko still did not respond.

Konan’s fingers lingered on Yahiko’s cold cheek for a long time, then slowly withdrew.

She lowered her head, her shoulders trembling violently, and finally burst into tears.

She cried for a long time.

Long enough that the sky darkened, long enough that the sound of the rain became clearer, and long enough that her crying turned from intense to low sobs.

She cried until she had no strength left, curled up in the corner, her back against the wall, her head drooping onto her knees, and fell into a deep sleep.

Nagato kept watch alone beside Yahiko.

The rain was still falling, but the sound had quieted.

The drizzle became fine and dense, like countless silver threads hanging down from the sky.

Moonlight leaked through the gaps in the broken roof, falling on the broken face of the statue, on Yahiko’s body, and on Nagato’s kneeling back.

Nagato did not move.

He knelt beside Yahiko, his hands on his knees, his back held very straight.

The Rinnegan glowed faintly in the darkness, the pale purple light flickering, as if breathing.

But he did not use it to do anything.

He just knelt there, looking at Yahiko’s face.

Yahiko’s face was very peaceful.

He had no pain when he died, at least that was how it appeared on the surface.

The Kunai had pierced his heart, killing him instantly.

He did not struggle, did not leave any last words, and did not even close his eyes.

Nagato had helped him close his eyes.

But that smile was gone forever.

Yahiko was always smiling.

He smiled when he said he would bring peace, he smiled when he said « Wait for my good news, » and he smiled when he believed that dialogue could solve problems.

There was light in his smile, hope, and a power that made people want to believe.

Now he could no longer smile.

Nagato looked at Yahiko’s face for a long time.

The moonlight moved across Yahiko’s face, sliding from his forehead to the bridge of his nose, and from the bridge of his nose to his lips.

Yahiko’s face looked pale in the moonlight, as if made of wax.

Nagato’s fingers tightened on the back of Yahiko’s cold hand.

He remembered many things.

He remembered the first time he met Yahiko.

At that time he was still small, having just lost his parents, just awakened the Rinnegan, walking blankly in the rain.

Yahiko walked over, handed him a piece of bread, and asked him, « Are you hungry ? »

He remembered Yahiko saying, « The three of us are a family. »

He remembered Yahiko saying, « I will change this country. »

He remembered Yahiko saying, « As long as we sit down and talk, there is no problem that cannot be solved. »

Yahiko believed these words.

Yahiko believed that peace could be achieved through understanding.

Yahiko believed that human nature was inherently good, believed that great nations and small nations could sit together as equals, and believed that war could be avoided.

So Yahiko went alone.

So he died.

Nagato lowered his head, his forehead resting on the back of Yahiko’s cold hand.

The patterns of the Rinnegan rotated slowly in the darkness, the pale purple light flickering.

« You were wrong. » Nagato’s voice was very soft, so soft that only he could hear it. « Yahiko, you were wrong. »

Dialogue cannot solve problems.

Understanding cannot bring peace.

Believing in the goodness of human nature will only lead one to die in a cold warehouse.

Nagato lifted his head, the Rinnegan rotating in the darkness.

There were no tears on his face, only rainwater dripping from the broken roof, hitting his cheeks, as if crying for him.

Just as Nagato was filled with regret, the shadows under the floor began to flow.

Nagato suddenly lifted his head, the Rinnegan rotating in the darkness.

He could feel something rising from the ground.

The shadows slowly coalesced in the moonlight ; a white figure emerged first, pure white all over, with only a human-like silhouette, no facial features, and only a single eye rotating in the center of its head.

Then came a black figure, seeping out from the depths of the shadows, its body pitch black, with the same appearance and the same single eye.

This was White Zetsu and Black Zetsu slowly merging into one in the moonlight.

Nagato looked at this fusion, not retreating, nor speaking.

He was sizing up this thing.

The power of the Rinnegan flickered in the darkness ; Nagato could feel a strange aura on this fusion.

It was not Chakra, but something more ancient, like the smell of soil that had settled for a long time, like a stone buried underground for many years.

« Who are you ? » Nagato’s voice was hoarse, as if he had not spoken for a long time.

Black Zetsu spoke, its voice seemingly coming from the deepest depths of the earth, carrying an ancient echo, like wind passing through an empty cave.

« I am Zetsu. » It said. « I am the will of the world. »

Nagato did not speak.

He looked at this fusion, the Rinnegan rotating in the darkness.

Black Zetsu continued, « This world has been at war for thousands of years. It is only because of the desires of certain people that there is constant death, suffering, and separation. »

As it spoke, Black Zetsu looked at Yahiko’s body.

« This is just one time in countless cycles, but it will not be the last. »

Nagato’s fingers tightened on the back of Yahiko’s hand, his knuckles turning white.

« The Senju and Uchiha have fought for hundreds of years. The Land of Fire and the Land of Water have fought for over a hundred years. Konoha and Iwagakure, Sunagakure, and Kumogakure have fought over and over again. » Black Zetsu’s voice echoed in the empty shrine.

Nagato did not speak.

« Peace is never achieved through understanding. » Black Zetsu slowly approached Nagato, its single eye flickering in the moonlight.

« Peace is maintained through power. Only by making everyone feel the same pain will they stop creating pain. »

Nagato looked at Yahiko’s body, looking at Yahiko’s closed eyes.

« You are the chosen savior. » Black Zetsu said. « That pair of Rinnegan is the proof. Those are the eyes of the Six Paths Sage ; the world chose to give them to you for a reason. You should not be kneeling here. You should make this world feel the same pain, and then end it. »

Nagato’s Rinnegan rotated slightly.

« Yahiko believed in dialogue. » Nagato said, his voice very soft. « He believed that as long as we sat down to talk, we could solve problems. »

« Yahiko is dead. » Black Zetsu said. « Because he believed in the wrong things. »

Nagato was silent.

Black Zetsu did not urge him.

It just stood there, waiting.

White Zetsu gently added, « If you need it, you can start at any time. We will help you. »

Nagato did not answer.

He looked at Yahiko’s face for a long time.

The moonlight moved across Yahiko’s face, sliding from his forehead to the bridge of his nose, and from the bridge of his nose to his lips.

Yahiko’s face looked pale in the moonlight, his orange hair wetly plastered against his forehead.

Nagato’s fingers slowly tightened on the back of Yahiko’s cold hand, his knuckles turning white.

He looked at Yahiko’s face, at Yahiko’s closed eyes, and at Yahiko’s lips that would no longer smile.

« You were wrong, Yahiko. » Nagato said, his voice very soft. « But I will complete it for you. »

He would use another way.

He stood up.

He was going to achieve Yahiko’s dream in another way, using pain to make the world understand peace.

His Rinnegan rotated in the darkness, the pale purple light brighter than before, as if it were burning.

He looked at the fusion of Black Zetsu and White Zetsu, not speaking, just looking.

His gaze shifted from Black Zetsu’s single eye to the leaky roof of the shrine, toward the gray sky, toward the endless rain.

The rain in the Land of Rain never stopped.

But a new turning point was being born.
