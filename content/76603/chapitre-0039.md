# Chapitre 39 — Falsehood

Wednesday morning, Ninja Academy, Class 3-A.

Teacher Mitomon stood on the podium giving a theory lesson on Chakra nature transformation. Shuichi sat by the window, pen in hand, eyes fixed on the blackboard, but his mind was elsewhere.

The five basic Chakra nature transformations — Fire, Wind, Lightning, Earth, Water. Teacher Mitomon had drawn the diagram of the five elements' mutual generation and restriction on the blackboard, marking the strengths and weaknesses of each nature with chalk. Looking at the diagram, Shuichi found it slightly amusing. He had just finished digesting the Lightning Release Nature three days ago and had only been introduced to the Fire Release Nature a month and a half prior ; the theories Teacher Mitomon was lecturing on were long past his current stage.

« Student Shuichi, what are the three basic types of Fire Release form transformation ? » Teacher Mitomon suddenly called on him.

« Flame type, projectile type, and line type. » Shuichi stood up and answered.

« Sit down. » Teacher Mitomon nodded and continued the lecture.

Shuichi sat down, his gaze shifting to the window. On the playground, first-year students were practicing Taijutsu. He could see Obito’s shadow from afar ; that thin figure was practicing front lunges, his movements much more standardized than last semester, but his center of gravity still occasionally tilted.

Shuichi watched for a moment, then withdrew his gaze.

If one practiced normally in the academy, how long would it take to go from a Chunin-level Chakra reserve to a Jonin-level ? The increase in Chakra reserves was the slowest ; it wasn’t like Ninjutsu, which could be broken through with insight, nor like Taijutsu, which could be honed through repetitive practice. Chakra reserves simply required piling on time and training volume.

Shuichi looked at the five-element diagram Teacher Mitomon had drawn on the blackboard and sighed inwardly.

He was sitting in this classroom now, listening to a theory lesson he had mastered a year or two ago, pretending to be an ordinary third-year student. Spending six hours a day at the academy, the things he learned were of almost no help to him. After returning home, he would practice Fire Release for an hour or two, and on weekends, he would go to the Forest of Death to hunt bears. After a week, his progress was negligible.

The mindset of a transmigrator really couldn’t be relied upon. Three years ago, he had sworn to « advance steadily without impatience, » only to be beaten back to his original form in two weeks.

But then again, advancing steadily was indeed too slow.

---

After school in the afternoon, Shuichi walked home along the Naka River. Tetsuya ran up from behind.

« Shuichi ! » Tetsuya panted. « Tomorrow’s Taijutsu class has a test on footwork transitions. How’s your practice coming along ? »

« It’s alright, » Shuichi said.

« I practiced for two hours yesterday, but I still get stuck occasionally. » Tetsuya scratched his head. « That transition from a backstep to a front lunge you taught me—I always pause half a beat too long. »

« Don’t rush to shift your center of gravity forward, » Shuichi said. « When the backstep lands, keep your center of gravity on the back foot. Wait until you’re steady before pushing forward. If you rush the forward shift, the flow breaks in the middle. »

« Oh— » Tetsuya thought for a moment. « Center of gravity steady, then push. Right, right, right. I’ll give that a try. »

The two walked along the Naka River for a while, then separated at the fork in the road. Shuichi watched Tetsuya’s retreating figure, and the feeling of strangeness in his heart grew stronger.

Tetsuya had limited talent ; even with two more years of practicing footwork, he would probably only be at a mid-level among Genin. But if Shuichi went out to hunt Ninjas and Plunder to get stronger, his strength in two years might be that of an elite Jonin or even higher. By then, the gap between Tetsuya and Shuichi wouldn’t just be « occasionally getting stuck, » but a completely different level.

Shuichi crossed the stone bridge and walked home along the path by the river.

He needed to seriously think of a plan.

The idea of leaving Konoha to hunt Ninjas and Plunder to get stronger had been spinning in his mind since testing Lightning Release yesterday. But leaving wasn’t a simple matter.

Shuichi couldn’t just disappear.

Minako was still here. If he suddenly vanished, Minako would go crazy looking for him. The clan would send people to search, and relatives and neighbors would help. An eight-year-old child going missing wouldn’t cause a huge stir, but it wouldn’t be small either. Most importantly, Shuichi didn’t want to lose his identity as a Konoha Ninja, nor did he want to make Minako sad.

He had to find a win-win solution.

Shuichi’s existence in the academy as an ordinary third-year student, attending classes, going home, and chatting with classmates every day—these seemingly ordinary daily routines were his greatest cover. As long as he appeared at the academy and returned home on time every day, no one would suspect anything.

Therefore, Shuichi needed to satisfy one condition simultaneously : maintain a normal existence as « Uchiha Shuichi » in Konoha.

This condition seemed contradictory. A person cannot be in two places at once.

But a Shadow Clone could.

The Shadow Clone is a clone constructed of Chakra, possessing the main body’s appearance, memories, and combat abilities. If Shuichi created a Shadow Clone to stay in Konoha, taking his place at school, returning home, eating with Minako, and chatting with Obito, then his true self could leave Konoha to hunt Ninjas outside. No one would discover that « Uchiha Shuichi » had left Konoha, because « Uchiha Shuichi » would still be living normally in the Village.

But the premise was that the problem of the Shadow Clone’s limitations had to be solved.

---

After returning home, Shuichi headed to the small grove to experiment with the Shadow Clone.

He had already learned many common Jonin-level Ninjutsu while digesting Sakumo’s combat experience, such as the Body Replacement Technique, Transformation Technique, and Rope Escape Technique ; the Shadow Clone Technique was naturally among them. As a Kage-level powerhouse, Sakumo’s combat experience included the usage of Shadow Clones—hand seals, Chakra distribution, and techniques for maintaining the clone—all engraved in Sakumo’s neural pathways. After Shuichi digested the combat experience, these Ninjutsu felt as natural as riding a bicycle ; no extra learning was required.

He stood under a tree and performed the hand seals.

The cross seal.

Chakra split into two streams from his body, one remaining in the main body and the other pushed to his side. A distortion appeared in the air, and then a person exactly like Shuichi appeared beside him.

Shadow Clone.

Shuichi looked at his clone. Appearance, clothing, expression—completely identical. The clone also looked at Shuichi, its eyes as calm as Shuichi’s.

« How does it feel ? » Shuichi asked.

« Like looking in a mirror, » the clone said. The voice was exactly the same as Shuichi’s.

Shuichi reached out and touched the clone’s shoulder. It didn’t disappear. His fingers felt the solid touch—the fabric of the clothes, the bones of the shoulder, the temperature of the skin. The Shadow Clone was not a phantom that would disappear upon touch ; it had a physical form, could be touched, could speak, and could act.

Shuichi withdrew his hand, took a Kunai from his Ninja tool pouch, and gently scratched the clone’s arm. The Kunai slid across the skin, leaving a shallow red mark. The clone frowned ; it could feel pain.

Seeing the clone’s reaction, Shuichi increased his force, and the Kunai stabbed into the clone’s upper arm.

Poof !

The clone dissipated.

Like a cloud of mist blown away by the wind, fragments of Chakra drifted in the air for a second before vanishing. At the same time, a wave of memories and Chakra flowed back from the clone’s position into Shuichi’s body—the pain of the clone being stabbed, the shock of the moment before dissipation, and the remaining Chakra the clone carried, all flowed back into the main body.

Shuichi felt the amount of returned Chakra. Most of the clone’s Chakra had flowed back ; before consumption, the Shadow Clone’s Chakra would return the same way.

Shuichi looked at the empty spot and thought for a moment.

The Shadow Clone had a physical form and could withstand light touches and scratches, but it would collapse if it received damage beyond a certain level. Shuichi’s Chakra reserve was only barely at the Chunin level, so the Chakra he could distribute to the Shadow Clone wouldn’t be much, and the clone would be relatively fragile. It might be fine if grazed by a shuriken, but it would dissipate if stabbed by a Kunai.

More critically, there was the Chakra consumption. The Shadow Clone would continuously consume Chakra while it existed—the clone moving, speaking, thinking, every action consumed the Chakra the clone carried. Once the Chakra was exhausted, the clone would automatically disappear, and the remaining Chakra and memories would flow back to the main body. Shuichi’s Chakra reserve was already low ; after splitting half to the Shadow Clone, if the Shadow Clone were to maintain a normal life for a full day—going to school, walking, eating, chatting with classmates—the consumed Chakra would probably only last four to five days. Beyond that time, the clone would disappear due to Chakra exhaustion.

Four to five days. Not enough. Shuichi needed the Shadow Clone to last at least one to two months, giving him enough time to operate outside.

A normal Shadow Clone was not enough.

Shuichi squatted down, looking at the faint trace of Chakra left on the ground after the clone dissipated.

The reason the Shadow Clone was fragile and consumed Chakra quickly was because it was « false. » It wasn’t a true physical entity, but a projection constructed of Chakra. Chakra needed to be continuously consumed to maintain this « false » form ; once it took damage or the Chakra was exhausted, the « falsehood » could not be maintained, and the clone disappeared.

If he could strip away this « false » characteristic, wouldn’t the Shadow Clone become a true physical entity ? A true physical entity wouldn’t need to consume Chakra to maintain its form, nor would it collapse due to injury.

With this thought, Shuichi performed the hand seals again and created another Shadow Clone.

The clone appeared before him. Shuichi reached out his right hand and pressed it onto the clone’s chest.

He expanded his perception. The internal structure of the Shadow Clone unfolded layer by layer in his mind—the Chakra pathways, the nodes maintaining the form, and the mental energy channel connected to the main body. The « falsehood » of the Shadow Clone was not in any single neural pathway or any piece of Chakra, but in the entire way the clone existed. It was like a thin veil covering every corner of the clone, making it look like a real person, but in reality, it was just a projection of Chakra.

Shuichi tried to locate the core of the « falsehood. »

It wasn’t in the Chakra pathways, not in the form-maintenance nodes, and not in the mental energy channel. The « falsehood » was not a local feature, but a holistic attribute—

Wait.

Shuichi’s perception caught an extremely tiny point at the core of the clone.

That point was very small, so small that Shuichi had almost ignored it. It was hidden in the deepest part of the clone’s Chakra structure, like a grain of sand hidden in a beach. When Shuichi’s perception swept over it, he felt a strange fluctuation ; this was not a Chakra fluctuation, but something on a conceptual level.

The core of « falsehood » ?

Shuichi tried to capture that point with his mental energy.

He could feel it. He could clearly feel it right there, emitting fluctuations on a conceptual level. But he couldn’t touch it. It was like standing on Earth and seeing the stars. He could see that star, and even feel its light, but he didn’t know how far away it was, nor how long he would need to fly to reach it. There was an immeasurable distance between his mental energy and that point—so close, yet so far.

Shuichi increased his output of mental energy.

The point trembled under the pressure of his mental energy, and then—

Poof !

The Shadow Clone dissipated.

Shuichi squatted on the spot, looking at his right hand.

He had failed. But not completely.

He had captured that point at the core of the Shadow Clone. That point did exist, and it was indeed the core of « falsehood. » It was just that his mental energy couldn’t reach it ; he couldn’t touch it. Like seeing stars but being unable to touch them.

What if he could touch that point ?

If he could use enough massive mental energy to strip the core of « falsehood » from the Shadow Clone, the Shadow Clone would lose its « false » attribute. A Shadow Clone without the « false » attribute would be a real, physical entity. It wouldn’t need to consume Chakra to maintain its form, and it wouldn’t collapse due to injury.

Theoretically feasible. But the current Shuichi couldn’t do it. The distance between his mental energy and that point wasn’t something that could be bridged by just one or two levels of difference. Perhaps it was a huge gap. Perhaps it was far beyond his imagination.

But the direction was right.

Shuichi stood up and patted the mud off his knees.

The path of Plundering « falsehood » was currently a dead end. But there was another path. If he couldn’t take away « falsehood » by Plundering, could he do the opposite and add « reality » by bestowing it ?

Plunder everything could take everything, and it could also bestow everything. Bestowal was the reverse operation of Plunder. Shuichi had previously stripped away the Flight Instinct, Dark Vision, and vocal ability to bestow them onto Lue—that was bestowing. If he could Plunder the concept of « reality » from some creature and then bestow it onto the Shadow Clone, couldn’t he turn the Shadow Clone into a true physical entity ?

« Reality » and « falsehood » were two sides of the same coin. If he couldn’t take away « falsehood, » then he would add « reality. »

From what creature should he Plunder the concept of « reality » ?

Shuichi thought for a moment and realized that « reality » was not a concept unique to any one creature. Just as vocal ability was unique to parrots, « reality »—existence itself—was real. Every living ant, every tree, every fish, their existence was « real. » « Reality » existed in all living things.

This meant Shuichi didn’t need to find some special creature. Every living creature carried the concept of « reality. » It was just that the concentration of « reality » in each creature might differ ; an ant’s « reality » was very weak, a bear’s « reality » was more intense, and a human’s « reality » might be the most complete.

Shuichi could try to Plunder the « reality » concepts from different creatures, accumulate enough quantity, and then bestow it onto the Shadow Clone.

Shuichi stood in the backyard, looking at Lue on the plum tree branch.

He had a direction.
