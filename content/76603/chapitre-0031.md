# Chapitre 31 — Contemplation

On Thursday morning, when Shuichi left, Lue was still sleeping on the plum tree.

It had practiced diving all afternoon yesterday and spent the night repeatedly trying to vocalize in the courtyard, so it was more exhausted than usual. Shuichi didn’t wake it, walking alone along the Naka River toward the Ninja Academy. The morning mist had not yet dispersed ; the water vapor from the river carried a chilly aura that felt cold against his face.

The matter regarding White Fang was still brewing.

As Shuichi passed the shopping street, he heard people discussing something at the entrance of a Ninja tool shop. Two young men who looked like Genin were standing at the shop entrance, holding newly purchased Kunai, their tones filled with disdain.

« I heard that kid from the Hatake family had stones thrown at him at school yesterday. »

« Serves him right. How many people did his old man get killed ? »

« Exactly. Saving a teammate ? If that mission had succeeded, it could have saved the entire Village. Giving up the whole Village for one person, what kind of Ninja is that ? »

Shuichi didn’t stop, walking past them. The two Genin didn’t notice him and continued discussing Sakumo Hatake’s « disgrace ».

---

Hyuga Shimizu stood at the gates of the Ninja Academy, the Caged Bird Seal headband on his forehead tied very tightly.

He had arrived ten minutes earlier than usual today. There weren’t many students at the school gate yet, only a few older students practicing shuriken throwing. Hyuga Shimizu didn’t enter ; he stood under the locust tree outside the gate, his gaze sweeping over everyone walking into the school.

He was checking to see if there was anyone from the Hyuga Main Family.

Last week, he had been called aside by two older Hyuga Branch Family children, who told him, « Don’t perform too well in public recently. » The Caged Bird Seal locked the upper limits of the Branch Family ; if a Branch Family child performed better than their Main Family peers in public, the Main Family would take notice. Being noticed was not a good thing.

Hyuga Shimizu understood this principle. He had been hiding his strength since he enrolled ; he deliberately slowed down in taijutsu class and intentionally showed only the lowest level in Chakra refinement class. But during last week’s field practice, his concealment had cracked under extreme conditions. The diamond-cross tarp binding method was the optimal solution he used subconsciously ; in that moment, he had forgotten that he was supposed to « perform normally ».

Group Five was full of weak teams. Teacher Mitomon didn’t know he was hiding his strength. Hyuga Shimizu stared at the list for three extra seconds, assessing whether this grouping would cause him to be exposed further.

Now he was waiting at the school gate to see if anyone from the Main Family would come to remind him to « mind his place ».

There were more and more people at the school gate. Hyuga Shimizu saw a familiar figure walk in : Uchiha Xiu Yi, Third Year Class A, whose taijutsu grades were consistently in the top five of the class, but who would « just happen » to lose to those stronger than him during every sparring practice.

Hyuga Shimizu knew that Shuichi was also hiding his strength.

The two had never confirmed this directly. But Hyuga Shimizu had observed Shuichi’s movements in taijutsu class ; the timing of those « mistakes » was too precise. Every time they sparred, Shuichi would « just happen » to lose his balance or « just happen » to be a half-beat slow at the critical moment. This kind of precise mistake was not a real mistake.

Hyuga Shimizu didn’t greet Shuichi. He turned and walked into the school gate, his footsteps as light as usual.

---

During lunch break, Shuichi went to the Training Ground.

He was used to being alone during lunch break. Tetsuya knew not to disturb him, and Obito usually ate his bento with classmates. The shade of the oak tree in the corner of the Training Ground was his fixed spot, from where he could see the movements across the entire Training Ground.

Today, Obito came.

When he ran into the Training Ground, his left foot tripped over the threshold, and he stumbled forward two steps before steadying himself. Seven-year-old Obito was a little taller than last year, but he was still thin, and his hair was messier than the last time they met, with a small tuft sticking up at the left end, as if he had trimmed it himself with scissors. His forehead protector was still that wooden one, engraved with the words « Fourth Hokage », the edges already worn until they were turning white.

« Shuichi-niisan. » Obito walked to the shade of the oak tree and sat down next to Shuichi. His voice was lower than usual, lacking that boisterous excitement.

« Why aren’t you eating your bento ? »

« I already ate. » Obito took a paper parcel out of his pocket and opened it, revealing half a red bean rice ball. He looked down at the rice ball without eating it immediately, his brows furrowed, lips pressed into a thin line.

Shuichi glanced at him. Obito was spacing out, his eyes fixed on an ant on the ground, a heavy weight in his gaze.

« You’re thinking about the White Fang matter, » Shuichi said.

Obito looked up, real confusion in his eyes. His pupils contracted slightly, the corners of his mouth turned down, and the expression on his face looked like he wanted to say something but didn’t know how to start.

« I’ve been thinking about it for two days, » Obito said, his voice muffled, « and I still can’t figure it out. »

« What can’t you figure out ? »

Obito took a deep breath, placed the rice ball on his knees, and clenched his hands into fists on his legs. His knuckles turned slightly white from gripping so hard.

« The school says the mission comes first, » Obito said, his tone carrying a suppressed dissatisfaction, « but you said rules are dead, people are living. I was just thinking, if it were me, what would I choose ? »

Shuichi didn’t speak. Obito continued, speaking slower than usual, every word sounding as if he had thought about it for a long time before saying it.

« I imagined it. If I were on a mission and Rin was captured, should I save her or complete the mission ? » Obito’s voice was very low, a tremor in his throat, « If I save her, the mission fails, and the villagers scold me. If I don’t save her, the mission succeeds, but Rin dies. »

« And then ? »

Obito looked up, and there was something in his eyes that Shuichi had never seen before. It wasn’t confusion ; it was judgment. There was a stubborn light in his pupils, and the corners of his mouth turned up slightly, as if he had made a decision.

« I choose to save Rin, » Obito said, his tone very firm, biting every word clearly, « I don’t care how the villagers scold me. Rin is my friend, I can’t watch her die. »

Shuichi looked at Obito. The seven-year-old child’s expression had changed from confusion to determination. His eyes no longer wandered, his gaze looking straight at Shuichi, as if confirming his own answer.

« You’ve figured it out. »

« Yeah. » Obito nodded, lifting his chin slightly, « But I have one more question. »

« What is it ? »

« Why didn’t the people who were saved speak up for White Fang ? » Obito’s brows furrowed again, confusion reappearing in his eyes, « White Fang saved them ; they should have stood up to defend White Fang. Why did they remain silent ? »

Shuichi was silent for a moment.

This was one of the core issues in the White Fang incident. The rescued teammates had truthfully written down what happened in their reports, but they didn’t stand up to defend White Fang. They remained silent. This silence was more hurtful than the villagers' scolding.

« Because they were afraid, » Shuichi said.

« Afraid of what ? » Obito’s eyes widened slightly, his eyebrows raised.

« Afraid of being scolded along with him, » Shuichi watched the older students practicing shuriken on the Training Ground, « When White Fang was being scolded by the whole Village, standing up to speak for him was the same as standing against the entire Village. Not everyone has that kind of courage. »

Obito was silent for a long time. He looked down at the rice ball on his knees, his lips pressed together, his brows furrowed tightly. After a while, he looked up, the confusion in his eyes gone, replaced by a kind of stubbornness.

« That’s not right, » Obito said, his tone very hard, « White Fang saved them, they should have stood up. That is the right thing to do. »

« What should be done is one thing ; whether or not one does it is another. »

« If it were me, I would stand up, » Obito said, lifting his chin, his eyes looking straight at Shuichi. There was a stubborn pride in his tone, as if he had made a resolution that would never change.

Shuichi looked into Obito’s eyes. The seven-year-old child had a stubborn light on his face. Shuichi had seen this stubbornness when Obito was besieged by four children. Obito was knocked down, got back up, was knocked down again, and got back up again. He would not admit defeat.

« Remember what you said today, » Shuichi said.

Obito nodded, the corners of his mouth turning up slightly, as if confirming his own determination.

---

After school, Shuichi walked home along the Naka River. Lue circled above the riverbank, and after seeing Shuichi, flew down and landed on his shoulder.

« Coo. » Lue let out a low chirp.

Clearer than yesterday’s « coo », but still incoherent. Shuichi reached out to let Lue hop onto his palm, looking into its eyes.

Lue’s pupils switched between low-light mode and normal mode, tilting its head to look at Shuichi. It opened its mouth, and a blurred sound came from its throat, as if it wanted to say something but its vocal organs didn’t support it.

An eagle’s throat and beak were not designed for speaking. The Monkey King’s wisdom concept gave Lue language ability, but the limitations of its vocal organs made it unable to truly vocalize. Lue could understand language and try to express itself with sound, but its body didn’t support the pronunciation of human syllables.

Shuichi needed to give Lue a vocal organ ability.

Parrot.

Parrots could mimic human speech ; their vocal organs supported complex syllables. If he plundered the vocal ability from a parrot and stripped it to Lue, Lue’s throat would be able to support real language.

Shuichi walked upstream along the Naka River, Lue perched on his shoulder. He needed to find a parrot. He wasn’t sure if there were parrots around Konoha. Parrots were not common local birds ; they lived more in warm southern places. But Konoha was a Ninja Village, and exotic species might have been brought in by some Ninjas.

Shuichi remembered there were a few pet shops on the shopping street. Some Ninjas kept birds as communication tools ; parrots might be there.

He changed direction and walked toward the shopping street.

The shopping street was in the center of the Village, about a quarter of an hour’s walk from the Naka River. Shuichi followed the path along the river, crossed the Training Ground, passed over the stone bridge, and walked into the streets inside the Village. In the evening, there weren’t many people on the shopping street ; most shops were still open, but there were few customers.

Shuichi stopped in front of the third pet shop.

A wooden sign hung at the shop entrance, on which was written « Bird Specialty Shop ». The shop door was open, and inside, several cages could be seen, with birds of various colors kept inside. Shuichi walked into the shop, his gaze sweeping over the contents of the cages.

There were three parrots.

One green one, medium-sized, with bright feathers. One blue one, slightly smaller, with a ring of white feathers around its eyes. One red one, the largest, with a wingspan of about half a meter when spread.

Shuichi walked to the red parrot’s cage. This parrot was the largest, so its vocal organs should also be the strongest.

« Coo-coo. » The red parrot looked at him and let out an imitative sound.

« How much for this one ? » Shuichi asked the shopkeeper.

The shopkeeper was a man over fifty, with graying hair and deep wrinkles on his face. He glanced at Shuichi, then glanced at Lue on Shuichi’s shoulder.

« Are you a child of the Uchiha family ? »

« Yeah. »

« What are you buying a parrot for ? »

« Training, » Shuichi said, « The hawk I’m raising needs to learn how to vocalize. »

The shopkeeper looked at him, his gaze carrying a sense of scrutiny. But Uchiha children buying birds for training wasn’t an uncommon thing in Konoha. The Uchiha Clan was famous for Fire Release, but there were also many people in the clan who kept birds as reconnaissance tools.

« Three thousand ryo, » the shopkeeper said, « This red parrot is a southern breed ; it’s very smart. »

Shuichi took his money pouch out of his Ninja tool bag. He didn’t usually save much money ; most of it came from the allowance Minako gave him, plus the occasional reward for doing odd jobs within the clan. Three thousand ryo was a bit expensive for him, but not unaffordable.

He handed the money to the shopkeeper. The shopkeeper took the money, opened the cage, took out the red parrot, and tied its feet with a string.

« Be careful, this parrot has a bit of a bad temper, » the shopkeeper said.

Shuichi took the parrot and placed it on his left arm. The parrot’s claws gripped his clothes, it flapped its wings twice, then quieted down. It tilted its head to look at Lue and let out a « coo-coo ».

Lue also tilted its head to look at the parrot, its pupils switching between low-light mode and normal mode.

Shuichi walked out of the pet shop and walked back along the shopping street. Lue was perched on his right shoulder, the parrot on his left arm, the two birds looking at each other, occasionally letting out a low chirp.

He needed to find a quiet place to Plunder the parrot’s vocal ability. There were too many people on the shopping street, so it wasn’t suitable. He walked upstream along the Naka River to behind a large rock on the riverbank, confirming that there was no one around.

Shuichi placed the parrot on the rock and pressed his right hand onto its back.

Perception expanded. The vocal organs inside the parrot unfolded layer by layer in his mind : throat muscles, vocal cord structure, trachea shape, tongue flexibility. The parrot’s vocal ability was completely different from an eagle’s ; its throat could support complex syllable changes, its vocal cords could adjust frequency, and its tongue could assist in pronunciation.

Plunder.

A warm suction came from the palm of Shuichi’s right hand. The parrot’s body trembled slightly but didn’t struggle violently. Vocal ability was a physical-type ability, so the consumption from plundering it was not great. About three seconds later, a warm sensation appeared in Shuichi’s palm, and the template of the parrot’s vocal organs lay there completely.

The parrot was still alive, but its vocal ability had been stripped away. It opened its mouth, and a blurred, incoherent sound came from its throat ; it could no longer mimic human syllables.

Shuichi didn’t kill it. After the vocal ability was stripped, the parrot could still live ; it just lost the ability to speak. He put the parrot on the ground, and the parrot turned its head to look at Shuichi in confusion.

Shuichi looked at that gaze, patted its back to remind it that it was free.

Just like that, the parrot flew away ; it had traded its vocal organs for freedom.

Shuichi refocused his attention on Lue, pressing his right hand onto Lue’s chest.

Fusion.

Lue’s body shook violently. Its pupils switched frantically between low-light mode and normal mode, its wings spread out suddenly, and its claws scratched a few shallow marks into the rock. It tilted its head back and let out a cry, the sound longer than usual, the trailing note having a new frequency.

Then it quieted down.

Lue perched on the rock, tilting its head to look at Shuichi. Its pupils stabilized, and a low sound came from its throat.

« Shu...ichi. »

Incoherent, the pronunciation blurred, but it was much clearer than before. Lue’s throat could now support the pronunciation of human syllables ; the Monkey King’s wisdom concept gave it language ability, and the parrot’s vocal organs gave it the tool for pronunciation.

With the two abilities combined, Lue could speak.

« Practice slowly, remember not to speak in normal times, » Shuichi said.

Lue nodded, then opened its mouth.

« Yeah. »

One word, a clear syllable.
