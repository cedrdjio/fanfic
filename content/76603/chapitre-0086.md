# Chapitre 86 — The Reaction of Iwagakure

At the Iwagakure front lines, a patrol team was conducting routine inspections of the outer camps.

The patrol captain, Iwasaki, was a seasoned Chunin. He had served in Iwagakure for twelve years, working his way up from a Genin, and was familiar with every camp along this front line. He knew which camp’s sentries liked to doze off, which camp’s cooks made the best food, and which camp’s Jonin had the shortest temper.

Today, they were inspecting Camp Seven.

Iwasaki led his five men along the ridge, crossed two hills, and arrived at the valley where Camp Seven was located. According to custom, the camp should have been cooking at this time ; smoke from the bonfires would be drifting from the tops of the tents, the cooks would be busy in the yard, and sentries would be patrolling the perimeter.

But today, there was no smoke.

Iwasaki stopped, crouched behind a rock, and looked at the camp in the valley.

The tents were still standing, arranged neatly. No one was moving in the yard. The bonfire pits were cold ; there was no firelight, no smoke.

« Something is wrong, » Iwasaki said, his voice lowered.

The five team members behind him also crouched down, watching the camp. No one spoke. They all felt that something was wrong—a quiet, deathly wrongness.

Iwasaki observed for ten minutes, confirming there was no movement around the camp’s perimeter. Then he stood up and said to two of his team members, « You two come with me. The other three wait outside ; if anything happens, retreat and report immediately. »

The two members nodded and followed Iwasaki toward the camp.

They climbed over the rock wall on the north side of the camp, without alerting anything.

The sight in the camp made Iwasaki stop in his tracks.

The yard was littered with corpses.

Some had fallen by the bonfires, some in the walkways, some by the doorways, and some in front of the tents. Blood had flowed from every corpse, dried, and turned into dark red stains, gathering into thin streams on the ground.

Iwasaki counted ; there were over twenty bodies in the yard alone.

He walked over to the nearest corpse and crouched down to examine it. The throat had been slit, the wound clean and consistent. Blood had flowed from the wound and dried. This corpse hadn’t even had time to draw its weapon.

Iwasaki stood up and walked to the next corpse. The same situation. Throat slit, no resistance.

He checked every corpse in the yard one by one, then lifted the tent flap. There were corpses in the tents too, though not many, ranging from three to five. Everyone had died from a slit throat, and no one had drawn their weapon.

Thirty in total.

Iwasaki walked to the center of the camp, looking at the bloodstains on the ground. The blood had dried, turning into dark red marks that extended from each corpse, gathering into thin streams on the ground.

Iwasaki crouched down and looked at the longest trail of blood.

Thirty people, one killer.

All wounds were caused by the same short blade. The angle, depth, and position of the wounds were all identical. The killer had climbed in from the north side of the camp, started clearing from the perimeter, and worked their way in, taking a life with every swing of the blade.

Thirty people, all dead in the same camp.

Iwasaki stood up and looked around. The tents were still standing, the bonfires extinguished. The camp seemed frozen in time, thirty people having died in the exact same instant.

He said two words to the two team members behind him : « Go back. »

The report reached the Iwagakure command center that very night.

The command center was built inside a cave, its entrance concealed by a barrier, with a spacious and bright interior. Several Iwagakure Jonin sat around a stone table, looking at the report submitted by the patrol captain.

The report was very brief. Camp Seven was discovered, all thirty people dead, throats slit, single perpetrator, no signs of resistance.

The atmosphere at the stone table was very quiet. No one spoke.

The first factor to be ruled out was numbers.

A thirty-person camp, three Jonin, twenty-seven Chunin and Genin.

This configuration was considered mid-level among Iwagakure’s outer camps ; while not the strongest, it was definitely not a pushover.

The killer was only one person.

All wounds were caused by the same short blade. There were definitely no more than twenty people in the entire Ninja World who could slaughter a camp of this configuration single-handedly in such a short time.

But they were at war now. The top combat power of every Village was pinned down on the front lines, watching each other ; everyone knew where each Ninja of this level was.

Konoha’s Minato was holding back Kumogakure’s AB combo, Orochimaru was the front-line commander, and Jiraiya was active in the Land of Rain.

On the Iwagakure side, all Jonin were watching Konoha’s movements on the main front line. The top combat power of Kumogakure, Sunagakure, and Hidden Mist were the same, all at the core positions of their respective front lines.

No one could disappear from under so many eyes to slaughter an outer camp.

A Jonin at the stone table spoke. « This style of close-quarters swordplay is a bit like Konoha’s White Fang. »

Another Jonin shook his head. « White Fang is already dead. He committed suicide last year. »

« And even if White Fang were alive, » the first Jonin said, « he could manage a one-on-one assassination. But for thirty people to all die silently in the same camp, it’s impossible. White Fang’s swordplay is strong, but he couldn’t achieve this level of silent slaughter. »

There was silence around the stone table for a while.

Then someone pulled out a briefing.

Two months ago, Kusagakure.

The entire Ninja Village was slaughtered. Four Jonin, one leader, dozens of Chunin and Genin, all dead. The same modus operandi : throat slit, no traces of Ninjutsu, no Chakra residue, no resistance.

That time it was a Village eradication. This time it was a camp.

If these two incidents were the same killer, then this person could have wiped out a Ninja Village single-handedly two months ago. Coming to the battlefield to hunt camps now was more than enough.

The Jonin at the stone table looked at the briefing, no one speaking.

The news of Kusagakure being wiped out had spread through the Ninja World, but no one knew who the killer was.

Konoha sent Jiraiya to investigate, but Jiraiya only brought back a vague report—a person claiming to be a survivor of the Sengoku period, who knew Uchiha Madara and possessed immense strength.

The identity of that person was unknown, their appearance unknown, their origin unknown.

It was only known that they used swordplay, rarely used Ninjutsu, and possessed vast Chakra but left no identifiable traces.

Now, that killer had appeared on the Iwagakure front lines.

The Jonin at the stone table began to discuss countermeasures.

The first plan was to track down the killer.

But tracking required intelligence, and they currently had none.

The killer left no traces, no witnesses. The only clue was that briefing from Kusagakure, but that briefing didn’t provide any useful information either.

The second plan was to strengthen defenses. But strengthening defenses meant withdrawing the outer camps to within the main front line, concentrating forces, and leaving the killer with no targets.

The third plan was to notify other Villages. But notifying other Villages meant exposing Iwagakure’s weakness, letting the enemy know that someone was hunting Iwagakure camps on the battlefield, and letting the enemy know that Iwagakure could not stop this slaughter.

Finally, the Iwagakure command center made a decision.

Do not disclose this finding to the public. At the same time, order all outer camps to increase their alert level, and if any abnormalities are discovered, immediately send someone to leave and report.

The order was issued that very night. Camp Six, Camp Eight, and Camp Nine near Camp Seven all received the alert directive. Outer camps were to increase the number of sentries, shorten patrol intervals, and any disturbance had to be reported immediately.

But no one really felt that the alert would be useful.

If someone who could single-handedly slaughter a Village was targeting Iwagakure camps, a few more sentries would just mean a few more corpses. Discover abnormalities and send someone to report immediately ? But the thirty people in Camp Seven hadn’t even managed to sound a single alarm.

Iwasaki stood outside the command center, looking in the direction of the valley.

Thirty people, one killer. The wounds were clean and consistent. No one resisted.

He knew what that kind of swordplay was like.

He had seen White Fang’s swordplay and had seen the battles of other top swordplay experts.

But the swordplay in Camp Seven was different. Faster, more precise, more silent. Every swing of the blade took a life, without any wasted movement.

That kind of swordplay wasn’t fighting.

It was harvesting.
