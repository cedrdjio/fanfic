# Chapitre 17 — Precise Fusion

That evening, Shuichi lit a small oil lamp in the courtyard.

Minako was already asleep.

The plum tree in the backyard swayed gently in the night breeze. Lue crouched on a plum branch, its head tucked under its wing, though its pupils glimmered with a dim light in the darkness.

Shuichi placed the oil lamp on the stone slab beneath the plum tree, sat cross-legged beside it, and rested his right hand on his knee, palm facing upward.

The flame of the oil lamp was only the size of a thumb, flickering slightly in the night breeze.

In less than half a stick of incense’s time, the first moth arrived.

A small, grayish-brown moth darted from the darkness outside the courtyard wall into the circle of light, tracing a crooked flight path around the flame.

Then came the second, the third.

They circled and collided around the flame, their wings shimmering with the fine, powdery luster of scales within the halo of light.

Light-seeking Instinct.

Moths flying into a flame—this is one of the simplest and purest concept-level abilities in the natural world.

It does not rely on any specific organ.

The compound eye structure of a moth is a somatic visual carrier, but phototaxis itself is not eyesight.

It is a set of fixed behavioral patterns engraved deep within the moth’s nervous system ; when there is a specific deviation in the direction of light in the surrounding environment, the body automatically adjusts its flight angle to face the light source.

It requires no thought, no learning, and no special modification of any organs.

It is a pure, underlying instruction.

It belongs to the same concept class as [Growth], but the complexity difference is more than an order of magnitude.

Growth is a developmental blueprint that permeates every cell, while phototaxis is merely a simple behavioral rule.

If he wanted to practice precisely fusing concept abilities to specific body parts, there was no lower-risk material than this.

Shuichi extended his right hand and gently pinched a moth that was circling the flame.

The moth fluttered against his fingertips, and the scale powder from its wings rubbed onto his finger pads.

Perception expanded.

Inside the moth was a minimalist structure—nervous system, flight muscles, digestive tract, reproductive glands.

And in the deepest part of the nervous system, there was a thin layer of instruction with a faint yellow halo.

It was more than a hundred times weaker than the Growth concept of the tadpole, and much simpler than the Flight Instinct.

But it was indeed a concept—not an organ, not a cell, not a tissue, but a set of behavioral rules.

Shuichi focused his consciousness, and his suction precisely touched that layer of pale yellow halo.

Plunder.

The amount of mental energy drained was extremely light, roughly equivalent to the consumption of plundering Vitality from a grasshopper.

The moth suddenly stopped fluttering at his fingertips ; its wings remained intact, but it no longer flew.

Shuichi released his fingers, and the moth fell onto the stone slab. Its six legs were still moving, and it was crawling, but it had lost the instinct to fly toward the light source.

It crawled away in a completely random direction, disappearing into the shadows of the stone slab.

In the palm of Shuichi’s right hand lay a ball of extremely faint warmth.

Light-seeking Instinct.

A concept-level ability, its magnitude so small it was almost negligible, but the type was correct—it was a concept.

Now, what he had to do was to precisely fuse it into his own eyes.

Shuichi closed his eyes and pressed his right palm against his right eyelid.

Previously, when stripping the dim-light vision from Lue, he had performed a [Push], pushing the ability from within himself into Lue.

Now he had to perform the opposite operation, but the direction was different—not a push, but a lock-in.

He had to push the Light-seeking Instinct in his palm into his eye, and then, at the moment of entry, lock it firmly within the ocular tissue, preventing it from spreading along the optic nerve to the brain, and preventing it from circulating through the blood to the rest of his body.

Chakra surged from his Dantian, gathering at the junction of the retina and the optic nerve, forming a very thin ring of energy.

This was the step he had rehearsed in his mind several times that afternoon : using Chakra to establish a temporary energy barrier in the eye to lock the ability within the range of the eye.

The Light-seeking Instinct was pushed in from his right palm.

Shuichi felt an imperceptible warmth pass through his palm, through his eyelid, and into his eyeball.

The warmth began to spread the moment it touched the retina ; it wanted to flow along the optic nerve toward the brain.

Shuichi’s Chakra barrier tightened at the same instant.

The warmth collided with the barrier and was blocked.

It retreated, spinning inside the eyeball like a fish trapped in a small pond.

Shuichi did not forcibly compress it but instead slowly guided it to attach to the surface of the retina, specifically the thin membrane between the photoreceptor cell layer and the pigment epithelium layer.

The warmth stabilized.

Shuichi opened his eyes.

A subtle difference appeared between the vision of his right eye and his left eye ; it wasn’t that his vision had become clearer, nor that the colors had become more vivid, but rather that there was an indescribable, subtle shift in the perception of the direction of light.

He turned his head to look at the oil lamp ; his right eye could capture the most minute changes in the halo at the edge of the flame more keenly than his left eye—this was an instinct-level reaction to the direction of light.

Success.

Precise fusion of a concept ability to a specific body part was technically feasible.

Shuichi let out a soft breath.

The Light-seeking Instinct itself had no real combat value because it only made the eyes slightly more sensitive to the direction of light.

But the significance did not lie in the effect, but in validating a path.

A path to locking a concept ability firmly onto the eyes.

If the Light-seeking Instinct could be precisely fused, then the Growth concept could theoretically be locked onto the eye in the same way.

The problem was that the intensity of the Growth concept was more than ten times higher than the Light-seeking Instinct, so the Chakra barrier it required would also need to be thickened accordingly.

Additionally, he still lacked a gentle material that could truly verify the extraction of the Growth concept.

The tadpole’s metamorphic development was too drastic ; he needed a creature with a gentler Growth.

He blew out the oil lamp.

The moths circled in the darkness for a moment before scattering into the night.

The next morning, Shuichi got up very early.

He took a small piece of dry bread from the kitchen, crumbled it, and placed it into the wooden compartment in the corner of the courtyard, where three field mice were rustling and snatching the food.

Two grayish-brown adult mice, and one slightly smaller sub-adult.

No pregnant female mouse, no newborn pups.

Wild field mouse nests could be found in the Mixed Forest.

Last autumn, he had discovered a burrow system beneath a pile of rotting leaves in the northwest corner of the forest.

« We’ll see after school today, » Shuichi muttered to himself.

After school, Shuichi did not go home.

When he walked along the path by the Naka River to the entrance of the Mixed Forest, Lue was already circling above the old beech tree.

This Mixed Forest was his first hunting ground after awakening his ability ; it was small in scale, with a limited variety of creatures, but the field mouse burrow system was still there.

Lue flew down from the beech tree, made a short circle above the pile of rotting leaves in the northwest corner, and landed on a low branch.

Shuichi walked to the pile of rotting leaves and crouched down, using his right hand to push aside the top layer of beech leaves that had fermented into a dark brown color.

Underneath was a layer of drier dead leaves, followed by soft, black humus.

On the surface of the soil layer was a hole the size of a thumb, and the soil at the edge of the hole was fresh.

Shuichi pressed his right hand onto the ground next to the hole.

He had flipped through the « Konoha Surrounding Creature Guide » at school regarding the structure of field mouse burrows : about a foot down from the entrance was the main passage, which branched into a storage room, an excretion area, and a nesting chamber.

The nesting chamber was at the deepest point, lined with chewed-up dry grass and mouse fur.

During the spring breeding season, female mice gave birth in the nesting chamber.

The newborn pups were hairless, their eyes tightly closed, surviving on the mother mouse’s milk.

He used his hands to dig away the soil layer around the hole.

Having devoured Vitality for over half a year, his hands felt almost no resistance against this soft humus.

Digging about a foot deep along the main passage, the soil color changed from black-brown to dark gray, and further down it turned sandy yellow—this was the gravel layer of the subsoil.

The passage turned to the right here, with fragments of dry grass mixed into the sandy soil.

The nesting chamber.

Shuichi softened his movements.

He pushed aside the last layer of sandy soil, revealing the cross-section of the nesting chamber—a cavity slightly larger than a fist, with inner walls smoothed and packed solid by the mice’s claws, and the bottom covered with a thick layer of chewed-up dry grass.

A ball of grayish-red little things curled on the dry grass.

Six field mouse pups.

They were hairless, their skin a translucent pink, and one could faintly see the network of blood vessels beneath the skin.

Their eyes were tightly closed, their ears were just two tiny buds, and their limbs were as short as grains of rice.

They huddled together, rising and falling slightly with their breathing, completely unaware that the ceiling of their nesting chamber had been opened.

Shuichi looked at them, his right hand hovering above the nesting chamber without descending.

He chose the smallest one.

It was curled in the leftmost corner, its body size a circle smaller than the other five, its breathing shallower and lighter.

Perhaps it was the one that was always squeezed to the back when competing for milk.

Its Growth was a bit slower than the other pups—but precisely because it was slow, the Growth concept within it should also be relatively weaker.

Shuichi gently picked up the smallest pup from the nesting chamber and held it in the palm of his right hand.

The pup’s body was only the size of his thumb, squirming uneasily in his palm, its mouth opening but unable to make a sound.

Perception expanded.

Inside the pup was a micro-world that was completely different from the tadpole but equally growing.

The bones were slowly transforming from cartilage to hard bone, muscle fibers were increasing one by one, and the nervous system was densely weaving new synaptic connections.

But there was no drastic structural reorganization like metamorphic development, because there were no gills turning into lungs, no legless forms turning into four-legged forms.

The Growth here was continuous and gentle, like a gentle slope rather than a cliff.

And right at the bottom of every cell, that instruction was clearly visible.

It was at least two orders of magnitude weaker than the tadpole’s instruction, no longer a string stretched to the limit, but a slowly flowing stream.

The underlying law of Growth, engraved in every dividing cell nucleus, drove bone elongation, muscle thickening, and neural extension.

Shuichi focused his consciousness.

Using the lesson learned from the disastrous failure with the tadpole, combined with the precise extraction method validated in the Light-seeking Instinct experiment last night, he only touched the outermost part of the instruction layer, not going deep into the Root.

Suction contact.

About a quarter of his mental energy was drained at once.

It was much better than the instantaneous loss of control during the tadpole incident.

No tinnitus, no narrowing of vision, no numbness in his right arm.

The suction operated stably, like a pipe with controlled flow.

The pup squirmed in his palm, then quieted down.

Its body did not wither because what Shuichi had plundered was not Vitality, but the Growth concept.

About four seconds later, the suction automatically disconnected.

Shuichi looked down at the pup in his palm.

It was still breathing, its mouth still opening and closing silently.

But the Growth rate of its bones and muscles had slowed down, dropping from a normal two-week developmental rhythm to a slower level.

A portion of the Growth concept on it had been drained, about one-third.

This was enough for Shuichi to use, without causing the pup to completely lose its ability to grow.

He put the pup back into the nesting chamber, huddled together with the other five.

Then he used his hands to fill back the dug-up soil layer by layer, spreading the rotting leaves back over the surface of the hole.

In the palm of his right hand lay a ball of gentle, continuous warmth.

Growth concept.

It was not the intensity that burned with pain like the tadpole’s, nor the weakness that was almost imperceptible like the Light-seeking Instinct.

It was a stable, substantial warmth, like a pebble warmed by body temperature.

Shuichi sat down against the pile of rotting leaves, closed his eyes, and began to construct the Chakra barrier.

The fusion of the Light-seeking Instinct only took ten seconds, and the density of the Chakra barrier was only enough to block an extremely weak concept ability.

But the intensity of the Growth concept was more than ten times higher than the Light-seeking Instinct ; although both were concept-level, their magnitudes were completely different.

Shuichi mobilized nearly twice as much Chakra from his Dantian as he had last night, constructing a double-layer barrier at the junction of his right retina and optic nerve.

One layer pressed against the retina, one layer surrounding the entrance of the optic nerve, with an extremely narrow gap left between the two layers—enough to allow the Growth concept to attach to the retina, but preventing it from crossing the boundary.

His right hand pressed against his right eyelid.

Fusion.

The Growth concept was pushed from his palm into his right eye.

The moment the warmth passed through his eyelid, Shuichi immediately felt a pressure completely different from the Light-seeking Instinct.

The Growth concept did not flow in docilely ; it began to spread immediately after entering the eye, not along the optic nerve, but by directly permeating through capillaries into the soft tissue around the eye socket.

It wanted to spread to the entire body.

The double-layer Chakra barrier was pushed outward by a circle during the first wave of spreading.

Shuichi gritted his teeth, injecting Chakra into the barrier, forcibly pressing the Growth concept back into the eyeball.

The barrier was trembling, resisting that force that wanted to spread outward.

Shuichi maintained the output of Chakra while guiding the Growth concept to the base of the retina, finding a more suitable entry point next to where the Light-seeking Instinct was attached.

The Growth concept slowly sank into the retina.

The barrier stabilized.

The Growth concept was locked inside the eyeball.

It did not spread to the eye socket, did not penetrate the optic nerve, and did not circulate through the blood to the rest of the body.

Shuichi opened his eyes.

The beech tree, rotting leaves, and light spots in the twilight seen by his right eye were the same as those seen by his left eye.

No change.

The Sharingan did not activate, his vision did not improve, and there was no abnormality in his pupils visible to the naked eye.

The Growth concept had been successfully locked onto his eye, but it was now in a dormant state.

He closed his eyes for a moment, then opened them.

He had a rough guess as to where the problem lay.

He had put [Growth] into his eyes, but the eyes didn’t know what to grow into.

The Light-seeking Instinct was a complete behavioral rule ; it took effect immediately upon being put into the retina.

But Growth was a pure driving force ; it needed a target blueprint.

The blueprint of the One-Tomoe Sharingan.

And his Sharingan had not yet opened ; the blueprint did not exist.

The Growth concept was equivalent to being put into an engine with no destination.

To make it start, he might need to first trigger the Sharingan in some weak way, even if it wasn’t a complete One-Tomoe, even if it was just a flash of ocular power for an instant.

As long as there was that instant of ocular power fluctuation, the Growth concept could be [awakened], and then begin autonomous Growth using that weak ocular power as a template.

Shuichi stood up and patted the residual soil off his hands.

The residual warmth of the Growth concept still lingered in the palm of his right hand.

He updated his progress in his mind.

For precise fusion of a concept to the eye, the required density of the Chakra barrier increases proportionally with the intensity of the concept.

The Growth concept was successfully locked into the eye, but it required an initial trigger of the Sharingan to start autonomous Growth.

The next bottleneck was not Plunder, not fusion, but how to make this pair of Uchiha eyes light up with the first wisp of ocular power.

Lue flew down from the beech tree and landed on his shoulder.

Shuichi stroked the chest feathers of Lue and walked back along the Naka River.

In the twilight, the lights of the Uchiha Clan Grounds lit up one by one in the distance.
