# Chapitre 29 — Wisdom

On Saturday morning, Shuichi entered the Forest of Death. His destination was the cliff on the north slope.

He had discovered monkey footprints here last week, but the trail had vanished after following them for half a mile. A week later, Shuichi searched deeper along the base of the cliff once more, while Lue scanned the rock walls above with Infrared Sensing from high in the sky. On the north side of the cliff was a scree slope covered by vines. When Shuichi had walked here last time, it was already getting dark, so he hadn’t climbed up. Today, he crouched below the scree slope for a while and saw several more toe prints in the crevices at the base of the slope. The edges were already cracked, meaning they had been left at least three days ago.

The monkeys were still there.

Shuichi climbed up the scree slope. Lue circled above the cliff. In the Infrared Sensing, there was a cluster of active heat sources at the top of the cliff—about seven or eight of them. They were small in size and moved quickly ; they were not acting alone. It was a troop of monkeys.

He stopped at the edge of the slope and hid behind the vines to look down. The top of the cliff was a small, flat area where several old oak trees grew out from the crevices in the rock, their crowns covering most of the clearing. The monkey troop was active among the oaks—two cubs were chasing each other on the branches, a female monkey was crouched on a fork in the tree nursing the cub in her arms, and the Monkey King was lying on top of the highest oak tree, scanning the surroundings. It was just as Shuichi had expected : a typical macaque troop, seven or eight of them, with a complete social structure.

His gaze rested on the Monkey King.

It was the largest in size, had the deepest fur color, and a faint, old scar on its forehead, likely left by internal strife. The Monkey King’s cognitive structure was the most complete within the troop : it not only had to forage, lead the troop, identify threats, and react differently, but also mediate conflicts within the group. Social cognition, risk assessment, emotion recognition, and communication skills—all these had corresponding modules in the Monkey King’s forebrain.

Completely stripping away the Monkey King’s forebrain cognitive structure would consume approximately the equivalent of Plundering three Growth concepts. Shuichi could handle it now because the mental slots released after digesting the Growth concepts were sufficient. But whether Lue could handle it was another matter, so Shuichi had mentally simulated it many times : Lue’s physical foundation, after two years of Vitality infusion, was far superior to a normal eagle, and its bone density and neural capacity were sufficient. The digestion cycle would be long—half a year or even longer—but Shuichi was not in a hurry.

What he needed was complete wisdom. Including emotion and language.

Shuichi stood up from behind the vines. The Monkey King locked onto him instantly, its reaction nearly twice as fast as a normal monkey. The Monkey King let out a sharp warning cry, and the entire troop switched from a relaxed state to a defensive formation within seconds. Two young male monkeys darted to the Monkey King’s sides, and the female monkey took the cubs to climb higher up the oak tree. The Monkey King itself jumped down from the treetop, landing on a large rock between Shuichi and the troop, its limbs spread out, emitting a low, threatening sound from its mouth.

It was protecting the troop. The Monkey King had assessed Shuichi’s size and intentions and decided to use deterrence instead of fleeing.

Shuichi did not draw his Kunai. He slowly raised his right hand, palm facing the Monkey King. Lue dove down from above the cliff, its two-meter wingspan casting a shadow over the large rock. The Monkey King looked up reflexively ; vigilance against eagles was an instinct for all monkeys. At that very moment, Shuichi moved.

Chakra infused into the soles of his feet, and the three steps across the scree slope shrank to zero in an instant. Shuichi’s right palm pressed against the back of the Monkey King’s head.

Plunder. Target : complete forebrain cognitive structure.

The amount of mental energy being drained was greater than ever before. It wasn’t like pouring water into a cup ; it was like a dam opening its floodgates. The Monkey King’s forebrain cognitive structure was far more complex than a crow’s ; social cognition, emotion recognition, risk assessment, communication instincts, causality, tool usage—neural signal templates were stripped away piece by piece, each one carrying the Monkey King’s decades of memory and experience.

The Monkey King struggled violently under his palm. Its claws dug into the skin on his wrist, and its tail wrapped around his forearm, squeezing until it hurt. Shuichi gritted his teeth, maintaining the suction, and his Sharingan activated automatically. Two tomoe rotated steadily at the edge of his pupils, clearly breaking down the Monkey King’s neural signals to ensure every template was stripped away completely.

Approximately twelve seconds. The Monkey King stopped struggling.

Shuichi withdrew his right hand, palm facing upward. The concept in his palm was heavier than ever before ; the complete Wisdom concept was not cold, but warm, like a lump of burning coal. Its internal structure was more than an order of magnitude more complex than the Growth concept : causal reasoning, social cognition, emotion recognition, communication instincts, tool usage—every neural pathway lay complete in his palm, waiting to be merged.

The Monkey King lay on its side on the rock, its chest still rising and falling, but the light in its eyes had vanished. It was still alive physiologically, but after the core cognitive structure of its forebrain was stripped away, it was no longer a monkey—just a breathing body.

Shuichi did not waste its Vitality. He pressed his right hand onto the Monkey King’s forehead again, Plundering the remaining Vitality as well. Then, he pressed his right palm against Lue’s chest.

Fusion.

Lue’s body shuddered violently. Its pupils switched frantically between dark-light mode and normal mode—three times, five times, eight times ; the frequency was faster than any previous fusion. Its two-meter wingspan snapped open, and its claws gouged four deep marks into the rock beneath its feet. Lue tilted its head back and let out a cry, a sound Shuichi had never heard before. It was long and trembling, the tail note rising.

Then it fell silent.

Lue crouched on the rock, tilting its head as it looked at Shuichi. Its gaze had changed. Previously, the way Lue looked at Shuichi was « waiting for instructions, » waiting for Shuichi to tell it what to do next. Now, Lue was looking at Shuichi’s face, identifying Shuichi’s expression.

« How do you feel ? » Shuichi asked.

Lue opened its beak. A short, incoherent sound came from deep in its throat. Lue itself seemed startled, tilted its head to think for a moment, and then tried again.

« ...Chirp. »

Shuichi paused for a beat. Lue was trying to speak. It wasn’t true language because its vocal organs were an eagle’s throat and beak ; it was impossible for it to produce human syllables. But Lue was trying to express « I heard you, I am responding to you » through sound. This attempt itself was the germ of language ability.

« No rush. » Shuichi crouched down, pressing his palm onto Lue’s chest feathers, « Digest it slowly. »

Lue tilted its head and watched him for a long time. Then it nodded—not the simple, trained nod from before. Lue was understanding the meaning of the four words « digest it slowly, » and then used the nod to tell Shuichi : I understand.

Shuichi stood up. The Monkey King’s corpse was already cold, its Vitality completely Plundered. Shuichi buried it under the rotting leaves at the base of the oak tree and brushed the dirt off his hands.

On the way back, Lue did not circle above Shuichi. It crouched on Shuichi’s shoulder, its two-meter wingspan folded, weighing down on Shuichi so that he tilted his head slightly. Lue’s pupils switched slowly between dark-light mode and normal mode, and Shuichi could sense a brand-new activity pattern in Lue’s brain through the connection in his right hand. It was not heat source signals, not sorting, not comparison. It was a slow, back-and-forth stream of information ; Lue was reminiscing. Lue was connecting the newly merged Wisdom concept with its memories from the past two years.

Shuichi paused for a moment on the Naka River Stone Bridge. Lue lifted its head, its gaze following Shuichi’s line of sight ; the water of the Naka River flowed downstream under the afternoon sun. Lue watched for a while, then turned its head toward the Village.

It was looking at the Village. Not scanning, but looking—looking with an understanding gaze.
