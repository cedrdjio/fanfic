# Chapitre 79 — War is Coming

The morning rain was finer than usual.

When Jiraiya opened his eyes, a toad was crouching on the windowsill.

It was a messenger toad, its red skin standing out against the gray morning light.

A small scroll with a seal was tied to its leg.

Jiraiya sat up from the tatami and walked to the window.

He undid the seal on the scroll and unfolded it.

It was a handwritten letter from Sandaime, Hiruzen Sarutobi.

The letter was short, but every word felt as heavy as a stone.

There were movements in every nation.

Kumogakure was increasing troops in the direction of the Land of Lightning, Iwagakure was frequently mobilizing at the border of the Land of Earth, and the Hidden Mist was restless on the sea.

The Village needed to recall the Sannin in advance to prevent the situation from deteriorating further.

Jiraiya clenched the scroll in his palm.

He stood at the window, watching the continuous curtain of rain outside.

The rain dyed everything in gray ; the eaves, the streets, and the shadows of the mountains in the distance were all blurred in the mist.

He had known this land for many years.

War ground small countries into ruins, rain soaked the ruins into mud, then sunlight dried the mud into dust, and the wind blew the dust away, leaving nothing behind.

He had seen this cycle too many times, until there was nothing left.

Footsteps came from inside the room.

Yahiko walked out of the room and paused when he saw Jiraiya standing by the window.

« Teacher, what’s wrong ? »

Nagato and Konan also came out.

The three of them stood behind Jiraiya, looking at the scroll in his hand.

Jiraiya turned around and placed the letter on the table.

« I have to return to Konoha. »

The room fell silent.

The sound of rain tapped rhythmically on the roof, making a pattering sound.

Konan stood by the door, her hand still resting on the doorframe.

Nagato looked at the scroll on the table, the pupils of his Rinnegan rotating slightly in the dim light.

Yahiko opened his mouth, wanting to say something, but didn’t.

Jiraiya looked at their faces.

When he first met them, Yahiko was still a thin, small boy shouting, « I will change this country, » Nagato didn’t even dare to meet his eyes, and the wings of the paper cranes Konan folded were always crooked.

Now, Yahiko was already able to stand on his own.

Jiraiya took a deep breath and then let it out.

Packing his luggage didn’t take long.

Jiraiya’s luggage was already sparse—a backpack, a few changes of clothes, and a few books.

He stuffed these things into his backpack and tied the opening.

The three of them stood in the courtyard waiting for him.

The rain was finer than usual, hitting his face like mist.

Yahiko stood at the front, hands in his pockets, the corners of his mouth pulled tight.

Nagato stood beside him, his gaze falling on Jiraiya’s backpack.

Konan stood at the back, head lowered, holding something in her hand.

Jiraiya shouldered his backpack and walked up to Yahiko.

« As the leader, don’t carry everything by yourself when things happen ; communicate more with Nagato and Konan. »

Yahiko looked at him, wanting to smile, but the corners of his mouth only lifted slightly before dropping back down.

« I know. »

Jiraiya nodded and walked over to Nagato.

He placed his hand on Nagato’s head.

Nagato’s hair was dampened by the rain, sticking to his forehead.

« Your eyes are meant to protect the people you care about. »

Jiraiya’s voice was calm, carrying a faint hoarseness.

« You are much stronger than you imagine. But strength doesn’t come from those eyes ; it comes from the things you care about. »

Nagato didn’t nod or answer.

He looked at Jiraiya, the pupils of his Rinnegan trembling slightly in the rain.

A few seconds later, Jiraiya withdrew his hand.

Finally, it was Konan.

He walked up to her and looked at her.

Konan lowered her head and reached out her hand.

A paper crane lay in her palm.

Its wings were flat, not crooked at all.

« For you. »

Her voice was very soft, almost drowned out by the sound of the rain.

Jiraiya took the paper crane and tucked it into the pocket of his backpack.

« I will return. »

Konan raised her head and looked at him.

« When ? »

« I don’t know, » Jiraiya said. « But I will return. »

Jiraiya turned and walked into the curtain of rain.

He did not look back.

The rain slanted, quickly swallowing him up.

The figures of the three people behind him became blurred and finally disappeared completely into the gray mist.

Jiraiya walked out along the streets of the town.

The accumulated water rose above his ankles, the splashing water wetting his trouser legs.

People were already starting to go about their business on the street ; a woman was hanging laundry under the eaves, and a few children were chasing each other in the puddles.

They were already used to the rain.

They didn’t care if they got wet.

Jiraiya walked past the vegetable market, where a grain merchant was crouching on a wooden crate, looking at a half-empty rice bag.

The price of rice was rising rapidly ; the supply from the Land of Fire had stopped.

Life in the Land of Rain had always been like this.

When the games of the great nations rolled over this land, they never said hello.

The rain washed everything into a gray hue, but people still had to eat, still had to hang laundry, and still had to live on in the rain.

Jiraiya walked out of the town and headed north along the mountain road.

Konoha was in the Land of Fire, about three days' journey from the Land of Rain.

If he hurried, he could arrive in two days.

The rain was still falling.

Jiraiya looked up at the sky ; the gray clouds pressed down very low, with no end in sight.

The Third Shinobi World War was about to begin.

At the same time, in Konoha Village.

Many people had gathered in front of the Hokage Building.

Sandaime Hokage, Hiruzen Sarutobi, stood on the steps, wearing his black combat uniform, with the stone wall of the Hokage Building behind him.

His face was full of wrinkles, but his gaze remained sharp.

Wartime mobilization order.

The streets were covered with notices of material requisition, and wounded soldiers from the front line were being sent back to the Village one after another.

Ninjas from the Medical Corps pushed stretchers through the streets, with wounded soldiers wrapped in bandages lying on them ; blood seeped through the bandages, staining them a dark red.

The villagers stood by the roadside, watching the stretchers being pushed past.

Some covered their mouths, some lowered their heads, and some clenched their fists.

War had come again.

Hiruzen Sarutobi stood on the steps, looking at the villagers below.

« The Village needs the strength of every single person. »

His voice was calm, but every word reached everyone’s ears clearly.

« Material requisition begins today. All Ninjas at the Chunin level and above must be ready to accept mission assignments at any time. »

Whispering began in the crowd.

Hiruzen Sarutobi continued : « The situation on the front line is not optimistic. Kumogakure, Iwagakure, and Hidden Mist are all making moves. We must prepare for the worst. »

His gaze swept over the crowd and landed on a few Jonin.

« Minato Namikaze. »

A young blond Jonin stepped out from the crowd and stood before Hiruzen Sarutobi.

« Present. »

« Lead a squad and take charge of reconnaissance missions on the border of the Land of Fire. Report any abnormalities immediately. »

« Yes. »

Hiruzen Sarutobi nodded and looked at the others.

« Hiruzen-sama. »

A Chunin squeezed out of the crowd with an anxious look on his face.

« There are more and more wounded soldiers from the front, and the Medical Corps is short-handed. »

« I will arrange it, » Hiruzen Sarutobi’s voice was calm. « First, let the lightly wounded soldiers be transferred to the rear hospital, and keep the seriously wounded ones with the Medical Corps in the Village. »

The Chunin nodded and turned to run away.

Hiruzen Sarutobi watched his retreating back and was silent for a few seconds.

War had begun again.

He had seen scenes like this too many times : material requisition, wounded soldiers being sent back, villagers panicking.

Every war started this way, and every time, people would die.

But he had not retired yet, and now he had to stand up again to lead the Village through the difficulties.

Hiruzen Sarutobi took a deep breath and looked at the gray sky.

He walked into the Hokage Building.

The countdown to war had begun.
