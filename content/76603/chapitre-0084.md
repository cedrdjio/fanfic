# Chapitre 84 — Flying Thunder God

The morning mist had not yet dispersed when Shuichi left Konoha.

He did not take the main gate. Upstream of the Naka River, there was a mountain path known only to the Uchiha Clan, which cut through the edge of the Forest of Death, bypassed the sensory barrier of the Hokage Office, and led directly into the deep mountains north of Konoha. He had walked this path for five years ; every step was etched into his memory.

Lue circled overhead, its crow-like wings slicing through the mist without making a sound.

« Where to ? » Lue asked.

« North, » Shuichi said. « To find somewhere deserted. »

Lue did not ask again. It tucked its wings tight, gliding along the treetops, its Infrared Sensing sweeping across a three-kilometer radius ahead to confirm there were no Chakra fluctuations.

Shuichi walked along the mountain ridge for two hours, crossed two peaks, and arrived at a deep valley he had discovered while hunting bears in the past. At the bottom of the valley flowed a stream, flanked by dense cedar forests. The nearest village was twenty kilometers away. No Ninja patrol routes passed through here, there were no traces of hunters, and even wild beasts avoided the area due to the steep rocky walls of the valley.

Shuichi jumped down the rock wall and landed on the gravel beach by the stream.

He stood there, watching his reflection in the water. An eleven-year-old face, black hair, ordinary black pupils. The Mangekyo Sharingan was not activated ; he looked just like a callow youth.

He took a deep breath and closed his eyes.

Flying Thunder God Technique.

The structure of the technique unfolded in his mind.

Every node of the technique, every path of Chakra flow, and every seal pattern was engraved in his memory.

The core principle of the Flying Thunder God was not complicated. One left a Chakra mark at the target location, then resonated one’s own Chakra with the mark to instantly transfer through a spatial channel. The technique was divided into three parts : the marking technique, the resonance technique, and the transfer technique. The marking technique was the simplest, requiring only the injection of Chakra into a specifically structured seal pattern ; the resonance technique was the key, requiring precise control to synchronize Chakra frequency with the mark ; the transfer technique was the result, with the spatial channel opening automatically once resonance was achieved.

The principle was simple, but that did not mean it was easy to achieve.

Shuichi opened his eyes. He crouched down, concentrated Chakra on his right index finger, and carved the marking technique onto a stone by the stream. The Chakra seeped into the texture of the stone, forming a thumb-sized seal pattern. The pattern glowed for a moment, then dimmed, stably adhering to the surface of the stone.

The mark was complete.

Shuichi stepped back ten paces and stood at the other end of the gravel beach. He took a deep breath, formed hand seals, and began guiding his Chakra to resonate with the mark.

Chakra surged from his Dantian, flowed along the meridian system to his hands, and then extended outward, attempting to touch the mark on the stone.

But he could not find the mark.

Shuichi frowned.

He could feel the presence of the mark. It was right there, on the stone ten paces away, the Chakra pattern stably attached to the stone’s surface. But when he tried to touch that mark with his Chakra, it felt as if it were separated by a layer of mist. The location of the mark was blurry, like the moon reflected in water—it looked like it was there, but when he reached out to grab it, he couldn’t.

He began to adjust his Chakra frequency, attempting to resonate with the mark.

First attempt. The Chakra fluctuations dissipated in the air ; he did not find the resonance point. Failure.

Shuichi was not discouraged. He adjusted the frequency and tried again.

Second time. The same result. The mark was right there, but he could not precisely perceive its position and frequency.

Third time. Fourth time. Fifth time.

Every time he tried, he could feel the existence of the mark, but he could not perceive it clearly. It was as if an invisible barrier stood between his perception and the mark, making the mark blurry, distant, and difficult to reach.

Shuichi stopped trying and stood in place, thinking.

Where was the problem ?

He re-examined the structure of the Flying Thunder God Technique in his mind. There was no problem with the marking technique ; he had successfully carved the mark. He also fully understood the principle of the resonance technique ; it only required synchronizing his Chakra frequency with the mark. But the prerequisite for synchronization was precisely perceiving the frequency and position of the mark.

And he could not do that.

Shuichi closed his eyes, carefully recalling the feeling from just moments ago.

His Chakra sensory ability was very strong. Infrared Sensing could lock onto living organisms within three kilometers, the Sharingan could see through Chakra flow, and his mental energy was sufficient to support precise Chakra control. But these were all perceptions targeting physical entities, or perceptions targeting Chakra itself.

The Flying Thunder God mark was not a physical target ; it was a Chakra node attached to space. Perceiving such a node required a different ability.

Spatial perception. Or rather, spatial talent.

Every Ninja’s Chakra had elemental affinities : Fire, Wind, Lightning, Earth, and Water. But beyond these five basic attributes, Chakra had deeper characteristics. Some people’s Chakra was naturally more sensitive to certain concepts. The Uchiha Clan’s Chakra was sensitive to mental energy, which was why the Sharingan could see through illusions and Chakra flow. The Uzumaki Clan’s Chakra was sensitive to Vitality, which was why they possessed powerful recovery abilities and a talent for Sealing Technique.

Minato Namikaze’s Chakra was sensitive to space.

This sensitivity allowed Minato to precisely perceive the location of every Chakra mark in space. His Flying Thunder God marks did not need to be seen with his eyes, nor did they need to be locked onto with conventional Chakra perception ; he could directly « feel » where the mark was, how far away it was, and what its frequency was. This feeling was intuitive—it was space itself responding to him.

But Shuichi’s Chakra did not have this spatial sensitivity.

His Chakra possessed all five elements, and each was very strong. The Uzumaki Bloodline gave him recovery abilities and a talent for Sealing Technique, while the Uchiha Bloodline gave him the Sharingan and mental energy. But neither involved space.

Without spatial talent, he could not clearly perceive the mark he had carved. The mark was right there ; he knew it was there, but he could not precisely lock onto its position and frequency. If he couldn’t lock onto it, he couldn’t resonate. If he couldn’t resonate, he couldn’t open the spatial channel.

Shuichi stood by the stream, watching the ripples on the water.

He tried again. This time he did not strive for resonance, but instead carefully felt his own perception of the mark.

Chakra extended outward, touching that layer of mist. The mist was thin, but enough to make the mark blurry. He strove to penetrate that layer of mist, trying hard to see the outline of the mark clearly, but every time he could only get a blurry image, like looking at something through a layer of frosted glass.

He tried repeatedly twenty times. The result was the same every time : the mark existed, but the perception was blurry. He could know the approximate location of the mark, but he could not precisely lock onto it. Resonance required precise frequency synchronization ; being off by even a fraction would lead to failure.

After twenty times, Shuichi stopped trying.

He sat on the stone by the stream, watching the water.

The conclusion was clear : without spatial talent, the Flying Thunder God Technique could not be performed. He could carve the mark, but he could not perceive the mark. It was like a person who could draw a dot on a map, but whose eyes were covered, unable to see where that dot was. There was no problem with the map, and no problem with the dot ; the problem was with himself.

Shuichi raised his right hand and looked at his palm.

Plunder everything.

This ability could Plunder everything. Physical matter, Chakra attributes, Bloodline Limits, Ninjutsu skills, Vitality, sensory abilities, and even conceptual existences.

Spatial talent, could it be plundered ?

Shuichi closed his eyes and began to think.

Spatial talent was not a Chakra attribute, not a Bloodline Limit, and not a Ninjutsu skill. It was closer to an underlying characteristic of Chakra, a natural affinity. Just like the Uzumaki Bloodline’s affinity for Vitality, just like the Uchiha Bloodline’s affinity for mental energy.

He had plundered the Uzumaki Bloodline. That was a Bloodline Limit, which had a clear physical carrier and could be plundered through contact. But spatial talent was not a Bloodline Limit ; it had no physical carrier ; it only existed in the underlying structure of Chakra.

Then, he needed to find a Ninja who possessed spatial talent, make contact with his right hand, and Plunder the other person’s spatial talent.

The question was, who had spatial talent ?

Minato Namikaze. The user of the Flying Thunder God, the person with the most obvious spatial talent. But the current Shuichi could not yet make a move against Minato.

Besides Minato, who else ?

Shuichi recalled the knowledge from his past life and information about this world.

The Hidden Cloud Village. The Heavenly Transfer Technique. That was a spatial transfer Ninjutsu unique to the Hidden Cloud, requiring specific geographic markers and technique coordination, but the user had to possess a certain degree of spatial affinity to withstand the pressure of the spatial channel. There were not many users of the Heavenly Transfer Technique, but the Hidden Cloud definitely had them.

Iwagakure. Iwagakure did not have famous spatial Ninjutsu, but their Barrier Ninjutsu was very strong. The essence of Barrier Ninjutsu was spatial sealing, capable of distorting spatial structure within a certain range. Ninjas with high attainments in Barrier Ninjutsu should have a certain degree of sensitivity to space in their Chakra.

The Hidden Sand Village. Elder Chiyo’s Puppet Technique did not involve space. But the Hidden Sand’s Sealing Technique had branches of spatial sealing, and although not as profound as the Uzumaki Clan’s, the users should have a weak spatial affinity.

The Hidden Mist Village. Spatial Ninjutsu was almost non-existent. The Hidden Mist’s fighting style was mainly based on Water Release and Bloodline Limits.

Inside Konoha. Besides Minato, only the Fourth Hokage could use the Flying Thunder God ; no one else had mastered it. But within Konoha’s Sealing Technique and Barrier squads, there might be people with spatial talent.

Shuichi opened his eyes.

The battlefield.

The Third Ninja World War was ongoing ; the Ninjas of the Five Great Nations were gathered on four battlefields. Someone among them must possess spatial talent. He didn’t need to find the strongest ; he just needed to find one whose spatial affinity was enough for him to perceive the Flying Thunder God mark clearly.

Even if it was just a weak spatial talent, after plundering it, he could turn the Flying Thunder God from « completely unable to perceive » to « barely able to perceive. » Then he could Plunder a second, a third, and stack them up ; he would eventually be able to reach Minato’s level.

Shuichi stood up from the stone.

He looked to the south ; that was the direction of the northern border of the Land of Fire, and also the battlefield where his Shadow Clone was located.
