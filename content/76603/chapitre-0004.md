# Chapitre 4 — Plunder and Gift

Three days later, the fledgling’s left wing could move.

Shuichi crouched beside the small wooden box temporarily set up under the eaves of the backyard, watching the fledgling tentatively spread its left wing. The movement was still stiff, and the wingtip trembled slightly, but the extent of its spread was already about the same as the right wing.

The fledgling itself seemed satisfied with this progress ; it lifted its head and let out a short chirp, then lowered its head to continue tearing at the strips of meat Shuichi had placed by the side of the wooden box.

« It recovered faster than I thought, » Shuichi said softly.

He gently touched the fledgling’s back with his right index finger, and the sensory function inherent to his Plunder ability activated once again.

The situation inside the fledgling was more than twice as clear as it had been three days ago ; the minor tears in the wing muscles had healed, and the joints were no longer swollen. The heartbeat was strong and powerful, more vigorous than any small animal he had encountered.

It was ready. This body could already withstand the impact of an external ability.

But before that, there was one thing he had to deal with first.

Shuichi looked down at the back of his left hand. The patch of pale grey, rough skin had spread from around the bloodstain to the entire back of his hand ; when he washed his face this morning, he discovered that the same texture had appeared on the inside of his wrist.

Carapace Hardness was slowly but continuously taking effect within his body, a function he did not want.

The feeling of rejection grew with each passing day. Every morning, when he looked into the water basin and saw that his skin had changed a little more, the nausea churning in his stomach became a fraction more intense than the day before.

This was not a rejection of power—he had no psychological barriers to using his Climbing Ability. Rather, it was an instinctive resistance to something that was slowly altering his appearance.

He was not a rhinoceros beetle. He did not want his skin to turn into a carapace.

Shuichi stood up and carried the fledgling, along with its wooden box, to the plum tree in the yard. The spring sunlight filtered through the sparse plum branches and spilled onto the wooden box, and the fledgling squinted its eyes comfortably.

« Mom, I’m taking the little eagle to the woods to get some fresh air. »

Minako poked her head out from the kitchen, glanced at the fledgling sunning itself under the plum tree, then looked at Shuichi’s serious expression and didn’t say much else. « Don’t stay too long. We’re having grilled fish for lunch. »

« Okay. »

Shuichi picked up the wooden box and walked along the path at the edge of the Uchiha Clan Grounds toward the Mixed Forest again. He paused when passing the Naka River Stone Bridge ; there were no traces of Fire Release on the river surface, and Minato was not there today.

He withdrew his gaze and continued walking, his wooden clogs making a soft crunching sound on the gravel path.

The Mixed Forest was the same as ever. Crooked zelkova trees, low bushes, and the ground fragmented by sunlight filtering through the leaves. Shuichi set the wooden box down in the usual spot and lifted the thin cloth covering the fledgling. The fledgling blinked, curiously observing this unfamiliar environment.

« Don’t move around ; your wings can’t fly yet, » Shuichi tapped the fledgling’s head with his fingertip. « Wait for me a moment. »

He searched the base of the bushes for less than five minutes before finding a rhinoceros beetle. It was a size smaller than the previous one, slowly crawling in a pile of rotting leaves. Shuichi picked it up, placed it in his palm, and focused his consciousness into his right hand.

The information surging inside the rhinoceros beetle was the same as last time : Carapace Hardness, horn strength, and weak Vitality. Shuichi took a deep breath and pressed his right hand onto the beetle’s back shell.

This time he was not Plundering. He was stripping it away.

He pushed the Carapace Hardness—the force that was making his skin rough—into his right palm, and then pushed it into the rhinoceros beetle. The feeling of stripping was the exact opposite of Plundering ; it was not drawing in, but pushing out. A warmth came from his palm, followed by a sense of relief, as if he had finally exhaled a breath he’d been holding for a long time.

The rhinoceros beetle’s body suddenly swelled. Its back shell became darker and glossier at a rate visible to the naked eye, and its already hard chitinous shell thickened by another layer. But it did not die ; this rhinoceros beetle seemed to possess stronger Vitality and adaptability.

Meanwhile, Shuichi looked down at the back of his left hand. The patch of pale grey, rough texture was receding at a rate visible to the naked eye. It didn’t disappear instantly ; instead, like melting ice, the grey color faded from the edges first, and then the roughness peeled away layer by layer, revealing the normal skin of a five-year-old child underneath. He turned over the inside of his wrist ; the alienation there was also receding. In less than half a minute, the back of his hand had completely returned to normal.

Shuichi let out a long breath. The nausea that had been pressing on his stomach dissipated, as if a small but constantly grinding stone had been lifted from his shoulders.

He placed the rhinoceros beetle back into the pile of leaves. The beetle froze for half a second, then used its thicker, glossier carapace to push aside a dead leaf and burrowed deep into the bushes.

« One physical slot has opened up. » Shuichi flexed his fingers, feeling the space inside his body becoming spacious again. Now, only the Climbing Ability remained as a tenant in his physical slot, while the Flight Instinct and that one-third of the dragonfly compound eye vision resided in his mental slot.

He looked up at the rough trunk of the old zelkova tree. The Climbing Ability Plundered from the Tree Frog had been in his body for five days, but it had always existed only passively. He could climb trees, but he had never seriously used this ability. And according to the rules he had summarized from his experiments, repeated use was the only way to digest it.

Shuichi walked to the zelkova tree and pressed his hands against the bark.

The tiny ridges at his fingertips were the projection of the Tree Frog’s toe pads onto his skin. He immediately found an attachment point ; without needing to exert deliberate force, his palms felt as if they were glued to the bark by invisible glue. Then came his feet. He had kicked off his wooden clogs long ago, and as he stepped barefoot onto the bark, the Climbing ridges on his toes provided the same gripping power as his fingers.

He began to climb up.

It was not the clumsy posture of a child climbing a tree, using both hands and feet, but a fluid, continuous, and natural climb, just like a Tree Frog moving on a slippery leaf. His body clung tightly to the trunk, his center of gravity shifting from one hand to the other, from one foot to the other. Every change of hand and foot precisely found the most suitable grip on the bark, as if his body naturally knew where to grab.

He climbed to the top of the ten-meter-high zelkova tree in less than twenty seconds.

Shuichi sat astride the highest branch of the zelkova tree, breathing heavily—not from exhaustion, but from excitement. Then he turned around, head facing down, and climbed down step by step using the same Climbing movements. Gravity was pulling at him, but the gripping power of his fingers and toes steadily resisted the downward acceleration.

After reaching the ground, he didn’t stop ; he climbed it again. And then again.

When he climbed to the top of the tree for the fourth time, Shuichi noticed a change. The delay of [needing to think] during the climbing process had disappeared. For the first three times, before every change of hand or foot, he still needed a subconscious judgment : Is this bark firm enough ? Can this protrusion support my weight ? But starting from the fourth time, these judgments were no longer judgments. They had become instinct.

Just like breathing. No need to think.

Shuichi sat on the tree branch, feeling the subtle sensation at his fingertips. The Climbing Ability was still there, not stripped away. But its state inside his body had undergone some indescribable change, as if it had transformed from a piece of clothing worn on his body into a layer of skin grown on his body.

This was the ability being digested.

He slid down from the tree, brushed the bark shavings off his hands, and walked over to the wooden box to crouch down.

The fledgling had grown impatient and was pecking at the wooden board on the edge of the box with its beak. Seeing Shuichi approach, it lifted its head and let out a loud chirp.

« Tired of waiting ? » Shuichi reached out and stroked the fledgling’s head. « Then let’s begin. »

He carried the wooden box to a relatively open clearing in the center of the Mixed Forest. Sunlight poured down through the gaps in the canopy, casting a bright spot of light on the ground. The fledgling was placed in the center of the light, tilting its head to look at Shuichi, its small, black, shiny eyes reflecting the spring light.

Shuichi sat cross-legged, extended his right hand, and gently pressed his palm against the fledgling’s back.

The sparrow’s Flight Instinct was waiting quietly in his mental slot. The ability, Plundered five days ago from the sparrow that died under the plum tree, was almost useless to him, because the human body size and bone structure destined that even with the Flight Instinct, he could not truly fly. But for an eagle, the Flight Instinct was like a puzzle piece tailor-made for it, fitting perfectly into the wings and wing muscles it was born with.

« It might be a little uncomfortable, » Shuichi whispered. « You’ll have to endure it. »

He focused his consciousness on the Flight Instinct and then stripped it away.

What came from his right palm was not the usual suction-like heat flow of Plundering, but a warm flow emerging outward, carrying his body temperature. The Flight Instinct was extracted from his body and, along an invisible channel in his palm, slowly injected into the fledgling’s body.

The fledgling suddenly spread its wings.

Its body tensed up, its claws digging tightly into the bottom of the wooden box. Its wings were fully spread to an extent far beyond when it was injured three days ago. The grey-brown flight feathers had not yet fully grown, but the wingspan was already close to the combined width of Shuichi’s two palms. Its pupils contracted first, then dilated, then contracted again, as if digesting some massive amount of information that had suddenly flooded into its mind.

Flight is not knowledge. Flight is instinct ; it is sensing the subtle changes in wind direction, the subconscious reaction to adjust the angle of the wing surface when the wind rises, and the muscle memory to instinctively flip and regain lift when stalling in the air. These things are not learned ; they are engraved in the bones of every bird that can fly.

Now, the sparrow’s Flight Instinct and the fledgling’s body were merging.

The fledgling let out a long cry. It was different from any previous chirp. This long cry carried an indescribable wildness, as if a switch had been flipped.

Then it jumped out of the wooden box.

Shuichi did not stop it. He watched the fledgling jump onto the ground, its wings twitching unnaturally. It wasn’t a spasm, but a test. It was using the newly acquired Flight Instinct to re-understand its wings. The opening and closing angles of its left and right wings, the feathers' perception of airflow, and the posture its tail should maintain in the wind—all this information was rapidly reorganizing in its mind.

The fledgling lifted its head and looked at the sky.

Shuichi also looked up. The canopy of the Mixed Forest blocked most of the sky, but through the gaps in the zelkova and oak trees, one could still see a few clouds moving slowly.

The fledgling spread its wings.

Its legs bent, and then it pushed off the ground violently. Both wings flapped downward simultaneously, kicking up a small patch of fallen leaves and dust. Its body left the ground for ten centimeters—twenty centimeters—and then it tilted. The left wing was clearly half a beat slower than the right, and its whole body slanted and crashed into the bushes nearby.

Shuichi hurriedly ran over and pushed aside the bushes. The fledgling crawled out from between the branches, shook the broken leaves off its head, and looked not only uninjured but even more excited.

It tried again.

This time, the flapping amplitude of the left wing balanced with the right wing. Its body rose steadily to half a meter high. Then one meter. Then it flew up from the clearing in the Mixed Forest, crossing the tallest bushes, crossing the low branches of the crooked zelkova tree, the sound of its wings flapping against the air becoming more and more powerful.

Shuichi stood on the ground, head tilted back, watching the fledgling circle above the Mixed Forest.

It was not flying high enough yet. The arc of its circling still carried obvious awkwardness, and it needed to flap its wings a couple of extra times when turning to maintain altitude. But it was indeed flying. A fledgling less than two weeks old. Normally, it would need at least a month to leave the nest, yet it was circling above his head.

The Flight Instinct had taken effect.

The fledgling circled in the air three times and then began to descend. It aimed for Shuichi’s shoulder ; although its movements were very clumsy, its claws hooked onto Shuichi’s collar as it landed, and its whole body swayed before stabilizing. Shuichi reached out to support its body, and he could feel its heart beating violently, but in those black, shiny eyes, there was something new that hadn’t been there before.

Confidence.

« You can fly now, » Shuichi said softly, the corners of his mouth slightly upturned. « But it’s not good enough yet. When your wings are fully healed, you will fly higher than that cliff, higher than all the adult eagles that have ever circled over the Training Ground. »

The fledgling tilted its head and let out a low chirp.

Shuichi looked at it and thought for a moment. « I’ll give you a name. »

Several options flashed through his mind—Falcon, Shadow, Wind, Blade. In the end, he chose the simplest and most direct one.

« Plunder. » Shuichi tapped the fledgling’s head with his fingertip. « I’ll call you Plunder. »

This name subtly matched his ability : Plunder. It also matched the eagle’s nature : predation. More importantly, this fledgling would become the first node in his ability ecosystem in the future ; it would carry the abilities stripped from his hands, scouting for him and keeping watch for him.

Plunder pecked at Shuichi’s earlobe, indicating its agreement.

Shuichi placed Plunder back into the wooden box and carried it out of the Mixed Forest. On the way back, passing the Naka River Stone Bridge, he paused. There were still no traces of Fire Release on the river surface, but there were a few shallow scorch marks left on the stone slabs of the bridge—those were left by Minato practicing the Dragon Fire Technique a few days ago.

Shuichi looked at those scorch marks for two seconds and continued walking back.

When he returned home, Minako was hanging laundry in the yard. Seeing Shuichi walk in carrying the wooden box, she put down the wet clothes in her hands, walked over to take a look, and then froze.

Plunder was standing on the edge of the wooden box, wings slightly spread, looking ready to fly again at any moment.

« It... can fly ? » Minako looked at the fledgling in disbelief. « It’s only been three days. Wasn’t its wing injured ? »

« It recovered quickly, » Shuichi answered in the most innocent tone. « Maybe Dad is watching over it from heaven. »

Minako opened her mouth, but in the end, she just shook her head and wiped the sweat from her forehead with the back of her hand. She didn’t continue to press for answers ; perhaps she felt there wouldn’t be an answer even if she asked, or perhaps she felt that it was better not to know some things.

Shuichi moved Plunder’s wooden box back under the plum tree in the backyard. Plunder jumped out of the wooden box, circled low in the yard for half a lap, and then landed on the lowest branch of the plum tree, looking down at everything in the yard—the clothesline, the water vat, the woodpile, Shuichi, and the layers of eaves of the Uchiha Clan Grounds in the distance.

Shuichi stood under the plum tree, looking up at Plunder.

The Flight Instinct had been stripped away. The Carapace Hardness had also been stripped away. Now, his physical slots were almost empty—only one Climbing Ability remained, which was being digested. His mental slot was also half empty. He had plenty of space on hand, and there were plenty of creatures around to Plunder.

He had already thought of the target for his next Plunder.

But not today. Today was the day of Plunder’s first flight. Shuichi leaned against the plum tree trunk, feeling the breeze brought by the fledgling’s wing flaps above his head, and closed his eyes, a faint, imperceptible smile hanging on the corners of his mouth.

The eighteen-year countdown was still ticking. But today, he didn’t want to count it.
