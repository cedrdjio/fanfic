# Chapitre 35 — Reaction

The news of White Fang’s suicide spread throughout Konoha within half a day.

Shuichi heard various versions of the story on Saturday afternoon. Walking along the commercial street, people were discussing it in front of every shop. The owner of the Ninja tool shop stood at the entrance talking to two Chunin, his tone filled with a flustered sense of unpreparedness. The ramen shop owner was wiping the table, and a customer nearby whispered, « I didn’t expect him to commit suicide. » The owner of the confectionery shop sighed behind the counter, saying, « Two days ago they were still calling him a disgrace, and now that he’s dead, they’re starting to say it’s a pity. »

Hiruzen Sarutobi sat in his chair in the Hokage Office, his fingers pressed against the desktop.

A report that had just been delivered lay before him. Sakumo Hatake, a Konoha Jonin and a powerhouse known as the White Fang of Konoha, had committed suicide by stabbing his heart with his personal blade, « White Light, » in front of the Memorial Stone early this morning. The time of death was approximately six in the morning, and the discoverer was Sakumo’s son, Kakashi Hatake.

After reading the report, Hiruzen remained silent for a long time.

He had not anticipated that Sakumo would commit suicide.

The suppression of public opinion against Sakumo had been calculated. Sakumo had abandoned a mission to save his comrades, causing Konoha to lose a critical intelligence line. This matter had to be met with consistent negative evaluation throughout the Village ; otherwise, other Ninja might choose to save their comrades and abandon missions in similar situations in the future, threatening Konoha’s mission completion rate and intelligence security. Therefore, the direction of public opinion had been set from the start : Sakumo was a disgrace, and abandoning a mission was tantamount to betraying the Village.

This public opinion was not intended to drive Sakumo to his death. It was to make him obedient. To ensure he would not make the same mistake in future missions, and to let other Ninja see the consequences of abandoning a mission, thereby prioritizing the mission when faced with similar choices.

What Hiruzen needed was a living, constrained White Fang. A White Fang who knew he had made a mistake and would obey the rules in the future. Konoha needed White Fang’s combat power ; such a powerhouse was an irreplaceable strategic resource.

But now, White Fang was dead.

Hiruzen put down the report and called for an Anbu member.

« Go and bring Danzo here. »

The Anbu member vanished. Hiruzen sat in his chair waiting, his fingers pressed against the desktop, his knuckles slightly white.

About ten minutes later, the door to the office was pushed open.

Danzo Shimura walked in. His pace was as steady as ever, his cane making rhythmic tapping sounds on the floor. There was no extra expression on his face, but Hiruzen noticed his gaze sweep across the report on the table as he walked in.

Hiruzen looked at him, remaining silent for three seconds.

« You went too far. »

Danzo’s expression did not change. He looked at Hiruzen as if listening to an irrelevant remark.

« The intensity of the public opinion exceeded expectations, » Hiruzen said, his voice not loud, but his tone heavy. « I had you push the direction of public opinion to constrain Sakumo’s behavior, not to drive him to suicide. You should understand what the loss of a powerhouse with a title means to Konoha. »

« The direction of public opinion was not the problem, » Danzo said. « Sakumo abandoned his mission and violated the code ; he had to be punished. This is a matter of principle, and it cannot be an exception just because he is a powerhouse with a title. »

« Principle ? » Hiruzen stared at him. « Your principle drove a Kage-level powerhouse to his death. »

« It wasn’t my principle that drove him to death, » Danzo said, his tone cold and hard. « It was his own choice to die. He couldn’t withstand the pressure of public opinion ; that is his own problem. He was too cowardly. »

Hiruzen’s fingers stopped on the desktop.

« Cowardly ? » Hiruzen’s voice lowered. « How many enemies did Sakumo kill on the battlefield ? How many S-rank missions did he execute ? He was cowardly ? »

« Not being cowardly on the battlefield doesn’t mean not being cowardly in his heart, » Danzo said. « He chose to commit suicide, and that is cowardice. A true powerhouse should withstand the pressure and prove with actions that his choice was right. He chose death, which shows that even he himself did not believe in his own choice. »

Hiruzen stood up.

« Regardless of whether Sakumo was cowardly or not, the result is that Konoha has lost a powerhouse with a title. » Hiruzen’s voice was not loud, but every word felt like a nail driven into the air. « You know what a powerhouse with a title means. If Konoha has one less, other countries will have ideas they shouldn’t have. The Daimyo of the Land of Fire will not sit idly by while the Village’s combat power declines. Sakumo is dead because the intensity of the public opinion you pushed spun out of control. You will bear this responsibility. »

Danzo’s brow furrowed slightly.

« Root’s funding will be cut by thirty percent, » Hiruzen said.

Danzo’s gaze sharpened. « You— »

« This is not a discussion, » Hiruzen said. « Someone must bear the cost of the death of a powerhouse with a title. You pushed the public opinion, and the public opinion spiraled out of control, leading to Sakumo’s suicide ; this is a fact. Cutting funding is the lightest consequence. »

The office was silent for a few seconds. Danzo looked at Hiruzen, and Hiruzen looked at Danzo. There was no extra emotion in either pair of eyes, only a calm, calculated firmness.

Danzo stood up and picked up his cane.

« Hiruzen. » His voice was not loud, but every word was clear. « You will regret this. »

Hiruzen retorted loudly, « I am the Hokage. »

Danzo glanced at him before turning and walking out of the office, the tapping of his cane gradually fading down the corridor.

Hiruzen stood by the window, watching the direction Danzo had left. He moved his fingers from the desktop and picked up his teacup to take a sip. The tea had already gone cold, but he didn’t mind.

He returned to the table and picked up his pen to write two documents. The first was an official statement from the Hokage Office : Jonin Sakumo Hatake passed away due to personal reasons, and the Hokage Office expresses its respect for his many years of contributions to Konoha. The wording was cautious, mentioning neither the specific method of suicide nor the background of the suppression of public opinion. It only stated « personal reasons, » blurring the cause of the suicide.

The second was an instruction to the Anbu : Closely monitor the villagers' discussion regarding Sakumo’s suicide, and if large-scale questioning of the Ninja code arises, report it immediately.

Hiruzen sealed the two documents and placed them on the corner of the table. He needed to wait for public opinion to ferment naturally and then decide on the next step based on its direction.

After returning to Root’s underground office, Danzo called over a Root member.

« Closely monitor the Hokage Office’s handling of Sakumo’s suicide, » Danzo said. « At the same time, record the direction of the villagers' discussions. If there is questioning of the Ninja code, do not interfere ; let the questioning spread naturally. »

The Root member nodded and left. Danzo sat in his chair, his fingers tapping lightly on the desktop.

White Fang’s death was not part of his plan, but perhaps it could be utilized.

Shuichi returned home on Saturday evening.

Minako was cooking in the kitchen, and smoke was rising from the chimney. Shuichi walked into the house, and Lue flew up from his shoulder and landed on a plum branch.

« So many people in the Village are discussing White Fang’s matter today, » Minako said, her tone heavy. « I heard he committed suicide. And right in front of the Memorial Stone. »

« Mm. » Shuichi walked into the kitchen.

« It’s such a pity, » Minako said. « A few days ago, the whole Village was cursing him, and now that he’s dead, they’ve started saying it’s a pity. When a person is alive, no one stands on his side ; only when he’s dead do they start to sympathize. »

Shuichi didn’t speak. What Minako said aligned with his previous judgment. Villagers were forgetful. They would forget everything Sakumo had done for the Village just because he made a choice they didn’t approve of. Now that Sakumo was dead, they started saying « it’s a pity » again. But « it’s a pity » didn’t mean they were on his side ; it was just an instinctive reaction to death. If Sakumo were still alive, they would still continue to curse him.

« Shuichi ? » Minako looked at him. « What are you thinking about ? »

« Nothing, » Shuichi said. « I’ll help you carry the dishes. »

He walked into the kitchen and picked up the bowls and chopsticks. The burning sensation in his palm was still there ; Sakumo’s combat experience was waiting quietly within the mental barrier. Shuichi knew he needed to start digesting it as soon as possible ; the volume of combat experience was too large, and compressing it in the barrier for a long time would consume mental energy to maintain.

But not today. Today, the whole Village was discussing White Fang’s matter, and Shuichi needed to observe the direction of public opinion.

On Sunday morning, Shuichi took a trip to the commercial street.

The direction of public opinion had already begun to reverse.

In front of the Ninja tool shop, two Chunin were discussing. Their tone was different from a few days ago. A few days ago, it was anger and disdain ; today, it had turned into a flustered silence.

« I didn’t expect him to commit suicide, » one Chunin said. « I was cursing him a few days ago, and now that I think about it... »

« Don’t think about it, » another Chunin said. « He violated the code ; that is a fact. Suicide was his own choice. »

« But tell me honestly, when we were cursing him, did we ever think he would come to this point ? »

Silence.

Shuichi walked past them without stopping. He needed to hear more.

In front of the ramen shop, three villagers were eating breakfast and discussing in low voices.

« I was saying he was a disgrace two days ago, » one middle-aged man said, his tone uncomfortable. « Now that he’s dead, I always feel... »

« Always feel what ? » the person next to him asked.

« Always feel that we cursed him too much. »

« Too much ? » the third person frowned. « He abandoned the mission and caused the Village to lose an intelligence line ; that is a fact. Cursing him as a disgrace was because he made the wrong choice. »

« But he saved people, » the middle-aged man said. « He chose to save people ; although the mission failed, at least the people who were saved are still alive. »

« What’s the use of the saved people being alive ? The intelligence line is broken, and the Village’s loss is greater. »

« Then what do you say we should do ? Mission and people, which is more important ? »

The three of them fell silent.

Shuichi walked past the ramen shop and continued forward. Public opinion was shifting from « cursing White Fang as a disgrace » to « questioning the Ninja code. » This questioning was still small, with only a few scattered people discussing it, but the direction had changed.

Shuichi took a walk around the commercial street and listened to the discussions of over a dozen people. Most people’s reactions could be divided into three categories :

First category : Unpreparedness. They were cursing White Fang a few days ago, and now that White Fang is dead, they don’t know how to react. This group is the largest.

Second category : Starting to reflect. They feel they cursed too much and are starting to question « which is more important, mission or people. » This group is not large, but the direction is the most dangerous. If this questioning spreads, it will shake Konoha’s Ninja code.

Third category : Maintaining the original stance. White Fang violated the code, that is a fact, and suicide was his own choice. This group is the smallest, but their stance is the firmest.

Shuichi walked home along the Naka River. Lue circled above the riverbank, saw Shuichi, flew down, and landed on his shoulder.

« Public opinion is reversing, » Shuichi said in a low voice.

« Reversing ? » Lue asked.

« A few days ago, they were cursing White Fang as a disgrace ; now they are starting to say it’s a pity. Some people are starting to question the Ninja code, » Shuichi said. « But this kind of questioning won’t last long. The Hokage Office should control the direction of public opinion. »

Lue nodded, seemingly understanding.

Shuichi didn’t pay much attention either, and just walked towards home like this.
