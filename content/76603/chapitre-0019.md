# Chapitre 19 — Taijutsu Class

When Shuichi sat up in bed the next morning, his left rib throbbed with a dull pain.

He lifted his shirt and glanced down. The bruise left by the badger’s kick stretched from the side of his ribs to his lower back. The color had shifted from the dark red of last night to a blue-purple this morning, with the edges beginning to turn yellow. It wasn’t serious ; there was no sharp pain of a fracture, just a soft tissue contusion. The bloodstain on the webbing between his left thumb and index finger had scabbed over.

Shuichi got dressed and paused in front of the mirror. His pupils were pure black. Before going to sleep last night, he had checked repeatedly : when the one-tomoe was not activated, his eyes were indistinguishable from those of an ordinary Uchiha. There was no residual red on his irises, and no outline of tomoe around the edges of his pupils. As long as he didn’t actively channel his ocular power, no one would notice anything unusual when looking into his eyes.

He tried injecting a trace of extremely weak Chakra into his eyes. A complete black tomoe instantly appeared on the edge of his iris, and the crimson iris shone coldly in the mirror. He immediately withdrew the Chakra, and his eyes returned to pure black. The entire process took less than half a second.

He absolutely could not activate his eyes at the Ninja Academy. If news reached any adult Ninja that a six-year-old Uchiha had activated a one-tomoe in the second week of school, it would attract catastrophic levels of attention. What he needed was to remain unnoticed, but he couldn’t deliberately pretend to be weak either. With a physical foundation approaching that of a twelve-year-old, he was naturally at the forefront among his six-year-old peers. What he needed to hide wasn’t his strength itself, but the source and limits of that strength.

During breakfast, Minako took an extra look at him. He held the bowl in his right hand and picked up food with his left, but his body leaned slightly to the right to avoid pressing his left ribs against the edge of the table.

« I slept in a wrong position last night, » Shuichi offered proactively.

Minako didn’t say anything, just pushed the miso soup closer to him. Her gaze lingered on the scab on his left hand for less than a second before moving away.

Shuichi lowered his head to drink the soup. Since his mother didn’t ask, he wouldn’t say anything. This was the unspoken understanding between them. He would also try his best not to let her see any more injuries.

The classroom for Class 1-A of the Ninja Academy was bustling in the morning light. More than twenty six-year-old children were squeezed behind long wooden desks, chatting or flipping through books. Tetsuya leaned over from the back row and patted Shuichi on the shoulder.

« Shuichi ! We have Taijutsu class this afternoon ! »

« I know, » Shuichi said, pulling the Chakra refinement tutorial from his bag and placing it on the desk. « It’s on the schedule. »

« Aren’t you excited ? Taijutsu class ! It’s not staring at leaves, it’s real fighting ! »

Shuichi turned his head to look at Tetsuya. A pure expectation shone in Tetsuya’s eyes—the instinctive excitement of a six-year-old boy for the word 'fighting'. Shuichi wanted to say 'Taijutsu class isn’t fighting,' but he didn’t say it.

« They will probably teach basic footwork first, » Shuichi said.

« How do you know ? »

« I guessed. »

When Teacher Mitomon walked into the classroom in his wooden clogs, the noisy room fell silent. He spread out his lecture notes on the podium and began to explain the basic application of Chakra in Taijutsu. Shuichi listened from his seat by the window in the third row, his right index finger unconsciously drawing circles on the desk.

The theory itself wasn’t difficult for him. Drawing Chakra from the Dantian, channeling it along the meridians to specific muscle groups, and releasing it at the moment of exertion—these were the fundamental logic of Ninja Taijutsu. He had done it many times. From silent movement in the riverbed to Chakra-infused suppression in close-quarters combat against the badger, his practical experience in this area might even exceed that of the Chunin teacher on the podium.

The difficulty wasn’t in the theory. The difficulty was in this afternoon’s practical Taijutsu class.

He had to control himself within a precise range : stronger than the average six-year-old, but not too outrageous. His physical foundation was solid ; his bone density, muscle fibers, and cardiovascular endurance were already approaching the level of a twelve-year-old. These were physical facts accumulated through Life Force Plunder, and he couldn’t just 'turn them off' temporarily. A child with the physique of a twelve-year-old performing Taijutsu among six-year-olds would naturally be faster, steadier, and stronger than them.

He couldn’t unleash all these advantages. But he couldn’t hide them all either. Because a child who clearly had good physical condition acting clumsy on purpose would be even more suspicious. The best strategy was : utilize his physical advantages, but put away all his combat experience. Let Teacher Mitomon see a piece of good raw material, not a seasoned warrior.

Where was the balance point ? His footwork could be steady ; good coordination was innate. His exertion could be sufficient ; good stamina was also innate. But he couldn’t use Chakra adhesion on his soles, couldn’t reveal the muscle memory of close-quarters suppression, and couldn’t show anticipation of attacks. A talented child would instinctively close their eyes or retreat when seeing a fist coming, rather than precisely sidestepping the line of attack and then countering by grabbing the opponent’s wrist.

Shuichi set a standard for himself : his Taijutsu performance should be in the top three to five of the class. He wouldn’t take first place, because first place was too conspicuous. But he wouldn’t fall behind fifth place ; his physical foundation wouldn’t allow him to be that bad. It would be best if a strength-type classmate like Tetsuya took first, while he stayed steady in second or third, winning occasionally.

The afternoon Taijutsu class was held at the Third Training Ground. It was an open-air field covered with fine sand, surrounded by wooden fences, with a few old pine trees swaying in the spring breeze outside. The twenty-three children of Class 1-A lined up in four rows on the sandy field, with Teacher Mitomon standing in front of the formation.

« The first step of Taijutsu training is the standing posture, » Teacher Mitomon said, his hands behind his back. « Feet shoulder-width apart, center of gravity lowered, knees slightly bent. Maintain this posture and stand for a quarter of an hour first. »

A murmur of complaint arose in the line. Standing for a quarter of an hour was torture for a six-year-old child. Shuichi spread his feet, bent his knees slightly, and lowered his center of gravity. His posture was standard, but he restrained the degree of his knee bend slightly to make himself look like someone with good comprehension but who hadn’t fully mastered the technique yet.

Tetsuya stood to Shuichi’s right and started swaying after less than three minutes. His center of gravity kept shifting—now to the left, now to the right—and he couldn’t help but straighten his knees halfway through. Shuichi saw it but didn’t follow suit. His physical foundation could support a standard standing posture for a quarter of an hour—if he swayed even at this, it would seem fake.

But he didn’t stand like a nail either. He adjusted his breathing rhythm to be slightly faster than the standard and let his shoulders relax imperceptibly every few minutes, like a child with good physical fitness whose attention span was still developing.

« Stop. » Teacher Mitomon called a halt after a quarter of an hour. Tetsuya squatted down on the spot, rubbing his knees. Shuichi remained standing, just shaking his legs—his movements were natural, neither rushed nor slow.

Teacher Mitomon walked along the line, pausing in front of each student. When he reached Shuichi, his gaze lingered on Shuichi’s posture for two seconds.

« Your standing posture is very steady, » Teacher Mitomon said. « Have you practiced before ? »

« No, » Shuichi shook his head. « I just... stood steady. »

Teacher Mitomon didn’t pursue the question further, just made a note in his notebook.

« Next is basic footwork. Forward step and backward step. I will demonstrate once. »

Teacher Mitomon’s forward step was clean and crisp : pushing off with his left foot, stepping forward with his right, and shifting his center of gravity in a fraction of a second. Shuichi evaluated in his heart : Chunin standard, the angle of the heel applying force when pushing off was a bit conservative, and the stride was controlled at a medium distance.

« Practice in pairs. Each pair will rotate for five rounds, and I will grade you. »

Tetsuya naturally stood opposite Shuichi. Shuichi didn’t refuse ; being in a pair with Tetsuya was the safest, as Tetsuya wouldn’t look at him with a scrutinizing eye.

Tetsuya attacked first. He performed his forward step with all his might, his left foot pushing off with a sound, and his right foot stepping half a step further than Teacher Mitomon’s demonstration. His whole body rushed forward two steps due to momentum before he barely managed to stop. Teacher Mitomon said from the side, « The stride is too big, control it, » and Tetsuya scratched his head and chuckled.

It was Shuichi’s turn. He took a deep breath, pushed off with his left foot, and stepped forward with his right. He adjusted the angle of the push-off slightly, not aiming for perfection, but the point of exertion was correct. His stride was two inches shorter than Teacher Mitomon’s demonstration, and the shift of his center of gravity was clean but not stunning. The movement was smooth overall, but the smoothness was controlled to the level a 'six-year-old child with athletic talent' should have, not that of a trainee with half a year of actual combat experience.

« Not bad, » Teacher Mitomon nodded, his tone carrying a bit more approval. « The stride and center of gravity are both fine. Tetsuya, you should observe Shuichi’s rhythm more. »

Shuichi lowered his head, not showing any expression. Teacher Mitomon’s praise was within his acceptable range, because his physical foundation was there ; it was normal to do a basic movement like a forward step better than Tetsuya. As long as he didn’t make the teacher think 'this child is abnormal,' it counted as passing.

The backward step practice was the same. When Tetsuya stepped back, his left foot tripped over his right, and he almost fell. When Shuichi stepped back, he landed steadily, his left heel touching the ground first before transitioning to the forefoot, the arc of his center of gravity shifting backward smoothly. Teacher Mitomon made another note.

Shuichi adjusted his strategy in his mind. His backstep had been executed a bit too perfectly. In the next practice session, he needed to show more flaws, perhaps losing control slightly at some minor point of his physical exertion.

For the second half of the class, Teacher Mitomon arranged a simple sparring exercise. Two people would stand face-to-face, each placing one hand on the other’s shoulder while keeping the other hand still. They had to rely on footwork and center of gravity shifts to push the opponent out of position. The winner stayed on the field, the loser stepped down, and anyone with three consecutive wins would swap out to rest.

After the first two groups finished, the winners were the two biggest boys in the class, one named Genji and the other named Oshima. Both were half a head taller than other kids of their age, giving them a natural advantage in strength. Shuichi was in the fourth group, and his opponent was a thin, small boy whose name Shuichi didn’t even remember.

The match began. The small boy placed his hand on Shuichi’s right shoulder with a soft, weak force. Shuichi didn’t rush to push back. He lowered his center of gravity slightly, standing on the sandy ground as if his feet had taken root. The opponent pushed twice, but Shuichi didn’t budge. Then, Shuichi gave a gentle push, using only about thirty percent of his strength, and the boy stumbled back three or four steps before finally stopping.

Teacher Mitomon made a note in his notebook.

For the second match, his opponent was Oshima. Oshima was half a head taller than him, and his palm pressed down on Shuichi’s shoulder with plenty of force. Instead of resisting head-on, Shuichi made a tiny circular motion with his center of gravity. Oshima’s pushing force was guided to the side, and Shuichi’s body swayed by less than two inches. Then, Shuichi pushed back in the opposite direction. Oshima lost his balance, took a step back with his right foot, and stepped out of bounds.

He won. But it wasn’t easy—at least, it didn’t look easy. Shuichi intentionally let his own body sway slightly as Oshima stepped back, making it seem as though he had barely managed to regain his balance at the very last second.

The third match was against Genji, the biggest boy in class, whose palms were as thick as anvils in a blacksmith shop. As soon as Shuichi’s hand touched Genji’s shoulder, he felt the power stored in the boy’s muscles—the absolute limit for a six-year-old child, but still just a six-year-old’s strength. Shuichi could have stood perfectly still and let Genji push, as his physical foundation could easily withstand it. But he didn’t.

He allowed Genji to push him back half a step. Then, he lowered his center of gravity, rooted his feet, and pushed back. Genji’s face turned beet red, his soles grinding two shallow grooves into the sandy ground. After a stalemate of two or three seconds, Shuichi suddenly relaxed his strength by a fraction, reducing his footing grip from one hundred percent to seventy percent. Genji’s push sent him out of bounds.

He lost the third match. Panting, Shuichi nodded to Genji and stepped out of the circle. Several classmates on the sidelines groaned in disappointment, with Tetsuya shouting the loudest, « Shuichi, if you had just held on for one more second, you would have won ! »

Shuichi shook his head, walked to the sidelines, and squatted down to drink water.

At the end of the class, Teacher Mitomon read out the rankings for the first round of the taijutsu class. Genji was first, Shuichi second, Oshima third, and a boy from the Hyuga branch family named Hyuga Shimizu was fourth.

Shuichi noticed the boy with the cloth band wrapped around his forehead. His footwork was much better than his strength, but he was clearly holding back during the matches. Tetsuya was ranked ninth.

« The top five, keep it up, » Teacher Mitomon said, closing his notebook. « The rest of you, don’t be discouraged. Taijutsu can be improved with practice. »

Hyuga Shimizu. Shuichi glanced at the boy again on his way back to the classroom. A child of the Hyuga branch family, whose Byakugan was sealed by the Caged Bird Seal and couldn’t be used freely, yet his taijutsu foundation was clearly much more solid than that of ordinary children, and there were traces of the Gentle Fist in his footwork. Shuichi didn’t go over to talk. An Uchiha and a Hyuga branch family member getting too close wouldn’t benefit either side.

After school, Tetsuya caught up with Shuichi at the school gate.

« Shuichi ! Second place ! Why didn’t you beat Genji too ? You were so close to winning ! »

« I couldn’t push him anymore, » Shuichi said.

« You’ll definitely beat him next time ! I think you’re stronger than Genji ! »

Shuichi glanced at Tetsuya. There was an absolute, pure conviction in Tetsuya’s eyes.

« You should practice your footwork properly, » Shuichi said. « Your strength is sufficient now, but you were pushed out because your stance wasn’t stable. »

« My stance isn’t stable ? »

« During the standing practice, you started swaying after just three minutes. »

Tetsuya froze for a moment, then nodded vigorously. « I get it ! I’ll practice as soon as I get home ! I’ll stand for fifteen minutes in front of the blacksmith shop ! »

Shuichi nodded and turned to walk north. Tetsuya waved behind him, his running footsteps thudding loudly on the stone-paved road.

---

It was still early when he returned to the Uchiha Clan Grounds. Shuichi walked along the path by the Naka River and paused on a stone bridge. The stream reflected his face—pitch-black pupils, a calm expression, just an ordinary Uchiha child heading home after school.

Then, he realized something.

During the taijutsu class, when his shoulder was pressed by Genji’s palm, his eyes had automatically captured the moment Genji’s center of gravity shifted. It wasn’t a guess, nor was it based on experience. Even without activating his Sharingan, he had precisely captured the fraction-of-a-second adjustment in Genji’s lower back muscles right before he exerted force. Then, his body had automatically determined the optimal timing to push back.

This was the passive effect of the one-tomoe. Even when the Sharingan wasn’t active, its ocular power was still operating. The concept of Growth used ocular power as nourishment, while the ocular power itself worked in reverse to enhance his eyes' basic sensory abilities : dynamic capture, timing judgment, and movement anticipation. It wasn’t the overwhelming clarity of all information rushing in at once when active, but rather a more low-profile, quiet, and fundamental upgrade. It couldn’t be seen by ordinary eyes, nor could it be detected by instruments.

Shuichi straightened up from the stone bridge and continued walking home. It was only when Lue flew down from a plum tree and landed on his shoulder that he realized he had already reached the courtyard gate.

« No hunting today, » Shuichi said, stroking Lue’s breast feathers. « Tomorrow. »

Lue tilted its head to look at him, let out a short, low chirp, and then flew back to the plum branch.

Shuichi walked over to sit on the porch, lifting the hem of his clothes to check the bruise on his left ribs. The yellow area around the edges had expanded, and the deep purple in the center had faded to a light gray. The healing speed was faster than expected. The physical foundation accumulated from Life Force Plunder also provided a boost in repairing soft tissue damage. It would probably fade completely by the day after tomorrow.

Minako poked her head out of the kitchen. « How was school today ? »

« Taijutsu class. I got second place. »

« Second place ? » Minako was taken aback for a moment, and then a smile spread across her lips—a smile that shone from her eyes. She wiped her hands on her apron, walked out of the kitchen, and squatted down in front of Shuichi.

« Second in the whole class ? »

« Yes. »

« Better than that Oshima boy ? »

« Oshima was third. »

Minako cupped Shuichi’s face with both hands, her thumb gently wiping away a small smudge of dirt on his cheek from the Training Ground. She looked closely into his eyes, as if trying to confirm something. Then she stood up and turned back to the kitchen, her movements a beat faster than before.

« We’re adding a grilled fish tonight, » her voice drifted from the kitchen, carrying a lightheartedness that Shuichi hadn’t heard in a long time. « No, wait, let’s add two. »

At dinner, there really were two extra grilled fish on the table. Tofu had been added to the miso soup, and a small dish of pickled radish sat next to it. Shuichi looked at the dishes on the table, then at Minako. She sat opposite him, scooped a bowl of rice for herself, and then stared at the two grilled fish in front of Shuichi, as if she suddenly felt two was a bit too much but felt too embarrassed to take one back.

« If you can’t finish them, you can leave one for tomorrow, » she said.

« I can finish them. »

Shuichi lowered his head and picked up a grilled fish. The skin was grilled to a fragrant crisp, and the saltiness was just right. He took a few bites, then looked up to see Minako watching him, her own rice barely touched. Her gaze was different from her silence this morning when she saw the scab on his hand ; there was no suppressed worry now, only the pure, simple contentment of a mother seeing her son achieve good results.

Shuichi suddenly realized that this might be the first time in over half a year that Minako could openly and rightfully be happy for him. Those bruises, bloodstains, and torn pant legs made her realize he was doing dangerous things, but she couldn’t ask about any of it. But second place in taijutsu class was something within the boundaries. It was school, normal schoolwork, and something any mother could be proud of her son for, with perfect justification.

« I’ll strive for first place next time, » Shuichi said, then lowered his head to continue eating.

Minako let out a soft laugh and finally picked up her bowl. « Second place is very good. You don’t have to push yourself to the front every single time. »

After dinner, Shuichi sat on the porch, listening to the sound of Minako washing dishes in the kitchen. The sound of the water was slightly louder than usual. She was humming a song, her voice very soft and the tune intermittent, as if she didn’t even realize she was humming.

Shuichi spread open his right palm and looked down at his hand. Today in the taijutsu class, these hands had used less than thirty percent of their strength. Tomorrow, they would return to the edge of the Forest of Death, fighting with all their might to Plunder once more.
