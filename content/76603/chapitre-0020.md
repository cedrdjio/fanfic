# Chapitre 20 — Wild Boar

Saturday morning, Shuichi was checking his equipment under the plum tree.

Calling it equipment, it was actually only two items : an old Kunai left by his father tied to the outside of his right calf, the blade edge had been sharpened many times but was well-maintained ; three shuriken in the ninja tool pouch on his left waist.

The ninja tools were uniformly issued when entering the Ninja Academy, every student had them, the quality was average.

For a six-year-old child, they were teaching tools, but for Shuichi, they were weapons.

He did two sets of stretches under the plum tree, confirming that the bruise on his left rib no longer had pulling pain during movement.

The edges were still turning yellow yesterday, but today it had faded to a light brown, and only a little faint soreness remained when pressed.

The healing speed was indeed much faster than an ordinary person.

Lue flew down from the plum branch and landed on his shoulder.

Its pitch-black pupils shone with a very faint glimmer in the morning light. After overlaying low-light vision and auditory localization, Lue’s pupils were sharper than ordinary hawks even during the day.

Shuichi stroked Lue’s chest feathers.

« Going to the Forest of Death today. A bit deeper inside than last time. »

Lue tilted its head to look at him and let out a short, low chirp.

Minako stood on the corridor, holding a cup of warm water in her hand.

She watched Shuichi tie the Kunai and buckle the shuriken pouch to his waist, her gaze pausing on those weapons for a moment.

The ninja tool pouch was issued by the school, but a six-year-old child going out fully armed on a Saturday morning didn’t look like he was going to practice.

« Are you coming back for lunch ? » she asked.

« Not necessarily. » Shuichi tied his shoelaces and stood up, « I’ll be back before the sun sets. »

Minako placed the water cup on the corridor and turned into the kitchen.

After a moment, she took out a rice ball wrapped in cloth and handed it over.

« Take this. »

Shuichi took the rice ball, put it into his bosom, nodded at Minako, and turned to walk out of the courtyard.

The entrance to the Forest of Death was still the same, that old oak tree that required three people to hug, that fallen log covered in moss.

But Shuichi did not stop at the entrance.

He crossed the fallen log, passed through the first layer of oak forest, and walked straight to the patch of rotting leaf ground where he fought the badger last time.

The badger’s corpse was still under the fallen log, covered well by the rotting leaves, with no signs of being disturbed by other animals.

Shuichi did not stop.

He continued walking in the direction of north by west.

Lue glided between the gaps in the canopy above his head.

The canopy of the Forest of Death was too dense, Lue could not circle at high altitude — it could only perform short-distance gliding between the middle-layer branches, leaping from one branch to another.

But low-light vision allowed it to see the details of the ground clearly even in the dim forest, and auditory localization allowed it to accurately lock onto the sound source without turning its head.

Shuichi walked through the forest for about half an hour.

The further he went, the denser the canopy, the darker the light on the ground, and the smell of damp mud in the air became stronger and stronger, mixed with a deeper fishy smell.

Not the fishy smell of rotting meat — it was the smell left by living large animals.

Lue stopped on the middle-layer branch of a crooked oak tree and let out a short, low chirp.

It wasn’t an alarm — it was an indication.

It had found something.

Shuichi lowered his body, Chakra attached to the soles of his feet, and silently passed through the last row of oak trees.

The scene in front of him made him stop.

A patch of pine soil that had been rooted up by a wild boar.

The area was about the size of two tatami mats, the layer of rotting leaves had been turned upside down, revealing the dark humus soil and broken tree roots underneath.

The mud was mixed with wild boar hoof prints, each hoof print was bigger than Shuichi’s fist, and the depth into the soil was nearly two inches.

The trunks of several surrounding oak trees had traces of being grazed by tusks, the bark was ripped open, revealing the light-colored xylem inside.

Shuichi squatted down and pressed his right hand next to a hoof print.

The mud at the edge of the hoof print was still wet.

It had been less than a day.

An adult wild boar.

Based on the depth and spacing of the hoof prints, it weighed at least one hundred and fifty jin.

According to the hunting classification table he summarized last time, this belonged to a level five prey.

A whole level higher than the badger.

Last summer, he told himself that level five prey was not recommended for now.

At that time, his physical constitution was not yet nine years old, he had no Chakra, no Sharingan.

Now it was different.

Shuichi walked forward along the direction of the hoof prints.

The wild boar’s activity route was easy to track, those rotted leaf layers that were arched open, the shrub branches that were stepped on and broken, the tree bark fragments that were rubbed off were all pointing the way for Shuichi.

Lue moved silently between the branches in front of him, and every landing position was just within the range of Shuichi’s line of sight.

The hoof prints turned around an old oak tree struck by lightning and entered a denser bush.

Shuichi squatted down again at the edge of the bush, because the hoof prints suddenly became deeper here, the depth into the soil changed from two inches to nearly three inches.

The wild boar had stayed here, and the hooves had ground a circle on the ground.

Then the hoof prints turned left into the depths of the bushes, and the form changed from walking prints to running prints.

This meant the wild boar had discovered something and chased after it.

Shuichi followed the traces of the chase through the bushes.

There were more and more branches that were knocked and broken, and several branches as thick as a thumb were broken off at the root.

Then the hoof prints turned sharply next to a fallen dead tree, rushed forward for another ten or so steps,

And then stopped.

A trace of a fierce struggle appeared on the pine soil ground.

The rotting leaves were dug out into a shallow pit, and gray-brown animal fur was scattered around.

It wasn’t the wild boar’s, these hairs were finer and softer, and there were many of them, scattered around the shallow pit.

There were dry, dark bloodstains in the mud.

Shuichi squatted next to the bloodstains and looked around at the hoof prints.

The wild boar had killed something here, looking at the distribution range of the soft fur and bloodstains, it was probably another badger or a small deer.

Then it dragged the corpse away.

The dragging traces extended to the north, leaving a curved shallow ditch on the pine soil.

He followed the dragging traces for another quarter of an hour.

The canopy became denser, the light became darker, and the fishy smell in the air became stronger.

Lue suddenly stopped on the branch above his head, and its feathers exploded slightly for a moment.

Shuichi stopped.

The dragging traces were covered by denser hoof prints here.

And they weren’t the hoof prints of one wild boar.

Large and small hoof prints had stepped out a groove on the pine soil that had been passed over repeatedly.

The groove was connected to a low dirt slope, and there was a dark hole dug out under the dirt slope.

The rotting leaves around the cave entrance were trampled very solidly, mixed with the wild boar’s feces and mud.

A boar den.

Shuichi slowly retreated behind an old oak tree, pressed his right hand on the trunk, and his heartbeat quickened by a beat.

One wild boar could be fought, but more than three was absolutely impossible.

He had a one-tomoe Sharingan, had Chakra, and had more than half a year of close-quarters actual combat experience, but adding all these things up, he still couldn’t beat three adult wild boars.

Any one of them weighed at least three times as much as him, and the collision force could knock an adult flying.

He squatted behind the oak tree and lowered his breathing.

Lue stopped on the branch directly above him, its claws slightly tightening and loosening on the tree bark.

Then a low grunt came from the boar den.

A wild boar walked out of the dark hole.

Shuichi saw it clearly, the shoulder height was over his waist, the body length was close to 1.5 meters, and the whole body was covered with coarse, hard black bristles.

The bristles at the shoulder blades were exceptionally long, like a row of upright black spikes.

On both sides of the snout, there was an upward-curving tusk, and the tips of the teeth shone with a pale yellow luster in the weak light.

An adult boar.

At least over one hundred and sixty jin.

Shuichi’s right hand touched the Kunai on the outside of his right calf.

A few lighter, sharper grunts came from the boar den.

These were piglets.

The sow might still be in the cave with the cubs, and this boar was out patrolling.

Shuichi lowered his body and moved slowly along the shadow of the oak tree.

He didn’t attack the boar immediately because the current position was not suitable, it was too close to the boar den, and once the war started, the sow would join the battle with the threat of the cubs.

He needed to lure the boar far enough away from the den.

The boar stood on the pine soil outside the cave entrance for a while, its snout pressed against the ground and rooted back and forth a few times, then walked towards the south.

Shuichi followed behind it, maintaining a distance of about fifty steps.

Chakra was attached to the soles of his feet, and every step was silent.

Lue glided and followed silently between the branches above his head.

After walking for about two or three hundred steps, the boar stopped next to a fallen log and began to use its nose to root at the rotten tree bark.

The area around the fallen log was a small, relatively open ground, enough to fight.

Shuichi pulled out the Kunai.

He did not force an approach.

Fighting a wild boar, sneak attacks were useless, because the boar’s skin thickness and fat layer were enough to negate the thrust of a Kunai.

His plan was to fight head-on.

Using the Sharingan to predict the boar’s charging trajectory, infusing Chakra into his legs for high-speed changes in direction, and launching progressive attacks in the opening during its turn after each charge.

Fighting a war of attrition, wearing the boar down until it was exhausted.

He walked into the clearing and stood at the other end of the fallen log.

The boar stopped rooting at the tree bark, lifted its head, and its two small eyes locked onto him in the dim forest light.

The boar did not charge over immediately like the badger had.

It stood in place, shoulders lowered, forehooves pawing the ground twice, and a low vibration emitted from its throat ; this was completely different from the sharp threat of the badger—the boar’s low growl was like the slight cracking sound before a large rock rolls down a hillside.

Shuichi injected Chakra into his legs.

At the same time, he injected another stream of Chakra along his optic nerves into his eyes, his irises turning crimson in an instant, and black tomoe simultaneously emerged at the edges of his pupils.

The boar charged over.

At a distance of less than twenty paces, a boar one and a half meters long and weighing over one hundred and fifty jin, charging at full speed—in his Sharingan vision, Shuichi saw a scene he had never witnessed before.

The muscle bundles under the boar’s shoulder blades tightened and snapped open like steel cables, the sequence of force applied by its four legs at different time points was clearly discernible, and the trajectory of its tusks through the air was like a drawn arc.

All this information flooded into his brain in the same instant.

Shuichi pushed off the ground with his right foot, his body shifting horizontally to the right for nearly four paces.

The boar rushed past his left side, the highest point of its tusks only a palm’s width away from his ribs.

The rotting leaves kicked up by the pig’s hooves splattered onto his trouser legs.

The boar ran out more than ten paces before stopping.

Shuichi had already run towards it before it could turn around.

The moment the boar turned, Shuichi had already closed in on its side.

His right hand thrust the Kunai towards the area below the boar’s shoulder blades, which was the area with the thickest muscle layer but also the densest distribution of nerves.

The Kunai pierced in less than two inches, the boar let out a sharp squeal, its body turned violently, and its tusks swept across.

Shuichi’s Sharingan had already seen the muscular premonition before it turned.

Shuichi had already been retreating after the Kunai pierced in.

The tusks swept past two inches in front of his chest, only tearing the surface fibers of his clothes.

This level of sensory precision was on a completely different level than when fighting the badger.

He needed experience and judgment to predict the badger’s attacks, but he saw the boar’s attacks directly ; his eyes would tell his brain « it will strike here in the next second, » and then his body would automatically dodge in that direction.

The boar turned around, its small eyes full of bloodshot veins.

Having been stabbed once in the first head-on clash, it began to treat Shuichi as a real threat.

The second round of charging.

The boar’s speed was a little slower than the first time ; although the blood loss wasn’t much, it had already begun to affect its explosive power.

Shuichi ran three steps towards the direction of the charge, and just before the boar lowered its head to prepare to gore with its tusks, he took a large diagonal step to the front left, spinning his whole body around the outside of the boar’s tusks.

His right hand thrust the Kunai into the back of the boar’s ear from the side.

The boar let out a squeal completely different from before, sharper, longer, and carrying panic beyond just pain.

It veered violently, its tusks slamming into the nearby fallen log.

A large piece of rotten wood was ripped off by the tusks, and wood chips splattered onto Shuichi’s face.

Shuichi did not blink.

In the Sharingan’s vision, the process of the boar’s loss of balance after hitting the log was broken down into several clear fragments, and the interval between each fragment was enough for him to make the next action decision.

He pulled out the Kunai.

The boar struggled to turn around from beside the log, its four legs slipping on the rotting leaves, and the wound on its neck was gushing blood.

Its next charge came fast and chaotic, no longer a straight-line sprint, but charging while tilting its head and sweeping its tusks wildly.

Shuichi did not take it head-on.

He retreated three steps in a row, each step landing outside the range of the tusk sweep predicted by the Sharingan.

The boar chased him and swept four times in a row, all missing.

Shuichi stepped forward after the fourth sweep finished ; the boar’s muscles already showed clear signs of fatigue.

He backhanded the Kunai into the boar’s throat, the tip piercing through the soft tissue under the chin, puncturing the trachea and the main blood vessels.

The boar’s body stiffened for a moment.

Then its four hooves pushed forward simultaneously, and its entire body toppled onto the loose soil on its side.

Its four legs were still twitching, and a gurgling sound of blood bubbles came from its throat ; it did not die immediately.

Shuichi pressed one knee onto its side, his right hand resting on its chest, which was still heaving weakly.

Plunder.

The suction force unfolded steadily.

The boar’s Vitality was extracted from its bones, muscles, fat layer, and internal organs, flowing into Shuichi’s body along the channel in his palm.

This time there was no struggle at all, and his mental energy was used entirely for the Plunder itself.

It consumed about one-sixth of his mental energy.

A warm torrent spread from his palm to his whole body.

Deep in his bones, there was a humming sound, muscle fibers contracted and relaxed at an extremely minute level, and the pumping rhythm of his heart and lungs accelerated by a beat unconsciously.

Shuichi knelt on one knee beside the boar’s carcass, closed his eyes, and let the Vitality complete the first stage of absorption.

It was not because he was tired, but because the density of the incoming Vitality was too high, and he needed to actively adjust his breathing to match his blood circulation.

About the time it takes to drink a cup of tea, the absorption tended to stabilize.

Shuichi opened his eyes, and the crimson in his vision had already faded.

He looked down at the boar’s carcass.

An adult male boar weighing over one hundred and fifty jin, defeated in less than a quarter of an hour of head-on combat.

Stabbed twice in the shoulder, once behind the ear, and once in the throat.

And the only injury on his own body was the surface of the clothes on his chest torn by the tusks.

It hadn’t even broken the skin.

The Sharingan allowed him to see the attack route clearly before every attack of the boar.

When fighting the badger, he used Taijutsu experience and on-the-spot judgment, and after the fight, his left ribs were covered in a large bruise from the badger’s kick.

When fighting the boar, his body was already not in that position before the tusks touched him.

The time lag between prediction and reaction was compressed to almost zero by the Sharingan.

Shuichi sat down on the fallen log and took out the rice ball packed by Minako from his bosom.

He finished the rice ball bite by bite, folded the cloth, and tucked it back into his bosom.

Then he stood up, grabbed the boar’s two hind hooves, and dragged it from the clearing to behind the fallen log.

Covering it well with rotting leaves.

On the way back, Shuichi updated his hunting classification in his mind.

He could now kill a level five prey, a wild boar, head-on.

But the pattern of mental energy consumption was also worth noting.

The Sharingan activation state lasted for about a quarter of an hour ; from opening his eyes to the end of the battle, his mental energy was continuously extracted during this process.

It was not the one-time large consumption like when Plundering, but a continuous, small loss.

After the battle ended, the eye-opening state automatically closed, his ocular power returned to dormancy, and his remaining mental energy was around three-fifths.

If the battle time were extended to more than half an hour, the continuous consumption of mental energy could become a problem.

When he returned to the Uchiha Clan Grounds, the sun had just begun to slant westward.

Shuichi washed his hands and face by the stream, wiped the Kunai clean, and tied it back to his lower leg.

The tear on his chest clothes caused by the tusks was not very noticeable.

Minako was in the yard collecting the sun-dried bedding.

Hearing footsteps, she turned her head, her gaze sweeping over him.

Her shoulders relaxed unconsciously.

« Have you eaten ? »

« I have. A rice ball. »

« There is still miso soup in the pot. »

Shuichi walked into the kitchen, ladled himself a bowl of miso soup, and sat on the veranda to drink it slowly.

Lue flew down from the plum branch and landed on the railing next to him, folded its wings, and buried its head in its chest feathers.

Shuichi watched the clouds on the horizon beginning to turn red, the residual warmth from the Vitality flowing in still lingering on his right palm.

The boar’s Vitality was slowly permeating into the deepest parts of his bones and muscles within his body.

The amount this time was too large ; the absorption process might last for four or five days.

When the absorption was complete, his physical foundation should be able to advance a small step further from the twelve-year-old level.

Shuichi finished the last mouthful of miso soup and placed the bowl on the railing.
