# Chapitre 22 — Caged Bird Seal

On Monday morning, after waking up, Shuichi discovered that the Vitality of the boar had been fully absorbed.

While brushing his teeth, he suddenly realized that the humming warmth deep within his bones was gone. He put down his toothbrush and clenched his right hand. His grip strength had improved slightly. He took a deep breath in front of the mirror ; his lung capacity seemed to have increased a fraction more than yesterday. His physique had advanced another small step forward.

He did two sets of push-ups under the plum tree, the rhythm a beat faster than last week. The bruise on his left rib had completely faded, leaving only a faint, light yellow mark on his skin that would be impossible to notice without a close look.

During breakfast, Minako didn’t look at him much. Ever since he placed second in the Taijutsu class, there was something new in the way she looked at him every morning, as if she had found a temporary balance between worry and relief. As Shuichi was leaving, she was wiping bowls in the hallway, looked up, and said, « Take it slow on the road. »

Ninja Academy was a bit quieter than usual on Monday. The wild running around over the weekend left most children in a state of mixed exhaustion and excitement on Monday morning, and Tetsuya was a classic example. He was slumped over his desk, his eyelids fighting each other, yet he was still muttering the essentials of the standing posture.

« Even weight on the soles of the feet... shift center of gravity forward... knees slightly bent... »

« You’ve been reciting that all morning. » Shuichi flipped his Chakra textbook to the third chapter.

« I’m memorizing ! Isn’t it said that Taijutsu relies on muscle memory ? I’m memorizing it with my mouth first, then I’ll let my body memorize it. »

Shuichi didn’t correct his logic. Tetsuya’s standing posture was indeed improving. Two days of practice had shown quick results in a six-year-old.

The first class was a review of Chakra refinement. Teacher Mitomon drew a set of meridian diagrams on the blackboard and had each student take turns coming to the stage to mark the conduction path of Chakra from the Dantian to the palm. Most children could only draw up to the wrist ; the major meridian from the Dantian to the wrist was content from the basic textbook. When Shuichi went up, he drew it all the way to the fingertips and was praised by Teacher Mitomon : « Very thorough preparation. »

Hyuga Shimizu was the second to go up. The path he drew was exactly the same as Shuichi’s, from Dantian to fingertips, not a single one of the five branch meridians wrong. As he put down the chalk and walked back to his seat, their gazes met in the middle of the classroom. Less than half a second later, they both looked away.

Shuichi made a mental note : the child of the Hyuga Branch House had a foundation in Chakra theory far exceeding the average student. Gentle Fist itself was a Taijutsu style centered on attacking Chakra meridians, and people of the Hyuga Clan had to learn the locations of the body’s meridian points from a young age.

After class was Shuriken throwing training. The Third Training Ground had new targets ; scarecrow targets were tied to six wooden stakes, and a palm-sized circle was painted in red lacquer on the center of the target. Teacher Mitomon gave everyone five wooden Shuriken ; six-year-old children used wooden products, which were unsharpened and weighed only half as much as real Shuriken.

Tetsuya’s hands were too big. The wooden Shuriken in his palm was like a pinched piece of bread crust ; the axis of rotation was off by a ridiculous amount when he threw it. Of the five, only two hit the scarecrow, and both were outside the bullseye. Teacher Mitomon watched his throwing gesture from the side and reminded him : « Don’t disperse your finger strength too much. » Tetsuya nodded, and the next one was off-target again.

The feel of the wooden Shuriken wasn’t right for Shuichi either. The wooden product was too light, and the rotational inertia was completely different from a steel Shuriken. He test-threw the first one, hitting the upper right corner of the bullseye. The second hit to the left of the bullseye. The third hit the bullseye dead center. The fourth was off to the upper right. The fifth was dead center.

Two out of five hit the bullseye, and three hit the target but missed the bullseye. Teacher Mitomon wrote something on the clipboard. When Shuichi walked over to retrieve his Shuriken, his peripheral vision swept across Hyuga Shimizu’s target. Only two Shuriken hit the bullseye on his scarecrow, while the other three were evenly distributed around it. It was too even. It wasn’t randomly off ; he had deliberately created a dispersed pattern.

His control precision was higher than Shuichi had expected. To create a stable distribution pattern, it didn’t require luck, but precise mastery of throwing force. As Shuichi passed behind him to pick up his Shuriken, he saw the edge of a small cloth band exposed on the back of Hyuga Shimizu’s neck.

The Caged Bird Seal was right under that cloth band. A seal that could never be broken, locking away the Byakugan and also locking away his life’s potential as a member of the Hyuga Branch House. He hid his strength at school, perhaps for a different reason than Shuichi ; Shuichi did it to avoid exposing the source of his abilities, while Hyuga Shimizu’s reason might be that it simply wasn’t safe for a Branch House child to perform too well.

Shuichi didn’t look any longer. He picked up the wooden Shuriken from the ground and walked back to his target position.

During the lunch break, Tetsuya pulled Shuichi to a corner of the playground to eat his bento. Tetsuya’s bento was brought by himself—rice balls warmed by the residual heat of the charcoal used to roast sweet potatoes at the blacksmith shop, wrapped in a layer of charred seaweed. Shuichi was eating the plum rice ball that Minako had stuffed into his school bag that morning.

« I hit one out of five shuriken today. » Tetsuya said with his mouth full of rice balls, his voice muffled. « Teacher Mitomon said it would be fine if I practiced more. How long do you think I need to practice ? »

« Depends on how much you practice. »

« Then I’ll practice every day ! When I get home from school, I’ll stand in the standing posture for a quarter of an hour, then throw shuriken. My dad said the persimmon tree in the backyard can be used as a target, but the persimmons haven’t grown yet, so there are only branches now. »

Shuichi took a bite of the plum rice ball. Tetsuya’s desire for improvement was much stronger than he had expected. This child had been forging iron at the blacksmith shop for four years and never felt he had talent, so once someone told him how to become stronger, he would practice in the way he forged iron. Constantly repeating, trading quantity for quality. This kind of character would sooner or later show results in Taijutsu.

« Don’t practice too many shuriken. » Shuichi said, « Twenty a day is enough. More than twenty and your fingers will fatigue, your movements will deform, and you’ll just be practicing the wrong things. »

« Twenty ! Got it ! »

On the other side of the playground, Hyuga Shimizu sat alone under a cedar tree eating his bento. There were no other children within five steps of him. It wasn’t that anyone was deliberately isolating him—the children in Class 1-A didn’t yet understand things like Branch House and Main House—it was just that Hyuga Shimizu had chosen that position himself. Back against the tree trunk, facing the playground, his line of sight covered all directions.

Shuichi withdrew his gaze and stuffed the last bite of the rice ball into his mouth.

When school ended in the afternoon, Teacher Mitomon posted a notice at the classroom door. Next Friday, the first field practice class for the first grade would be an actual field drill. The destination was the outer edge of the Training Ground upstream of the Naka River, and the content was « Basic Field Survival and Direction Recognition. » Grouping method : four people per group, decided by drawing lots.

The classroom exploded. Six-year-old children hearing the words « field practice » reacted much like they would hearing « field trip. » Tetsuya was already standing up and shouting, « I want to be in a group with Shuichi ! » A few boys nearby joined in the commotion.

« Decided by drawing lots. » Teacher Mitomon repeated, then added, « There must be at least one girl in each group. »

Tetsuya shouted again, « Then we’ll draw lots, » but his voice was clearly a notch quieter than before. Shuichi looked at the grouping rules on the notice and calculated in his heart—four people per group, twenty-three students in the whole class, divided into about six groups. Drawing lots was uncontrollable, but it wasn’t a bad thing : it didn’t matter who he was assigned to ; the field practice was on the outer edge of the Training Ground, which was about the same as a walk for him. But when in a group with people he wasn’t familiar with, he needed to be more careful about hiding.

Hyuga Shimizu stood in front of the notice for a while, then turned and walked out of the classroom. Shuichi noticed his footsteps pause for a moment ; he had been stopped at the door by two older Hyuga children. Those two children also had cloth bands tied around their foreheads, also members of the Branch House. One of them whispered something to Hyuga Shimizu, who nodded and followed them. Looking from afar, the backs of the three Hyuga Branch House children walking side by side shrank into three silent gray-blue blobs at the end of the corridor.

Tetsuya tapped Shuichi on the shoulder from behind. « What are you looking at ? »

« Nothing. »

When Shuichi returned to the Uchiha Clan Grounds, Obito was already sitting at his courtyard gate.

Sitting with his back against the wooden pillar of the courtyard gate, knees curled up to his chin, the wooden forehead protector pushed up to his forehead. He was asleep.

Shuichi squatted down in front of him. Obito’s breathing was light, his eyelashes trembling slightly under his eyelids. There was a fresh red scratch mark on his left cheek. A small piece of fabric was missing from the front of his clothes, the torn spot near the left chest pocket.

Shuichi reached out and pressed Obito’s shoulder. « Wake up. »

Obito jolted, opened his eyes, and took half a second to realize who was in front of him. « Big Brother Shuichi ! I’m sorry, I fell asleep. »

« Come in. »

Obito followed Shuichi into the courtyard and walked to the spot where he had practiced the standing posture yesterday to stand ready. Shuichi glanced at him and found that his standing posture was already better than yesterday ; the foot distance was correct, the degree of knee bend was correct, and the center of gravity was more accurate than Tetsuya’s. After getting beaten up, he had run to his door to wait for his return, and then practiced yesterday’s content on his own first.

« We’re not practicing the standing posture today. » Shuichi said.

Obito was stunned for a moment.

« Yesterday’s standing posture passed. Today we learn the forward step. »

Shuichi stood in front of Obito, pushed off the ground with his left foot, and stepped forward with his right, at the slowest speed, breaking it down into two steps : the angle of the push-off and the timing of the center of gravity shift. Obito stared at his feet three times, then tried it himself. The first time, his stride was too big and he almost fell. The second time, the push-off was too light, and he stepped less than half a step. The third time, the stride was correct, but his center of gravity was left behind.

« The force of the push-off isn’t forward, it’s diagonally downward. The force goes down, the body goes up, not rushing forward. »

Obito tried the fourth time. This time the center of gravity followed ; he took a deep breath before stepping, his entire upper body tensed up unconsciously, and after tightening his core, the center of gravity naturally followed his feet. It wasn’t the standard way, but the result was correct.

Shuichi didn’t correct his breathing method. For a five-year-old child learning Taijutsu, learning how to get the body moving was more important than standardizing the posture.

« Fifth time. »

Obito practiced for ten more rounds. Every round was an improvement—Shuichi could tell. This child’s physical learning ability was stronger than he himself realized. Tetsuya’s progress was piled up by repeating quantity ; Obito’s was based on physical perception. Their directions of talent were completely different.

« You can stop. That’s enough for today. »

Obito didn’t stop. He did two more rounds, then bent over, supporting his knees, panting heavily. The wooden forehead protector on his forehead slid down to his eyebrows ; he pushed it back to the top of his head and looked up at Shuichi.

« Big Brother Shuichi, does your school teach shuriken ? »

« Yes. »

« Is it hard ? »

Shuichi took out the practice wooden shuriken issued by the school from his ninja tool pouch and placed it in Obito’s palm. Obito’s small hand couldn’t hold the whole shuriken ; his four fingers barely hooked onto three of the points.

« So light. »

« It’s wood. A real shuriken is twice as heavy. »

Obito turned the wooden shuriken over and over a few times, then returned it to Shuichi. « When I enroll, I’m going to practice shuriken too. Taijutsu plus shuriken, then next time they come at me four at a time, I won’t be afraid. »

Shuichi took the shuriken and put it back into his ninja tool pouch. When Obito said « next time they come at me four at a time, » his tone was very calm ; he truly believed he could beat four people next time. Because he learned standing posture and footwork today, he felt that if he learned eight more things, he could beat four people. A five-year-old’s logic, but that drive was real.

« What’s with that mark on your face ? »

Obito touched the scratch on his left cheek. « They scratched it. He was very angry because I pushed him down. He said I had no right to shout about wanting to be Hokage in the Clan Grounds. »

« And then ? »

« I said I can be Hokage. Then he scratched me. »

Shuichi was silent for a few seconds. When Obito said these things, there was no grievance or anger in his eyes, only a very straightforward certainty. Surrounded by four people, one against four, pushed down and scolded for not being qualified, climbing up with scratches on his face, and running to Shuichi’s door to wait to continue learning. He was a laughingstock in the eyes of other Uchiha children, but he never felt that way himself.

« Obito. »

« Hmm ? »

« Being Hokage isn’t about shouting. »

Obito stared at Shuichi for a while. Then he did an action Shuichi didn’t expect : he took the forehead protector off his forehead, carefully smoothed out the edges of the cloth that had curled from sweat, and tied it back onto his head. Tighter and straighter than before.

« I know, » he said, « that’s why I have to learn. »

Lue flew down from the plum branch and landed on Shuichi’s shoulder. Obito looked up at Lue’s spread wings, pure curiosity lighting up his eyes.

« Big Brother Shuichi, what is your hawk’s name ? »

« Lue. »

« Lue. » Obito repeated, « When I become Hokage, I want to keep a hawk too. »

Shuichi walked to the hallway and sat down, resting his right palm face up on his knee. Obito stood in the courtyard for a while, then returned to the spot where he practiced footwork and did two more forward steps. This time, the stride was controlled just right, and when he landed, his right foot ground a small pit into the ground.
