# Chapitre 7 — Heading North

Early the next morning, Shuichi woke up before the rooster crowed.

He put on his clothes quietly, took two leftover rice balls from last night from the kitchen, wrapped them in cloth, and stuffed them into his shirt. Then, he softly called out under the plum tree in the backyard. Lue glided silently down from the plum tree branch and landed on his shoulder. A week ago, it could only clumsily grab onto Shuichi’s collar to keep its balance, but now its claws could firmly grip his shoulder, with just the right amount of force—not so light that it would slip, nor so heavy that it would scratch.

« Let’s go. »

Shuichi pushed open the low gate of the backyard and walked into the final stretch of darkness before dawn.

The Uchiha Clan Grounds were still asleep. Morning mist drifted over from the Naka River, carrying the cold scent of river water, covering the rows of tiled roofs. In the distance, the stone faces of the three Hokage on the Hokage Rock were barely visible in the hazy morning light. Shuichi walked quickly along the path on the north side of the clan grounds, his wooden sandals making faint sounds on the gravel road. He didn’t take the main road ; the main road passed by the Military Police Force duty station. At this hour, the night shift workers were about to change shifts, and any questioning would disrupt his plans.

He took the small path along the Naka River. The end of this road was the Training Ground, which Minako had shown him last time. But today, he wouldn’t stop at the Training Ground ; instead, he would continue north, pass through the shrub slope on the west side of the Training Ground, and enter the wooded area at the foot of the mountain where Lue had discovered the rabbit tracks.

The sound of the Naka River was sometimes near and sometimes far on his right. Shuichi’s pace was steady. After plundering the Vitality of over forty insects and six tree lizards, his physical fitness was no longer at the level of an ordinary five-year-old child. He had walked for a quarter of an hour, yet his breathing remained steady, and his legs were not sore. This was the most direct benefit brought by the improvement of his physical foundation—not the enhancement of any specific ability, but a thickening of his entire body’s « foundation. »

The outline of the Training Ground emerged in the morning mist. The target stakes were still standing there, the large rocks by the river were still smooth, and the surface of the Naka River reflected the fish-belly white beginning to appear in the eastern sky. Shuichi did not enter the Training Ground but detoured around its west side. To the west of the Training Ground was a low shrub slope—it was called a slope, but in reality, the terrain just began to rise slightly ; the bushes gradually became denser, and the soil underfoot changed from hard, trodden earth to soft leaf mold.

Lue flew up from his shoulder. It circled half a turn above the Training Ground and then flew north, its figure appearing and disappearing in the morning mist. Shuichi followed in the direction of Lue, pushing aside the increasingly dense shrub branches, and began to climb the slope.

The slope of the shrubbery wasn’t steep, but the bushes were denser than he had imagined. The Climbing Ability of the Tree Frog had already been digested into a part of him, but the Climbing Ability couldn’t help him travel through the bushes because these were two different ways of moving. Shuichi used both hands to push aside the branches blocking his way, crouching down to squeeze through the gaps in the thickets, and his clothes were soon covered in broken leaves and burrs.

About ten minutes later, the shrub slope came to an end. The terrain leveled off here, and the bushes were replaced by a sparse pine forest. The ground between the pine trees was covered with thick pine needles, feeling soft underfoot, and the air was filled with the scent of pine resin. Shuichi stopped and looked up at the sky.

Lue had already flown above the pine forest and was circling high in the sky. Its circling radius was much larger than it had been at the Training Ground, which meant it had found prey worth paying attention to and was locking onto its position.

Shuichi quickened his pace through the pine forest. At the end of the pine forest was a small open space, with short grass and sporadic wildflowers growing on the ground. On the right was a shallow ditch, and at the bottom of the ditch was a tiny stream, which must have been a small tributary of the Naka River.

Lue tucked its wings in mid-air, performed a short dive, circled twice at a low altitude, and landed on a middle branch of a pine tree. Its head tilted about thirty degrees to the west—that was the direction of the rabbit.

Shuichi crouched down and moved westward with light steps.

A rabbit’s hearing is much sharper than a human’s, and so is its sense of smell. Shuichi’s only chance was to approach from downwind. His sense of smell was not as good as a rabbit’s, but Lue could see the wind vane in the air : the pine tree tops were leaning to the east, and the wind was blowing from west to east. He was approaching from downwind, so the rabbit couldn’t smell him.

There was a low shrub by the shallow ditch, with a hole the size of a fist at the base of the shrub. A few dark brown droppings were scattered around the entrance, still fresh.

Shuichi slowed his breathing and tiptoed around the bushes. Then he saw it.

A grayish-brown rabbit was crouching by the stream, drinking water. Its size was larger than Shuichi had imagined. It was about half the length of his arm from head to tail, its ears standing straight up, turning from time to time to capture the sounds around it.

Shuichi stopped about three meters away from the rabbit. Any closer and he would be discovered.

His right palm began to heat up. Everything surging within the rabbit appeared in his mind through the perception function—that was a Vitality much richer than that of the tree lizard, like a bowl of thick soup rather than a bowl of water. There was also the rabbit’s hind leg explosive power, auditory sensitivity, and the fur’s adaptability to temperature changes. Each one was stronger and « heavier » than any ability he had plundered before.

If he plundered the Vitality of the entire rabbit, the gain would be about six to eight times that of the tree lizard. The mental energy consumption would also be higher, but it was worth it.

Shuichi held his breath, pushed off with his right foot, and his body shot out suddenly.

The jumping ability plundered from the grasshopper had long since been stripped away to the stag beetle, but the improvement of his physical foundation made his explosive power itself more than a notch stronger than that of an ordinary five-year-old child. He crossed the three-meter distance in two steps. The rabbit sensed the vibration of the ground and tried to kick off its hind legs to escape, but it was too late. Shuichi’s right hand pressed down on its back.

Plunder.

The suction force erupted. The Vitality within the rabbit—that rich, warm-glowing stream of life—was extracted from every cell and surged into Shuichi’s body along the invisible channel in his palm.

This was not the faint warmth of the grasshopper, nor the fleeting warm current of the tree lizard. As the rabbit’s Vitality poured into his body, Shuichi felt his whole body heating up, from his right arm to his chest, from his chest to his abdomen, and from his abdomen to his legs and feet. The feeling of being « filled » deep in his bones was unprecedentedly clear, just like when he had been dripping one or two drops into a cup of water, and now suddenly half a bowl was poured in.

The rabbit’s body went limp in his palm. The fur was still there, the touch was still warm, but it was empty inside. Shuichi gently placed the rabbit’s carcass in the grass by the stream and crouched there, panting heavily.

Mental energy consumption was about one-quarter. Still within the safe range. But the influx of Vitality was much more intense than that of insects and tree lizards, and his body needed time to integrate this new energy. He could feel his physical constitution slowly but continuously strengthening.

« One rabbit is worth about six to eight tree lizards. » Shuichi recorded in his mind, « Mental energy consumption is about six times that of a tree lizard. The exchange ratio is close to one-to-one, but the improvement gained per unit of time is far higher than repeatedly plundering small creatures. The efficiency increase is substantial. »

He stood up and stretched his limbs. His grip strength was stronger again, and his body’s response speed when running two steps was significantly faster than yesterday. More importantly, the sense of fulfillment brought by plundering the rabbit’s Vitality not only stayed at the level of bones and muscles but also seemed to fine-tune his sensory acuity. The sound of the stream flowing was clearer than before, and the rustling of needles in the pine forest had more layers.

Lue flew down from the pine branch and landed next to the rabbit’s carcass, tilting its head to look. Shuichi knew what it was thinking.

« You can’t eat it. It’s empty inside. I’ll get you some fish when we go back. »

Lue let out a dissatisfied chirrup, but still flew back onto Shuichi’s shoulder.

Shuichi spent another half an hour searching in the pine forest. He found a second rabbit, slightly smaller than the first, and similarly plundered its Vitality. The influx of the second rabbit’s Vitality was just as rich as the first, with no sign of diminishing because it was the same species. Shuichi carefully felt the warm sensation of the two Vitality streams merging in his body, and confirmed an important rule in his mind : Vitality is different from specific abilities ; it is a basic energy shared by all living things, and there is no diminishing effect. It’s like taking water from two different streams and pouring it into the same tank ; the water won’t be diluted because it comes from different streams. The grasshopper was like this, and the rabbit was the same. As long as there were enough prey, he could accumulate it steadily. The grasshoppers he had relied on for quantity, and today’s rabbits, followed the same rule.

This was the biggest advantage of Life Force Plunder : it had no diminishing effect. Unlike the accumulation of jumping ability, where the gain became smaller each time, Vitality was a pure quantitative accumulation, and when it accumulated to a certain level, it would produce a qualitative change.

About half of his mental energy remained. Shuichi decided to quit while he was ahead. He still had to walk nearly a kilometer back ; if his mental energy was too low, it would be troublesome if he encountered any accidents on the way.

He returned along the original path. He passed through the pine forest, descended the shrub slope, and bypassed the Training Ground. He paused for a moment when passing the Training Ground ; there were a few young Ninjas practicing shurikenjutsu near the target stakes, looking about twelve or thirteen years old, not from the Uchiha clan. Shuichi crouched behind the bushes and waited for a while until they finished their practice and left before he continued on his way back.

When he returned to the Uchiha Clan Grounds, the sun was already high in the sky. The main road of the clan grounds was getting crowded : women going to the market to buy groceries, Ninjas carrying Ninja tool bags walking towards the Military Police Force duty station, and children chasing and playing in the square. Shuichi blended into the crowd ; a five-year-old child walking on the road wouldn’t attract anyone’s attention.

When he returned home, Minako was cooking porridge in the kitchen. Hearing him enter, she poked her head out of the kitchen.

« Up so early today ? »

« Went to watch the sunrise. » Shuichi changed his shoes, took off his coat covered in pine needles and leaf fragments, balled it up, and stuffed it into the corner of the hallway. « Mom, what’s for breakfast ? »

« Rice porridge and pickled radish. » Minako glanced at him, her gaze lingering on his muddy pant legs for a second, then she withdrew her gaze and asked nothing more.

Shuichi went to the plum tree in the backyard, and Lue flew from his shoulder onto the plum branch, beginning to preen the pine needle debris from its wings. Shuichi sat down against the plum tree, closed his eyes, and felt the two rabbit Vitality streams slowly merging into his body. The accumulation of one large tree lizard plus over forty insects was not as significant as the improvement brought by one rabbit today.

But rabbits were just a transition. Lue’s reconnaissance range would continue to expand, and he would find larger prey. Foxes, badgers, deer, or even the giant creatures that appeared in the depths of the Forest of Death. The rules of Life Force Plunder were already clear : the larger the prey, the higher the return per unit of mental energy, and no matter how many of the same species were plundered, the return would not diminish.

Shuichi opened his eyes and looked at Lue, who was preening its flight feathers with its beak on the plum tree.

« Fly further next time. » He said softly, « Towards the northeast. That’s the edge of the Forest of Death ; there might be bigger things there. »

Lue tilted its head to look at him and let out a short chirp. Then it spread its wings, flew from the plum tree to the roof, and gazed north into the morning sun.
