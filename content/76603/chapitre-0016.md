# Chapitre 16 — The Price of Growth

Regarding the conceptual Plunder of [ Growth ], Shuichi spent three whole days preparing.

On the first day, he combed through all the introductory materials related to biological Growth in the school library. Although the Ninja Academy library did not have a large collection, it still had the basics of biology.

In the appendix of a book titled « Illustrated Guide to Creatures Around Konoha, » he found a small section on metamorphosis : tadpoles turning into frogs, larvae turning into insects, transitioning from aquatic to terrestrial, completing the entire morphological transformation in one summer. During the fastest stage of Growth, it only took three to four days for a tadpole’s hind legs to appear from nothing.

The next day, after school, he went to the wetlands downstream of the Naka River. The wetlands were about two li south of the Uchiha Clan Grounds, a shallow water area formed by the Naka River where it leveled out, with the water surface divided into ponds of various sizes by reed beds.

The water temperature in spring had begun to rise, and the shallow areas at the edges of the ponds were densely packed with swimming tadpoles. Some had just hatched, only the size of a grain of rice, dragging transparent little tails. Others had already grown two hind legs, with the tiny toes at the tips clearly visible.

Shuichi squatted by the edge of the pond and watched for a long time. These tadpoles were undergoing the most intense Growth process in nature ; they were not simply growing larger, but undergoing a total transformation of their body structure.

From fish to amphibian, from breathing in water to breathing air, from having no legs to having four. This level of [ Growth ] was closer to the root of the concept than the physical size increase of any ordinary animal.

He did not act on the spot. The wetlands were too close to the Village, and villagers gathering herbs occasionally passed by. Moreover, he needed to observe first, determine the Growth rate of the tadpoles, and select a specific stage to strike.

On the afternoon of the third day, he returned to the pond with a bamboo tube. Lue circled once over the reed beds at the edge of the wetlands before landing on a dead branch ; because this area was too open, a hawk in the sky was too conspicuous.

Shuichi scooped a tadpole that was growing hind legs from the edge of the pond and put it into the bamboo tube. The tadpole swam in the tube, its two hind limb buds having just emerged as rice-grain-sized bumps, and the outlines of the differentiating bones were faintly visible beneath its translucent skin. He looked down at the tadpole in the tube and slowly gripped the edge of the tube with his right hand.

This was the most abstract Plunder he had attempted in the past half year.

Flight Instinct was a concept, but flight had a clear carrier, such as the skeletal structure of wings, the arrangement of flight feathers, and the contraction patterns of wing muscles. Low-light vision was not a concept ; it had photoreceptor cells on the retina as a physical foundation. But [ Growth ] was a purely conceptual ability. Where did Growth exist within the tadpole ? It was in the cells at the tips of the hind limb buds that were dividing crazily, in the hormones controlling metamorphosis, and in every gene expression being reorganized. It was everywhere, yet nowhere at all.

Shuichi reached his right hand into the bamboo tube, his fingertips touching the tadpole’s soft body.

Perception expanded. Inside the tadpole was a world in violent flux. The cells of the hind limb buds were dividing at a speed far exceeding normal levels, spinal nerves were extending toward the new limbs, the vascular network of the gills was withering while lung buds began to expand, and the entire developmental blueprint of the body was being executed at an incredible speed.

Beneath all these changes lay a layer of something he had never touched before. It was not heat, not light, not any attribute that could be captured by the senses. It was more like a command, an underlying decree carved into every cell nucleus, ordering the body to continuously push toward its adult form. The essence of Growth. It was not any specific organ, not any specific hormone, but the underlying law that drove all of this to happen.

Shuichi concentrated his consciousness, and the suction from his right hand reached out toward that command.

Then, his mental world suddenly went black.

It wasn’t fainting ; rather, a large chunk of his mental energy was drained the instant it touched the concept of Growth, like a hand reaching into a rapidly spinning whirlpool, being dragged down by an irresistible force. Shuichi’s right arm went numb from his fingertips to his shoulder, a buzzing sound rang in his brain, and the ringing in his ears drowned out the sound of the wind in the reed beds. He gritted his teeth, wanting to cut off the Plunder, but discovered his right hand would not obey him. The suction did not stop ; it was operating on its own, as if it had lost control after touching something too vast.

A second extraction. Another chunk of mental energy was gone. Shuichi felt his consciousness being torn away at the edges, piece by piece ; his vision narrowed, and his fingertips grew cold. The tadpole writhed violently in his palm, more fiercely than when the Red Fox had struggled, but not because of pain—it was because its body began to descend into chaos after its underlying developmental commands were drained. The division speed of the hind limb buds dropped sharply, and the cells lost their direction.

Shuichi slammed his left hand onto his right wrist.

The suction disconnected.

He collapsed onto the muddy ground by the pond, gasping for air. The bamboo tube tipped over, and the tadpole slid back into the pond with the water flow. Shuichi looked down at his right hand—the palm was intact, and his fingers could still move, but the skin from his fingertips to his wrist was tinged with an unnatural paleness. Nearly three-quarters of his mental energy had been consumed, half as much again as when he had Plundered the Red Fox, and the Plunder had lasted less than two seconds.

Two seconds, three-quarters of his mental energy. And he hadn’t Plundered anything.

Shuichi sat against the reed beds for a long time until the ringing in his ears faded completely. Then he stood up and washed the mud off his hands by the pond.

It was already getting dark when he returned to the Uchiha Clan Grounds. When Minako saw his face as he entered—lips pale, right hand trembling slightly in his sleeve—she didn’t ask any questions. She didn’t pry, but silently added more vegetables to his dinner, and before he returned to his room, she placed a cup of warm water on the hallway.

Shuichi sat in the hallway holding the warm water, looking at the sky outside, and began to reorganize his thoughts.

Tadpoles were absolutely impossible. The abstract level of [ Growth ] was too high, and the tadpole was at the peak of metamorphosis ; that degree of the Growth concept was like a string tightened to its limit, and the mental energy backlash released the moment he touched it far exceeded his capacity. But this line of thinking wasn’t necessarily wrong ; perhaps he didn’t need to challenge the Growth concept at its most violent stage of development right away. Finding a creature with a gentler Growth rate and a more stable developmental stage might make the threshold for extraction much lower.

He looked down at the wooden enclosure in the corner of the yard. Three field mice were being kept in there.

It took about two weeks for field mouse pups to go from birth to opening their eyes, and another week from opening their eyes to weaning. The entire Growth process was gradual and gentle ; their body size grew a little larger each day, their bones became a little denser each day, without the violent restructuring of the tadpole that grew entire legs within a few days. Plundering the Growth concept from field mouse pups might consume far less energy than from a tadpole. Although the gain would also be smaller, the Growth intensity of the field mice was far less than metamorphosis, which might allow Shuichi to have the capacity to withstand it.

As for precise fusion, he thought of another path. Before directly challenging the Growth concept, perhaps he could first practice precise fusion using an easier-to-obtain conceptual ability. Low-light vision had been successfully extracted from a turtledove and stripped/transferred to Lue ; if transferring to Lue could be done, then fusing it onto his own eyes should be the same principle, just with the direction changed from [ Pushing Out ] to [ Locking In ]. It didn’t need to be as high-level as the Growth concept, just a simple enough conceptual ability, a low-risk material that could verify the technical move of [ Locking onto a specific part ].

He stood up from the hallway, his thoughts clearer than before.

Two things. First, find a newborn field mouse pup. Second, find a simple conceptual ability to perform a precise fusion test on himself. Neither could be rushed, as long as the direction was correct.

In the darkness outside the window, Lue flew back from the direction of the Forest of Death and landed on the windowsill. Its pupils shone with a brighter luster than before in the darkness. Shuichi reached out and stroked the top of Lue’s head.
